# k=2 Cayley energy and cyclotomic exceptional primes

This note isolates the lower-dimensional `C_H` term in the M1 variance problem. The result is exact for odd prime subgroup order outside a finite, explicitly computable set of reduction primes.

## 1. Cayley transform

Let

`c(h)=(h+1)/(h-1)`

for `h!=1`. A direct calculation gives

`d(u,v)=(u-1)(v-1)/(v-u)=2/(c(u)-c(v))`.

Set

`A=c(H\{1})`.

Since `Delta -> 2/Delta` is a bijection of `F_N^*`, the `k=2` difference distribution `C_H(t)` is just the nonzero additive-difference representation function of `A`, with its argument relabelled. Therefore

`sum_t C_H(t)^2 = E_plus(A)-|A|^2`,

where

`E_plus(A)=#{a-b=c-d : a,b,c,d in A}`.

The active `verify_second_moment_reduction.py` checks this equality directly.

## 2. Central symmetry and the forced baseline

For every `h!=1`,

`c(h^-1)=-c(h)`.

Hence `A=-A`. If `n=|A|=K-1` and `z=1_{0 in A}`, every nonzero-difference representation `(a,b)` has the forced partner

`(a,b) -> (-b,-a)`.

This involution is fixed exactly when `a=-b`. There are `n-z` such fixed ordered pairs. Thus the forced nonzero-difference energy is

`E_forced = 2n(n-1)-(n-z)`.

For odd `K`, `-1 notin H`, so `z=0` and

`E_forced=(K-1)(2K-5)`.

Any excess above this baseline is a genuinely new additive collision.

## 3. PGL2 interpretation

If `c(k)=c(h)-x`, then

`k=g_x(h)=((2-x)h+x)/(2+x-xh)`.

The representing matrix

`M_x=[[2-x,x],[-x,2+x]]`

has determinant `4`, and direct symbolic multiplication gives

`M_x M_y = 2 M_(x+y)`.

Thus `g_x` is a one-parameter unipotent subgroup in `PGL_2`. The additive representation number has the exact intersection interpretation

`r_(A-A)(x)=#{h in H\{1}: g_x(h) in H\{1}}`.

Equivalently, if the fixed point `h=1` is included on both sides,

`r_(A-A)(x)=|H intersect g_x^{-1}(H)|-1`.

This makes Möbius-incidence results relevant to the exceptional/uniform problem, but no generic incidence bound is being claimed to give the sharp energy estimate by itself.

## 4. Odd-prime K: characteristic-zero collision classification

Assume `K` is an odd prime and let `zeta` be a primitive complex `K`-th root of unity. Write

`a_i=c(zeta^i)`,  `i=1,...,K-1`.

A difference collision

`a_i-a_j=a_r-a_s`

with `i!=j`, `r!=s` clears denominators to

`P(zeta)=0`,

where

`P = -X^r+X^s+X^i-2X^(s+i)+X^(r+s+i)-X^j+2X^(r+j)-X^(r+s+j)-X^(r+i+j)+X^(s+i+j)`

with exponents taken modulo `K`.

Because `K` is prime, the only rational linear relation among `1,zeta,...,zeta^(K-1)` is generated by

`Phi_K(X)=1+X+...+X^(K-1)`.

The coefficients of `P` sum to zero. Hence `P(zeta)=0` forces every aggregated coefficient to vanish, or equivalently the multiset identity

`{s, i, r+s+i, r+j, r+j, s+i+j}`
`={r, s+i, s+i, j, r+s+j, r+i+j}`

in `Z/KZ`.

The active verifier classifies all `6!=720` possible matchings as exact integer linear systems in `(i,j,r,s)`:

- ranks over `Q`: 30 systems of rank 2, 240 of rank 3, 450 of rank 4;
- the gcds of maximal minors can lose rank only in characteristic `2`;
- among the 30 rank-two systems, 26 force a zero exponent or a diagonal pair;
- the remaining four are two copies of each forced family
  `r=i, s=j` and `r=-j, s=-i`.

Thus for every odd prime `K`, the complex Cayley set has no non-forced difference collisions.

This is currently a small exact computer-assisted linear classification; a shorter hand proof or Lean formalization would be preferable before publication.

## 5. Reduction modulo N and the exact exceptional set

A finite-field collision can still appear after reduction modulo a rational prime `N`. This explains why excess collisions can disappear and then reappear as `N` varies.

By Galois scaling, normalize `i=1`. For every non-forced triple `(j,r,s)`, let

`R_(j,r,s)=|Res(Phi_K,P_(1,j,r,s))|`.

The characteristic-zero classification guarantees `R_(j,r,s)!=0`.

Define the collision discriminant

`D_K = product R_(j,r,s)`

over the normalized non-forced triples; only its radical matters.

For a rational prime `N == 1 (mod K)`:

- if a non-forced collision exists in the order-`K` subgroup of `F_N^*`, then `N | D_K`;
- conversely, if `N | D_K`, then `Phi_K` and one collision polynomial have a common root modulo `N`; because `N == 1 (mod K)`, that root has order `K` in `F_N^*`, so a genuine non-forced collision exists.

Therefore the exceptional primes are **exactly**

`{N prime : N == 1 (mod K), N | D_K}`.

Every conjugate of a collision polynomial has absolute value at most `12`, so every exceptional prime also satisfies the crude uniform bound

`N <= 12^(K-1)`.

This proves finiteness for each fixed odd prime `K`, although it is not a useful uniform exclusion when `K` grows with `N`.

## 6. Exact small-K exceptional sets

The verifier computes the resultants and factors them exactly.

For `K=11`:

`E_11={23,67,89}`.

The finite data show excess energies `240,48,32` respectively. At `N=199` there is no excess.

For `K=13`:

`E_13={53,79,131,157,313}`.

All five primes have excess collisions. At `N=521` there is no excess.

Independent exploratory calculations also reproduce finite exact sets for `K=17,19`; they are not yet part of the default verifier because the smaller `K=11,13` cases already certify the mechanism without making the routine audit unnecessarily heavy.

## 7. Exact energy outside the exceptional set

Let `K` be an odd prime and let `N == 1 (mod K)` be a prime with `N notin E_K`. Then the only nonzero-difference representations are the identity and inversion-swap families. Therefore

`sum_t C_H(t)^2=(K-1)(2K-5)`

and, equivalently,

`E_plus(A)=3(K-1)(K-2)`.

Since the total mass is

`sum_t C_H(t)=(K-1)(K-2)`

and `C_H(0)=0`, the exact population variance over `F_N^*` is

`Var_(t!=0) C_H(t)`
`= (K-1)(2K-5)/(N-1)`
`  - (K-1)^2(K-2)^2/(N-1)^2`.

Hence the mixed M1 layer

`M(t)=2(N-2)C_H(t)`

satisfies

`std_(t!=0) M(t)`
`<= 2(N-2) sqrt((K-1)(2K-5)/(N-1))`
`= O(K sqrt(N))`.

This is exactly the scale required by the global M1 variance target.

## 8. What remains open

This closes the `k=2` collision-energy component for odd prime `K` outside the explicit cyclotomic exceptional set. It does **not** yet give a uniform theorem through all exceptional primes or for composite `K`.

More importantly, the dominant unresolved part of M1 is still the pure off/off cubic-family term `O(t)` and its covariance with the now-controlled mixed term. Candidate PGL2-incidence results help organize the geometry, while the expected sharp `L^2` estimate for the cubic family still points toward trace-function/elliptic-family correlation methods.
