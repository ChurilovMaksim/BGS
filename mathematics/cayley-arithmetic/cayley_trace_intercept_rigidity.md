# Cyclotomic trace-intercept rigidity and scalar shift-resultant support

**Status: EXACT + EXTERNAL-THEOREM for the non-inverse characteristic-zero fibre classification; EXACT for the inverse-row fibre classification, cyclotomic-unit matching reduction, shift-resultant support criterion, and the per-ratio `O(K^3)` logarithmic support budget; FINITE-REGRESSION for the C++ and SymPy anchors; OPEN for a polynomial bound on the largest split divisor.**

This note sharpens the exact fixed-product-row programme in two ways.

1. Every characteristic-zero affine trace intercept for a fixed normalized row ratio has multiplicity at most two.  For non-inverse rows all double fibres are forced by one explicit step-two identity; for the inverse row the only double fibres are swaps.
2. The order-three finite-field event can be detected by the first three coefficients of **one scalar shift-resultant**.  This replaces a many-coefficient subresultant ideal by a three-generator ideal with the same split-prime support.

The result is compatible with, and does not replace, the stronger half-degree reflection reduction for `r=-1` in `cayley_product_row_reflection.md`.

## 1. Trace notation

Let `K=2m+1>=7` be an odd prime, let `zeta` be a primitive `K`-th root of unity, and put

```text
t_a=zeta^a+zeta^(-a),
s_a=zeta^a-zeta^(-a).
```

Trace indices are taken modulo the involution `a~-a`; write `[a]` for the corresponding trace class.  Thus the full trace set has representatives

```text
[a], 0<=a<=m.
```

Fix a normalized row ratio

```text
r in F_K^*\{1}.
```

The affine slope and the distinguished row intercept are

```text
alpha_r=s_r/s_1,
beta_r=-2 U_(r-2)(tau/2),
tau=t_1,
```

as in `cayley_product_row_real_trace_ideal.md`.  For arbitrary trace classes define the characteristic-zero intercept

```text
gamma_r(a,b)=t_b-alpha_r t_a.                            (1)
```

A row intersection for the normalized pair is exactly a pair with

```text
gamma_r(a,b)=beta_r.                                     (2)
```

The valid row problem excludes `[a]=[1]` and `[b]=[r]`; the fibre theorem below is stronger and is stated on the full trace-class square.

## 2. Prime-order cyclotomic absolute values are multiplicatively independent

For `1<=j<=m` put

```text
E_j=|1-zeta^j|>0.                                        (3)
```

### Lemma 1 (multiplicative independence)

The positive real numbers

```text
E_1,...,E_m
```

are multiplicatively independent.

### Proof

Bass defines `U'_K` as the multiplicative group generated by the numbers `1-xi`, where `xi^K=1`, `xi!=1`, and `U_K` as its quotient by torsion.  His prime-power theorem (`K=p` is the present case) identifies `U_K` with the free module generated by one Galois orbit modulo complex conjugation; equivalently, the classes of

```text
1-zeta^j, 1<=j<=m,
```

form a `Z`-basis of the torsion-free group.  See H. Bass, *Generators and Relations for Cyclotomic Units*, Nagoya Math. J. **27** (1966), 401--407, Theorem 3, DOI `10.1017/S0027763000026258`.

Suppose

```text
prod_(j=1)^m E_j^(n_j)=1.                                (4)
```

Squaring and using

```text
(1-zeta^(-j))=-zeta^(-j)(1-zeta^j)
```

gives, modulo torsion in `U'_K`,

```text
prod_(j=1)^m (1-zeta^j)^(2 n_j)=1.
```

The Bass basis therefore forces every `n_j=0`. `□`

This is exactly the amount of cyclotomic-unit theory needed below.  No unverified positive-characteristic analogue is used.

## 3. Exact classification of characteristic-zero intercept fibres

Two elementary factorizations will be used repeatedly:

```text
|s_n|=E_[2n],                                             (5)
|t_x-t_y|=E_[x-y] E_[x+y]                                (6)
```

for distinct trace classes `[x]!=[y]`.  Here `E_[u]` means `E_j` for the unique representative `j in {1,...,m}` of the nonzero class `+-u`.

### Theorem 2 (trace-intercept fibre rigidity)

For every odd prime `K>=7` and every normalized ratio `r!=1`, every fibre of

```text
([a],[b]) |-> gamma_r(a,b)                               (7)
```

has cardinality at most two.

More precisely:

- if `r=-1`, a double fibre consists only of the swap

```text
([a],[b]), ([b],[a]);                                    (8)
```

- if `r!=+-1`, every double fibre is, up to changing signs of representatives and swapping its two points, of the form

```text
([a],   [a+1-r]),
([a+2], [a+1+r]).                                        (9)
```

The equality in (9) is an exact identity:

```text
gamma_r(a,a+1-r)=gamma_r(a+2,a+1+r).                    (10)
```

### Proof: inverse ratio

If `r=-1`, then `alpha_r=-1` and

```text
gamma_(-1)(a,b)=t_a+t_b.
```

If `t_a+t_b=t_c+t_d`, expansion in powers of `zeta` gives a rational linear relation among the `K` roots of unity with total coefficient sum zero.  Since `Phi_K=1+X+...+X^(K-1)` is the only rational relation among the powers of a primitive prime-order root, every coefficient vanishes separately.  Hence the multisets

```text
{[a],[b]}={ [c],[d] },
```

which gives only identity or swap.  Thus every fibre has size at most two.

### Proof: non-inverse ratios

Assume now `r!=+-1` and suppose two distinct pairs lie in one fibre:

```text
gamma_r(a,b)=gamma_r(c,d).                               (11)
```

If `[a]=[c]`, then (11) forces `[b]=[d]`, so the two pairs would be identical.  Thus both source and target trace classes are distinct, and

```text
t_b-t_d=alpha_r(t_a-t_c).                               (12)
```

Taking absolute values and applying (5)--(6) gives

```text
E_[b-d] E_[b+d] E_[2]
 = E_[a-c] E_[a+c] E_[2r].                              (13)
```

Lemma 1 turns (13) into the exact multiset identity

```text
{[b-d],[b+d],[2]}
 = {[a-c],[a+c],[2r]}.                                   (14)
```

Because `r!=+-1`, one has `[2r]!=[2]`.  Therefore the `[2]` on the left must equal one of `[a-c]` or `[a+c]`.  After changing the sign of `c` and, if necessary, swapping the two source points, choose representatives with

```text
c=a+2.                                                   (15)
```

Cancelling `[2]` from (14) gives

```text
{[b-d],[b+d]}={[2r],[2a+2]}.                             (16)
```

Solving the sum/difference system in `F_K` shows that the unordered target trace classes are exactly

```text
{[b],[d]}={ [a+1-r],[a+1+r] }.                           (17)
```

For a fixed ordering of the two source classes only one ordering of the two target classes can satisfy (12): swapping `b,d` negates its left-hand side while the right-hand side is nonzero.  This proves the classification (9).

It remains only to check that the displayed double fibre really exists.  The identities

```text
t_(x+r)-t_(x-r)=s_r s_x,
t_(a+2)-t_a=s_1 s_(a+1)                                 (18)
```

give

```text
s_1(t_(a+1+r)-t_(a+1-r))
 =s_r(t_(a+2)-t_a),
```
which is exactly (10).

Finally, a fibre of size three would give three distinct source trace classes each pair of which is step-two adjacent.  After rescaling indices by `2^(-1)`, the step-two graph is the quotient of the `K`-cycle by the reflection `x~-x`.  For odd `K` this quotient is a path (with a harmless terminal loop) and contains no triangle.  Hence no fibre has size three. `□`

### Corollary 3 (the distinguished characteristic-zero intercept is isolated)

For every `r!=1`, the forced pair

```text
([a],[b])=([1],[r])                                      (19)
```

is the unique characteristic-zero solution of `gamma_r(a,b)=beta_r`.

For `r=-1` it is a diagonal singleton in (8).  For `r!=+-1`, inserting `[a]=[1]` into (9) would require `r=1` to recover `[b]=[r]`, which is excluded.

Thus the finite order-three phenomenon is genuinely caused by reduction modulo a prime; there is no hidden characteristic-zero triple.

## 4. Row intersections as congruences between explicit cyclotomic units

Let division by `2` in subscripts mean multiplication by `2^(-1) mod K`.  For `n!=0` put

```text
u_n=s_n/s_1.                                              (20)
```

For prime `K`, every `u_n` is a cyclotomic unit.  If `[a]!=[1]` and `[b]!=[r]`, then all four half-indices below are nonzero and

```text
t_b-t_r
 =s_((b+r)/2) s_((b-r)/2),
t_a-t_1
 =s_((a+1)/2) s_((a-1)/2).                              (21)
```

Since `alpha_r=s_r/s_1`, subtracting the distinguished intercept gives

```text
gamma_r(a,b)-beta_r
 =s_1^2 [ W_r(b)-V_r(a) ],                               (22)
```

where

```text
W_r(b)=u_((b+r)/2) u_((b-r)/2),
V_r(a)=u_r u_((a+1)/2) u_((a-1)/2).                     (23)
```

For every rational prime `p!=K`, the factor `s_1` is a `p`-adic unit because its norm is supported only at `K`.  Therefore, for every split prime `p==1 mod K` and every prime ideal `q|p`,

```text
gamma_r(a,b)==beta_r mod q
iff
W_r(b)==V_r(a) mod q.                                    (24)
```

So the exact finite row-intersection graph is a bipartite congruence graph between two explicit finite lists of cyclotomic units.  This is a more arithmetic formulation of the remaining split-prime problem than a generic curve-incidence statement.

## 5. A scalar shift-resultant whose zero order is the intersection count

Let

```text
H_K(T)=(T-2) C_K(T)
```

be the full trace polynomial.  In `O^+=Z[tau]` define the source and target-valid trace polynomials

```text
P_1(T)=H_K(T)/(T-t_1),
R_r(U)=H_K(U)/(U-t_r).                                   (25)
```

Both are monic of degree `m`.  Define

```text
mathcal R_(K,r)(Z)
 =Res_T(P_1(T), R_r(alpha_r T+beta_r+Z))
 =c_0+c_1 Z+c_2 Z^2+... in O^+[Z].                      (26)
```

The leading coefficient in `T` of the second polynomial is `alpha_r^m`, a unit, so there is no specialization-at-infinity ambiguity.

### Theorem 4 (zero order equals geometric row intersection)

Let `p==1 mod K` be split and let `q|p` choose one real embedding.  Then

```text
ord_(Z=0)(mathcal R_(K,r)(Z) mod q)=I_(1,r)(q),          (27)
```

where `I_(1,r)(q)` is the number of valid common Cayley parameters for that normalized row pair.

Consequently

```text
I_(1,r)(q)>=3
iff
c_0==c_1==c_2==0 mod q.                                  (28)
```

### Proof

Over the split residue field,

```text
P_1(T)=prod_([a]!=[1]) (T-t_a),
R_r(U)=prod_([b]!=[r]) (U-t_b).
```

Because `P_1` is monic, the product formula for the resultant gives exactly

```text
mathcal R_(K,r)(Z)
 =prod_([a]!=[1]) prod_([b]!=[r])
   (Z+alpha_r t_a+beta_r-t_b).                           (29)
```

Every factor is linear in `Z`; at `Z=0` it vanishes exactly for one valid row-intersection pair.  Therefore its zero multiplicity is the number of such pairs, proving (27).  Since a split prime satisfies `p>K>=7`, (28) is equivalent to zero order at least three. `□`

## 6. Three-generator exact support ideal

Define

```text
J^shift_(K,r)=(c_0,c_1,c_2) subset O^+,
J^shift_(K,r) intersect Z = Omega_(K,r)^(3) Z.            (30)
```

The standard contraction/Smith criterion and Theorem 4 give:

### Corollary 5 (exact scalar support)

For every split prime `p==1 mod K`,

```text
p|Omega_(K,r)^(3)
iff
some embedding prime q|p has I_(1,r)(q)>=3.              (31)
```

Hence `Omega_(K,r)^(3)` and the real-trace subresultant invariant `Lambda_(K,r)^(3)` have the same **split rational-prime support**.  Equality of the integers, or equality of their non-split prime factors/valuations, is not claimed.

This formulation is materially smaller: the exact support is generated by three scalar algebraic integers instead of all coefficients of three subresultant polynomials.

## 7. A per-ratio `O(K^3)` logarithmic support budget

The constant coefficient

```text
c_0=mathcal R_(K,r)(0)                                   (32)
```

is nonzero in characteristic zero by Corollary 3.  Every split order-three prime divides its absolute norm.  This already gives a useful deterministic mass bound.

### Theorem 6 (per-ratio support mass)

Uniformly in `r!=1`,

```text
log |N_(L^+/Q)(c_0)| = O(K^3),                           (33)
```

and therefore

```text
sum_(p==1 mod K, I_r(p)>=3) log p = O(K^3).              (34)
```

The implied constants are absolute.

### Proof

Index the real embeddings by `1<=j<=m`.  Under the `j`-th embedding,

```text
|t_a|<=2,
|sigma_j(u_n)|
 =|sin(2 pi j n/K)|/|sin(2 pi j/K)|.                    (35)
```

Put

```text
d_j=min(j,K/2-j)>0.
```

The elementary bound `|sin(pi x)|>=2 dist(x,Z)` for distance at most `1/2` gives

```text
|sin(2 pi j/K)| >= 4 d_j/K,
|sigma_j(u_n)| <= K/(4 d_j).                             (36)
```

Using `alpha_r=u_r`, `beta_r=-2u_(r-1)`, every factor in (29) at `Z=0` has absolute value at most

```text
2+K/d_j.                                                  (37)
```

There are `m^2` factors, so

```text
log |N(c_0)|
 <=m^2 sum_(j=1)^m log(2+K/d_j).                         (38)
```

The `d_j` consist, up to the midpoint, of integers and half-integers growing linearly from each endpoint.  Splitting at `K/4` and applying the elementary Stirling estimate to the two products gives

```text
sum_j log(2+K/d_j)=O(K).                                  (39)
```

Since `m=O(K)`, (33) follows.  If a split prime supports an order-three intersection then, by (28), some prime ideal above it divides `c_0`, hence the rational prime divides `|N(c_0)|`; summing distinct logarithms gives (34). `□`

Summing (34) over `O(K)` ratio classes returns an `O(K^4)` global scale, so this theorem does **not** by itself beat the maintained global resultant budget.  Its value is that the remaining order-three support for one ratio is now concentrated in the simultaneous gcd of three low shift coefficients.

## 8. Exact finite regressions

Two mechanically different regressions accompany this note.

### C++20 sparse group-ring verifier

`computations/cpp/cayley_trace_intercept_rigidity.cpp` represents

```text
s_1 t_b-s_r t_a
```

as an exact sparse coefficient vector in `Z[C_K]`.  For prime `K`, equality of these vectors is equivalent to equality in `Q(zeta)` because every vector has coefficient sum zero.  The verifier checks:

- every fibre has size at most two;
- inverse-row double fibres are swaps;
- every non-inverse double fibre has step-two source classes and the target classes predicted by (9).

The maintained quick regression checks the selected prime orders

```text
K=7,11,13,17,23,31,47,79,101,127,151,181,199.
```

It exhausts every normalized ratio and the full trace-class square at each
listed order, not every prime below `199`. An explicitly recorded extended run also checked

```text
K=251,307,401,503,601,701,809.
```

This is a regression/falsifier only; Theorem 2 is proved independently above.

### SymPy scalar-ideal anchors

`computations/verification/cayley_trace_intercept_shift_resultant.py` constructs (26), takes `c_0,c_1,c_2`, contracts their ideal to `Z` by Smith normal form, and checks

```text
(K,r)=(7,2):   Omega=7^3,                   Omega_(2K)'=1,
(K,r)=(11,7):  Omega=11^5,                  Omega_(2K)'=1,
(K,r)=(13,3):  Omega=13^6,                  Omega_(2K)'=1,
(K,r)=(13,12): Omega=2^4*5^2*13^6,          Omega_(2K)'=5^2.   (40)
```

The prime-to-`2K` values agree with the corresponding real-trace `Lambda'` anchors.  The full valuations need not agree, and no such claim is made.

A full symbolic resultant becomes expensive already around `K=17`; this is a computational bottleneck of the reference implementation, not a mathematical obstruction.  A future implementation should compute only the `Z^0,Z^1,Z^2` determinant coefficients in the quotient algebra instead of materializing the degree-`m^2` resultant.

## 9. Relation to the literature search

Hang Fu's projective-equivalence work uses the same underlying cyclotomic-unit mechanism: cross-ratio equality is converted into multiplicative relations among `|1-zeta^a|`; see H. Fu, *Projective Equivalence for the Roots of Unity*, J. Algebraic Combin. **56** (2022), 823--871, arXiv:1905.01510, especially Theorem 15 and the cross-ratio identity preceding it.  The present trace-intercept theorem is a much more special prime-order affine problem and is proved directly above.

Dvornicich--Zannier, *Sums of roots of unity vanishing modulo a prime*, Arch. Math. **79** (2002), 104--108, proves a finite-characteristic analogue of a Conway--Jones inequality under its own congruence hypotheses.  The abstract alone is insufficient to transfer that theorem to a relation at one chosen prime ideal of `Q(zeta_K)`.  In particular, the maintained Fibonacci collision family supplies short relations at individual finite-field embeddings for arbitrarily large prime `K`, so any naive interpretation that forbids such relations is false.  No Dvornicich--Zannier consequence is promoted here until the exact hypotheses are checked from the full theorem.

## 10. New arithmetic closure target

For each nontrivial ratio class the exact order-three event is now

```text
q | (c_0,c_1,c_2).                                       (41)
```

A sufficient pointwise closure theorem remains a polynomial largest-split-divisor bound, for example

```text
P_split^+(Omega'_K^(3)) <= K^(4+o(1)),                  (42)
```

where `Omega'_K^(3)` denotes the prime-to-`2K` product over ratio classes (with duplicate ratio classes quotiented as in the real-trace framework).

This proposed bound excludes supported split primes for each fixed
`0<epsilon<1/4` when `K<=p^(1/4-epsilon)`. It does not alone cover all of
`K=o(p^(1/4))`; an `O(K^4)` largest-split-divisor bound would suffice for
that full regime. See the quantifier distinction in
`cayley_product_row_real_trace_ideal.md`, Section 8.

The bound (33) is exponentially weaker than (42).  The highest-value next step is therefore **not** another height estimate for `c_0`; it is a simultaneous-gcd theorem exploiting the special cyclotomic-unit expressions (23) or an exact low-order formula for `c_0,c_1,c_2` that exposes cancellation/common factors.

Microscopic BGS/RMT remains downstream of the separate Heisenberg-scale trace-comparison problem in `trace_to_local_statistics_bridge.md`.
