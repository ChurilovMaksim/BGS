# Projective Bezout support and a universal polynomial for Cayley product rows

**Status: EXACT for the universal row polynomial, its discriminant and differential recurrence, the projective Bezout rank criterion, the exact determinantal support theorem, and the local determinantal-valuation formula; FINITE-REGRESSION for the explicit HNF and finite-field anchors below; OPEN for a growing-`K` bound on the exact support integer.**

This note strictly refines `cayley_product_row_subresultants.md`.

The fixed-degree dehomogenized subresultants used there are a safe obstruction,
but they can vanish when both row forms lose leading degree modulo a prime,
even if fewer than three **projective** row parameters coincide.  An ambient
Bezout matrix keeps the point at infinity inside the geometry and replaces that
one-way obstruction by an exact determinantal ideal.

A second exact input is that all prime-order product rows are generated from one
elementary polynomial

```text
P_K(Y)=sum_{j=0}^{(K-1)/2} binom(K,2j)Y^j.
```

Its discriminant is supported only at `2K`, and it satisfies a second-order ODE.
Consequently every row coefficient is generated by a two-step recurrence.  This
is the current algebraic route toward bounding the exact exceptional support.

No growing-`K` split-prime bound is proved here.

## 1. Setup

Let `K` be an odd prime and put

```text
m=(K-1)/2,
L=Q(zeta_K),
O_L=Z[zeta_K].
```

For the Cayley map

```text
c(x)=(x+1)/(x-1)
```

and a split rational prime

```text
p==1 (mod K),
```

let `H<=F_p^*` be the subgroup of order `K` and

```text
A=c(H\{1}).
```

The exact fixed-product-row theorem in
`cayley_product_row_intersections.md` gives

```text
Delta_K(p)<=4 I_K(p)(K-1)(K-2),                         (1)
```

where `I_K(p)` is the largest number of common nonzero Cayley parameters of
two distinct noncentral product rows.

The homogeneous row forms from
`cayley_product_row_subresultants.md` are

```text
R_i(U,V)=prod_{a in A_i}(D_(i,a)U-N_iV),                (2)
N_i=1-zeta_K^(2i),
D_(i,a)=zeta_K^i(zeta_K^a+zeta_K^(-a))-2.
```

They have degree `m`.  Their projective roots are exactly the row parameters in

```text
mu=t/(2-t),
```

with `t=2` represented by `[U:V]=[1:0]`.

For a Galois-normalized pair `(1,r)` write

```text
f_r(U)=R_1(U,1),
g_r(U)=R_r(U,1).                                         (3)
```

The ambient degree is always `m`, although reduction modulo a prime ideal can
lower the affine degree by moving a root to infinity.

## 2. Universal trace polynomial

Define

```text
P_K(Y)=sum_{j=0}^m binom(K,2j)Y^j.                       (4)
```

Equivalently,

```text
P_K(X^2)=Q_K(X)
        =((X+1)^K-(X-1)^K)/2.                           (5)
```

Let `zeta` be a nontrivial `K`-th root of unity and put

```text
C=c(zeta).
```

Since

```text
(C+1)/(C-1)=zeta,
```

we have `Q_K(C)=0`, hence

```text
P_K(C^2)=0.                                              (6)
```

The values `c(zeta^a)^2` are identified exactly under `a~-a`.  There are `m`
classes, while `P_K` has degree `m` and leading coefficient `K`.

> **Theorem 1 (universal trace polynomial).**  In characteristic zero,
>
> ```text
> Roots(P_K)={c(zeta^a)^2 : a in F_K^*/{+-1}}.           (7)
> ```
>
> For every split prime `p==1 mod K`, reduction gives the `m` distinct squared
> Cayley values of the nontrivial elements of `H` modulo inversion.

The finite-field distinctness follows from the inverse Cayley map and
`c(x^-1)=-c(x)`.

## 3. Exact discriminant of `P_K`

The universal polynomial is arithmetically much less ramified than a generic
polynomial of degree `m`.

> **Theorem 2 (universal discriminant).**
>
> ```text
> |Disc(P_K)|
> =2^((K-1)(K-3)/2) K^((K-3)/2).                        (8)
> ```

### Proof

The nonzero roots of `Q_K` are `C=c(zeta)` for all `zeta^K=1`, `zeta!=1`.
Differentiating (5) and using `C+1=zeta(C-1)` and `C-1=2/(zeta-1)` gives

```text
Q_K'(C)
 =-K 2^(K-2)/(zeta (zeta-1)^(K-2)).                    (9)
```

For prime `K`,

```text
prod_{zeta!=1} zeta=1,
prod_{zeta!=1}(zeta-1)=K.                               (10)
```

Since `deg Q_K=K-1` and its leading coefficient is `K`, the resultant formula
for the discriminant yields

```text
|Disc(Q_K)|
 =2^((K-1)(K-2)) K^(K-2).                               (11)
```

On the other hand `Q_K(X)=P_K(X^2)`.  Pairing the roots `+-sqrt(a_j)` shows

```text
|Disc(Q_K)|=4^m K |Disc(P_K)|^2.                         (12)
```

Substitution of `m=(K-1)/2` into (11)--(12) gives (8).

Thus the root Vandermonde of `P_K` is a unit at every prime ideal above a split
prime `p==1 mod K`; only `2` and `K` occur in (8).  This will be useful when
passing to root/Lagrange coordinates locally.

## 4. Differential equation and derivative recurrence

The polynomial (4) satisfies

```text
2Y(Y-1)P_K''(Y)
 +(((3-2K)Y)-1)P_K'(Y)
 +(K(K-1)/2)P_K(Y)=0.                                   (13)
```

Write

```text
P_K(Y)=sum_j b_jY^j,
b_j=binom(K,2j).
```

The coefficient of `Y^j` in (13) vanishes exactly because

```text
[2j^2+(1-2K)j+K(K-1)/2]b_j
 =(j+1)(2j+1)b_(j+1),                                   (14)
```

which is the binomial-ratio identity for `b_(j+1)/b_j`.

Let `a` be a root of `P_K` and define

```text
d_n(a)=P_K^(n)(a)/P_K'(a),
d_0=0,
d_1=1.                                                  (15)
```

By (8), `P_K'(a)` is nonzero in characteristic zero and at every split prime.
Differentiating (13) `n` times and evaluating at `a` gives

```text
2a(a-1)d_(n+2)
 +(((4n+3-2K)a)-(2n+1))d_(n+1)
 +(2n^2+(1-2K)n+K(K-1)/2)d_n=0.                         (16)
```

So every normalized derivative is generated by a second-order recurrence.

## 5. Universal monic row polynomial

Fix a noncentral row square root `w` and set

```text
C=c(w),
a=C^2.                                                   (17)
```

For a second subgroup parameter `z`, put

```text
S=c(z),
s=S^2.
```

The Cayley multiplication law gives

```text
c(wz)+c(wz^(-1))
 =2C(1-s)/(a-s).                                         (18)
```

Introduce

```text
Z=(1-a)/(s-a).                                           (19)
```

Then simply

```text
t=2C(1-Z).                                               (20)
```

The invalid source `z=w^(+-1)` is the deleted root `s=a`; the diagonal pair
`z=1`, where `c(z)` is infinite, becomes `Z=0`.

Define

```text
F_a(Z)
 =Z^(m+1)P_K(a+(1-a)/Z)/((1-a)P_K'(a)).                 (21)
```

Taylor expansion at the root `a` gives the monic degree-`m` polynomial

```text
F_a(Z)
 =sum_{k=1}^m
   [d_k(a)(1-a)^(k-1)/k!] Z^(m+1-k).                    (22)
```

Its roots are `Z=0` and `(1-a)/(s-a)` for the `m-1` other roots `s` of
`P_K`.  Therefore the monic polynomial of the `m` valid Cayley row parameters
is

```text
W_C(T)=(-2C)^m F_(C^2)(1-T/(2C)).                       (23)
```

Equations (16), (22), and (23) are a two-step coefficient generator for every
product row.

The first two elementary symmetric functions simplify to

```text
sum_{t in T_w}t
 =(C-C^(-1))/2,                                          (24)
```

and

```text
sum_{t<u in T_w}tu
 =(K^2-1)/6-(C^2-C^(-2))/2.                              (25)
```

These follow by applying (13) and its first derivative at `a`.

## 6. Ambient Bezout matrix and projective gcd

Let `F(U,V),G(U,V)` be nonzero homogeneous binary forms of common degree `m`
over a field `k`, and put

```text
f(U)=F(U,1),
g(U)=G(U,1),
n=max(deg f,deg g)<=m.                                  (26)
```

Define `Bez_m(f,g)` from

```text
(f(X)g(Y)-f(Y)g(X))/(X-Y)                                (27)
```

in the basis `1,X,...,X^(m-1)` in each variable.

For the ordinary `n x n` Bezout matrix, write

```text
f=hf_0,
g=hg_0,
h=gcd(f,g).
```

The Cayley quotient contains `h(X)h(Y)`.  Multiplication by `h` has full column
rank, while the Bezout matrix of the coprime pair `(f_0,g_0)` is nonsingular
because its determinant is, up to a nonzero scalar, their resultant.  Hence

```text
nullity Bez_n(f,g)=deg gcd(f,g).                         (28)
```

Padding to ambient degree `m` adds `m-n` zero directions.  Homogenization to
degree `m` adds exactly the common factor `V^(m-n)` at infinity.

> **Theorem 3 (projective Bezout nullity).**  Over any field,
>
> ```text
> nullity Bez_m(f,g)=deg gcd_hom(F,G).                   (29)
> ```

The proportional case is included: both sides are then `m`.

For context, the ordinary nullspace/gcd statement is classical; see also
G. M. Diaz-Toca and M. Fioravanti, *The null space of the Bezout matrix in any
basis and gcd's*, arXiv:1402.4952.  Equation (29) is proved above, so no
characteristic-zero external theorem is load-bearing here.

## 7. Exact order-three determinantal ideal

For a normalized ratio class `r`, let

```text
mathcal B_(K,r)=Bez_m(f_r,g_r) in Mat_m(O_L).             (30)
```

For `K>=7`, define

```text
mathfrak B_(K,r)^(3)
 = ideal generated by all (m-2)x(m-2) minors
   of mathcal B_(K,r).                                   (31)
```

The characteristic-zero row forms are coprime, hence this is a nonzero ideal.
For `K=3,5`, three common row parameters are impossible for cardinality
reasons.

Let `mathfrak P` lie above a split rational prime `p==1 mod K`.  Each reduced
row form is a product of `m` distinct projective linear factors by the exact
fixed-product uniqueness theorem.  Thus its homogeneous gcd degree with the
other row is exactly their projective intersection number.  By Theorem 3,

```text
I_(1,r)(mathfrak P)>=3
 iff rank(mathcal B_(K,r) mod mathfrak P)<=m-3
 iff mathfrak B_(K,r)^(3) subset mathfrak P.              (32)
```

Define

```text
B_(K,r)^(3)=Norm_(L/Q)(mathfrak B_(K,r)^(3))             (33)
```

and remove the irrelevant rational factors `2` and `K` to obtain
`B'_(K,r)^(3)`.  Let

```text
B'_K^(3)=product_[r] B'_(K,r)^(3)                        (34)
```

over one representative of each unordered ratio class `r~r^(-1)`.

A rational split prime divides an ideal norm exactly when at least one prime
ideal above it contains the ideal.  Galois normalization of an arbitrary row
pair therefore gives:

> **Theorem 4 (exact split-prime support).**  For every odd prime `K>=7` and
> every split prime `p==1 mod K`,
>
> ```text
> p | B'_K^(3)  iff  I_K(p)>=3.                          (35)
> ```

This is an equivalence, not merely a necessary obstruction.

Consequently

```text
p does not divide B'_K^(3)
 => I_K(p)<=2
 => Delta_K(p)<=8(K-1)(K-2)
 => E_+(A)<=11(K-1)(K-2).                               (36)
```

## 8. Local diagonal form and exact valuation mass

There is a useful local strengthening of (32).  Fix a split prime ideal
`mathfrak P` away from `2K`.  After a projective coordinate change with unit
determinant one may move a residue point outside both row root sets to infinity.
Both row forms then have full affine degree `m` and distinct finite roots

```text
mu_1,...,mu_m
```

for the first row.  Its Vandermonde is a `mathfrak P`-unit.  In the Lagrange
basis at these roots, the Cayley quotient (27) is diagonal:

```text
diag(f'(mu_1)g(mu_1),...,f'(mu_m)g(mu_m)).              (37)
```

Every `f'(mu_i)` is a unit because the first row is squarefree modulo
`mathfrak P`.  Let

```text
v_i=v_mathfrakP(g(mu_i))
```

and order them as

```text
v_(1)<=...<=v_(m).
```

The valuation of the ideal generated by all `(m-2)`-minors of a diagonal
matrix is the minimum valuation of a product of `m-2` diagonal entries.  Hence

> **Theorem 5 (third-and-beyond valuation formula).**
>
> ```text
> v_mathfrakP(mathfrak B_(K,r)^(3))
>  =sum_{j=1}^{m-2} v_(j)
>  =sum_{j=1}^m v_(j)-v_(m)-v_(m-1).                    (38)
> ```

Thus the exact determinantal ideal retains precisely the local cross-evaluation
valuation mass **after the two largest entries are discarded**.  In particular
it is a unit when at most two row roots collide and becomes nontrivial exactly
at the third projective collision.  This is the weighted version of (35).

## 9. Strict refinement of the coarse subresultant support

The integer `G'_K^(3)` in `cayley_product_row_subresultants.md` is deliberately
safe.  A true three-root intersection forces its first three fixed-degree
subresultants to vanish at the same prime ideal, so on split primes

```text
support(B'_K^(3)) subset support(G'_K^(3)).              (39)
```

The inclusion can be strict because fixed-degree dehomogenized subresultants
also react to leading-degree loss.

A clean negative control is

```text
K=11,
r=7.
```

The coarse computation gives

```text
G'_(11,7)^(3)=23,                                        (40)
```

but at `p=23` the exact projective Bezout nullity in this ratio class is at
most one, and globally

```text
I_11(23)=2.                                              (41)
```

Thus `23` is not in the exact order-three support.

Exact HNF computations give

```text
Norm mathfrak B_(7,2)^(3)  =7^7,                         (42)
Norm mathfrak B_(11,2)^(3) =11^39,
Norm mathfrak B_(11,3)^(3) =11^39,
Norm mathfrak B_(11,5)^(3) =11^39,
Norm mathfrak B_(11,7)^(3) =11^41,
Norm mathfrak B_(11,10)^(3)=2^30 11^39.                 (43)
```

Therefore

```text
B'_11^(3)=1.                                             (44)
```

The exact projective theorem consequently certifies

```text
I_11(p)<=2
```

for **every** split prime `p==1 mod 11`, not merely for a finite search window.
This is a fixed-`K` exact computation, not an asymptotic theorem.

Positive finite-field controls give

```text
(K,p,r)=(17,103,10): max projective nullity=3,
(K,p,r)=(23,47,18):  max projective nullity=5,
(K,p,r)=(23,139,18): max projective nullity=3.           (45)
```

The maintained verifier checks the first design by default and the `K=23`
designs under `--extended`.  Exact HNF recomputation is available under
`--ideal-norm-small` and is intentionally omitted from default CI because it is
much slower.

## 10. Remaining closure problem

The exact support integer (34) is strictly closer to the actual finite-field
geometry than the coarse subresultant integer.  The pointwise closure target is
now

> **Exact projective divisor problem.**  Bound the largest split prime divisor
> of `B'_K^(3)` as `K` grows; in particular prove
>
> ```text
> P_split^+(B'_K^(3))<=K^(4+o(1)).                       (46)
> ```

By (35)--(36), (46) closes the pointwise quadratic Cayley-energy target for
each fixed `0<epsilon<1/4` in the regime `K<=p^(1/4-epsilon)`. To cover the
full intended regime `K=o(p^(1/4))` by this exclusion argument, a bound
`P_split^+(B'_K^(3))=O(K^4)` would suffice. The unspecified subpower loss
in (46) does not by itself suffice for that full regime.

Equation (46) is **OPEN**.

The next algebraic attack is now concrete.  Use the recurrence (16) to express
the Bezout minors through low-height functions of the two row roots, and use
(8) to work in a root basis away from `2K`.  A single nonzero rational integer
of polynomial size inside each localized exact ideal would imply the desired
split-prime bound.  The local formula (38) also shows exactly which valuation
mass such an integer must control: the third and subsequent cross-row
collisions, not the first two.

No Gaussian law, local eigenphase statistic, or microscopic BGS/GOE theorem is
claimed here.
