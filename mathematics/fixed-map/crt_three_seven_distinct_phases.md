# Distinct eigenvalues for the literal kick at \(N=3\cdot7^k\)

Let \(d_N(\tau)\) count distinct eigenvalues of the literal \(U_N(\tau)\). The proof below gives
\[
 \liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{d_{3\cdot7^k}(\tau)}{3\cdot7^k}
 \ \ge\ \frac{266517}{268912}
\]
for every fixed nonzero real algebraic \(\tau\), in particular \(10^{-80}\). The small matrix-time neighbourhood in the splitting proof can depend on \(k\). It is used to exhibit a nonzero minor; algebraic specialization gives the stated conclusion at \(N\tau\) without a smallness bound on \(N\tau\). The same conclusion holds for every real coupling outside one countable exceptional set.

The nearest published results are the first-order depth calculation on \(7^k\) in [BGS Spectrum, §§3–9](archive_continuation/earlier_routes/BGS_Spectrum.tex), its second-order primitive and depth-two refinements in [BGS Exact Splitting](archive_continuation/exact_splitting/README.md), and the bare [weak-kick CRT calculation](archive_continuation/weak_kick/BGS_Second.tex). The exact splitting result on \(7^k\) gives the lower simple-phase fraction \(18867/19208\). Here the small Fourier factor produces three distinct phase lines. The conclusion counts distinct eigenvalues of the literal kick on the \(3\cdot7^k\) tower.

The [exact CRT checker](../../computations/verification/coupling_scope_audit/crt_splitting_check.py) regenerates the finite tables from the [literal model](fixed_map_path_model.md). The argument below supplies the analytic lift, exact field independence and algebraic-time specialization separately from these finite checks.

## Small factor and untwisting

Put \(n=7^k\), \(\nu=n^{-1}\bmod3\), and \(\beta=3^{-1}\bmod n\). Use modular shift–clock labels \((u,v)\). The factor \((-1)^{uv}\) converting ordinary to modular midpoint cancels the \((-1)^v\) from the literal \(q+1/2\) shift in the \(p\)-dependent modes, since \(u=\pm1\) there. All other modes have \(u=0\). Thus the checker enumerates the actual Gaussian-integer coefficients of \(B=4000A_N\).

The identity \(e_{3n}(x)=e_3(\nu x)e_n(\beta x)\) gives \(T_{3n}(u,v)=T_3(u,\nu v)\otimes T_n(u,\beta v)\) in CRT coordinates. In the bare kernel the diagonal quadratics vanish modulo three and the cross coefficient is \(2/8=1\bmod3\); hence \(M_{3n}=F_3^\nu\otimes M_{n,\beta}\), where \(F_3^\nu(x,y)=\zeta_3^{\nu xy}/\sqrt3\) and the large kernel has its exponents multiplied by \(\beta\).

The large Weyl cocycle is \(e_n(\beta\det(v,w)/2)\) in original label coordinates. Any determinant-one split label change therefore preserves multiplication and the adjoint and is inner on the full matrix algebra. The large metaplectic factor becomes a scalar times the dilation permutation, by identical covariance on this basis. Remove that common scalar. All matrices used in the nonvanishing tests then lie in \(K=\mathbb Q(i,\zeta_3,\zeta_n)\). The automorphism \(\zeta_n\mapsto\zeta_n^3\) fixes \(i,\zeta_3,\sqrt3=-i(2\zeta_3+1)\) and removes \(\beta\). It fixes the dilation permutation, commutes with conjugation and acts on the full algebraic expressions, including Gauss sums.

On the three Fourier eigenlines the small phase labels are \(i^\nu,1,-1\). Their ratios have order dividing four, while all large-factor phase labels have odd order dividing \(m=3\cdot7^{k-1}\). Consequently a bare full cluster is indexed by one small line and one large dilation character. Compression to the small line multiplies each Weyl coefficient by the real weight \(w_\eta(Q)\), \(Q=u^2-2v^2\bmod3\) in the original labels, whose three rows are
\[
 (1,-1/2,-1/2),\qquad (1,(1+\sqrt3)/4,(1-\sqrt3)/4),\qquad
 (1,(1-\sqrt3)/4,(1+\sqrt3)/4).
\]
Every weight is nonzero and has modulus at most one. Norm classes in the large-factor calculation therefore retain exactly the same supports, whether a mode is primitive at seven or has both coordinates divisible by seven.

## Lifting and the primitive cluster

Write \(\rho^2=2\) with \(\rho\equiv3\bmod7\); the large split coordinates of a literal label \((u,v)\) are \(a=u+\rho v\) and \(b=(u-\rho v)/(-2\rho)\). The finite characters use \(\mathcal L(x)=\log(x^6)/6\) on units, with \(\mathcal L'(x)=1/x\). In the primitive block the phase of the mode \(T(a,b)\), including a possible Weyl cocycle constant \(c_0/2\), is
\[
 f(x)=b(x+a/2)+c_0/2+\ell(\mathcal L(x+a)-\mathcal L(x)),\qquad
 f'(x)=b-\ell a/[x(x+a)].
\]
The finite character factor is constant on each residue class of \(x\bmod7\). If \(x,x+a\) are units, averaging the shifts \(x\mapsto x+7^{k-h}z\), \(z\bmod7^h\), retains only \(f'(x)=0\bmod7^h\) for \(k\ge2h\). This is exact from linear expansion modulo \(7^k\). In each unit ball the Taylor remainders for the logs have denominators at worst those of \(\log(1+u)\); for shifts divisible by \(7^J,\ J\ge1\), every term of degree at least two has valuation at least \(2J\). This justifies both the shift argument and the reductions for \(h=2,3\).

At a simple stationary root \(x_*\), Hensel gives a unique lift in its class modulo seven. On this class write \(f(x)=f(x_*)+(x-x_*)^2 u(x)\) with \(u(x_*)\) a unit and \(u/u(x_*)\in1+7\mathbb Z_7\). The map \(x-x_*\mapsto(x-x_*)\sqrt{u/u(x_*)}\) permutes the ball modulo every \(7^j\), as it is the identity plus a strict contraction on that ball. The root contributes its exact critical exponential times a power of seven, its finite character, and possibly a quadratic Gauss factor over \(\mathbb F_7\). The sum over a ball is \(7\) times an ordinary quadratic sum at conductor \(7^{k-2}\); reducing that sum by two powers at a time gives a nonzero factor. A nontrivial quadratic Gauss sum times its conjugate is \(7\) by character orthogonality. All these factors are units at primes over two. For primitive \(\ell\bmod7\in\{1,2,3,5,6\}\), the two tests are the oriented off-diagonal entry and the difference of the diagonal entries. The latter uses \(c_v-c_{-v}=2i\Im c_v\) by reflection \(x\mapsto-x\), which swaps square classes and negates the Weyl label. Both coefficients are multiplied by the same real nonzero small-line weight for \(v,-v\). The two real nonprimitive labels preserve square classes and give zero in both tests. For the first-order witness the check's condition that the phase be nonzero modulo seven implies \(2x+a\ne0\bmod7\), since the logarithms vanish modulo seven. Thus the isolated coefficients from the finite check really lift to nonzero coefficients. An isolated exponent class modulo \(7^h\) remains isolated over \(\mathbb Q(i,\zeta_3,\zeta_{7^{k-h}})\); this extension has degree \(7^h\) for \(k>h\). The checker isolates a simple phase in one of the two tests for all 245 required \(\ell\bmod343\). The \(h=3\) shift test is exact for \(k\ge6\); all nonwitness contributions are supported in different phase residues, whether or not their roots are simple.

For \(\ell\equiv4\bmod7\) set \(H=I_3\otimes P_7\) where \(P_7\) is the projector on the two-dimensional large cluster, and let \(P\le H\) select the small line. For the untwisted bare matrix \(M\) let \(\lambda\) be its eigenvalue on \(P\). The Schur complement of \(e^{sB}M-zI\) on \(P\), divided by \(\lambda\), has linear coefficient \(G=PBP\) and quadratic coefficient \((G^2+J_2)/2\) for \(z=\lambda+O(s)\), where
\[
 J_2=PB(I-P)(\lambda+M)(\lambda-M)^{-1}(I-P)BP .
\]
The inverse is on \(I-P\). Expand the exponential through order two and combine the second-order terms using \(I+2M(\lambda-M)^{-1}=(\lambda+M)(\lambda-M)^{-1}\). For scalar \(G\), a nonscalar \(J_2\) splits the doublet for small nonzero imaginary \(s\). Indeed \(J_2\) is skew-Hermitian by the scalar multiplier on the complementary unit-circle eigenvalues; rescaling \(z/\lambda=1+sG+s^2b\) gives disjoint root disks at its distinct eigenvalues after including the scalar term.

The complete necessary off-diagonal table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
\ell\bmod49&4&11&18&25&32&39&46\\ \hline
\text{possible literal mode}&(0,-6)&(0,1)&(0,8)&\varnothing&\varnothing&\varnothing&\varnothing .
\end{array}
\]
There is **at most one** possible mode; it need not contribute. For example, for a listed mode put \(\ell_c=-ab/4\) and \(\ell=\ell_c+7^3\bmod7^4\). Then \(a^2/4+\ell a/b\) has valuation three, and the stationary square comparison has no root modulo \(7^4\). For the table itself the \(h=2\) shift test is enough at \(k\ge4\). If \(PBP\) is scalar, the possibly surviving large-factor matrix element must vanish, since its literal coefficient and its small-line weight are nonzero. Hermiticity gives the other orientation. Consequently \(HBH\) and \(M\) preserve each large line tensored with the whole small factor. The \(H-P\) contribution to the off-diagonal entry of \(J_2\) vanishes in characteristic zero.

Use instead the integral projector onto \(H\) at a prime over two. Its large-factor expression is the cyclic character sum divided by \(m\), which is an odd unit. Distinct \(m\)-th roots stay distinct after reduction, as \(X^m-1\) is separable. The reduced characteristic polynomial of \(F_3^\nu\) is \((X-1)^3\), and \(\sqrt3\) is an integral unit. On a complementary integral large projector of eigenvalue \(z_7\ne\lambda_7\), the determinant of \(\lambda I_3-z_7 F_3^\nu\) reduces to \((\bar\lambda_7-\bar z_7)^3\ne0\). Hence the inverse on \(I-H\) is integral by the adjugate formula, and the multiplier \((\lambda+M)(\lambda-M)^{-1}\) reduces to the identity there. Compute its off-diagonal pairing with unnormalized large characters on the two unit orbits and the same unnormalized small eigenvector on each side. These vectors and their displayed conjugates have integral entries. Use unnormalized small vectors \((0,1,-1),(1+\sqrt3,1,1),(1-\sqrt3,1,1)\). Their reductions are all \((0,1,1)\). The unnormalized small Weyl numerator is then one on every nonzero label and zero at the origin. Because \(BHB\) has vanishing off-diagonal entry between the selected vectors in characteristic zero, the witness for the reduced Schur correction is the numerator of the \(B^2\) entry, without division by any small-line norm.

For \(B^2\) the checker first retains the twelve literal modes with odd Gaussian parity, then multiplies ordered pairs using the exact midpoint cocycle \(\det(v,w)/2\), cancels equal phase-label triples modulo two, and discards the zero small labels. It verifies the unique phase \(11\bmod49\) from the mode \((0,10)\); its root is simple. The cocycle included in the table is the exact midpoint product cocycle. Above two the field extension between the full and \(49\)-reduced conductor still has degree 49 once \(k\ge4\): \(\operatorname{ord}_{7^j}(2)=3\cdot7^{j-1}\) by lifting \(2^3-1=7\), and adjoining \(\overline\zeta_3\) doubles both finite field degrees. The isolated root gives a nonzero component by the quadratic lift. The Schur correction is consequently nonscalar. On a double cluster it is anti-Hermitian, up to the scalar common term, since \((\lambda+\mu)/(\lambda-\mu)\) is imaginary for the other unit-circle phases \(\mu\). Thus the two second-order branches split when the first-order term is scalar. This proves splitting on every primitive cluster for \(k\ge6\). No finite-\(k\) assertion for \(k<6\) is needed here.

## Positive depths and disjoint first-order branches

Fix \(d\ge1\), put \(\ell=7^d\mu\), and take \(k\) sufficiently large for this fixed \(d\). The compressions in this section are for the unscaled generator \(A_N=B/4000\). All available levels of the bare character have depths \(0,\ldots,d\). For a primitive mode, two unit endpoints have derivative \(b\) modulo seven and thus cancel. Two positive depths are incompatible with \(a\) a unit. On an edge from depth zero to depth \(r\), set \(x=-a+7^rY\). The phase is
\[
 -ab/2+7^r bY+7^d\mu[\mathcal L(Y)-\mathcal L(-a+7^rY)].
\]
For \(r<d\) dividing the nonconstant part by \(7^r\) leaves a unit derivative, and the sum in \(Y\) covers the full conductor \(7^{k-r}\). Hence only \(r=d\) remains. For the two modes with \(a,b\) divisible by seven one instead sets \(x=7X\). Edges on the depth-zero class again cancel after dividing by seven. The two depth-one endpoints have a nonstationary derivative unless \(d=2\), after dividing by \(7^{\min(2,d)}\). Edges from depth one to \(r\ge2\) use \(x=7(-a_0+7^{r-1}Y)\); the competing powers of seven are \(7^{r+1}\) and \(7^d\). For \(r+1\ne d\) their unit derivative forces cancellation, with sufficient period length because \(r\le d\). Two endpoints of depth at least two are incompatible. This proves the block support using exact finite character cancellation, independently of the finite residue witness.

For the \(0,d\) block \(C\), substitute \(Y=az\) and put \(q=Q/(-2\rho)\). The stationary equation is \(qz(7^d z-1)=\mu\), with unique simple root congruent to \(-\mu/q\) modulo seven. The unit quadratic factors modulo seven are all in the same square class, since the second derivative after extracting \(7^d\) is \(-\mu/z^2\) modulo seven. The normalization of the two character vectors is \((3/7)7^{k-d/2}\). The quadratic sum has modulus \(7^{(k-d)/2}\), so the common factor has modulus \(|\kappa|=(7/3)7^{-k/2}\). These calculations remain valid after the CRT automorphism and leave each \(b_Q\) from the pinned formula multiplied by the real nonzero \(w_\eta(Q)\). For the two critical polynomials \((CC^*)_{+-}\) and \(\det C\), the finite guard isolates one exponent modulo 343 in each, on every unit \(\mu\bmod343\). It tests phases from the lifted stationary equation for \(d=1\). For \(d\ge2\) the phase is \(-q/2-7^d\mu\bmod343\), and the common shift disappears from multiplicities. Weights \(w_\eta(Q)\) preserve the nonzero coefficients, including real parts of mixed products. The cyclotomic degree argument already used above proves that \(C\) has two distinct positive singular values, giving four distinct nonzero branches.

For clarity, in the main block the contribution of each norm \(Q\) to \(C'=C/\kappa\) is
\[
 \zeta_n^{f_Q}\omega(\mu/q)^h w_\eta(Q)
 \operatorname{diag}(b_Q,\overline{b_Q})P_0^{(1-\epsilon_Q)/2},
 \qquad \epsilon_Q=(\mu/q\mid 7),
\]
where \(P_0\) swaps the two columns and \(b_Q\) sums the literal coefficients whose row class \(-a\bmod7\) is square. The opposite classes are conjugate by negating the label. The column-to-row ratio modulo seven is \(-z=\mu/q\). The tame factors at the critical point therefore have the displayed value, and all quadratic coefficients have the same square class after removing \(7^d\). In \((C'C'^*)_{+-}\) the ordered terms are the opposite-sign pairs. In \(\det C'\) the unordered terms are the same-sign pairs with nonzero \(\Re(b_Q\bar b_R)\), including the diagonal pairs. The paired coefficient for \(Q\ne R\) is proportional to \(2\Re(b_Q\bar b_R)\); thus multiplication by the real weights retains exactly this support. The trace of \(C'C'^*\) has only the same-sign differences, which suffices for its support exclusion.

At \(d\ge3\) the nonprimitive block pairs depths \(1,d-1\). The same simple-root calculation uses \(7^{d-2}\) in the stationarity equation's logarithm, and the character sum runs modulo \(7^{k-d+1}\), seven repeats of \(7^{k-d}\). Its normalization denominator remains \(3\cdot 7^{k-1-d/2}\), since the two depths sum to \(d\), just as in the main block. Each of the two opposite modes supplies one nonzero entry of modulus \(7(9/20)|w_\eta(1)\kappa|\), in opposite rows and opposite columns. Thus the block gives two further distinct branches at their positive and negative common singular value. The smaller possible ratio \(7(9/20)|w_\eta(1)|\) is greater than \(0.57\), whereas \(\|C\|/|\kappa|\le(4744-3600)/4000<0.3\). For \(d\ge4\) the retained zero block gives a seventh distinct first-order value.

At \(d=2\) the block \(D\) commutes with reflection and has form \(\alpha I+\beta P_{\rm swap}\). Its entries are in \(K'=\mathbb Q(i,\zeta_3,\zeta_{7^{k-2}})\), since the phase conductor drops by two. The quantity \(|\kappa|^2\) is rational. A shared eigenvalue with the main block would give \(u=\lambda_D^2/|\kappa|^2\in K'\) and \(u^2-u\operatorname{tr}(C'C'^*)+|\det C'|^2=0\), where \(C'=C/\kappa\). The finite check singles out one nonzero coefficient at the scaled exponent \(115\bmod343\) in the last term. Its residue modulo 49 occurs in neither the constant nor the trace term. Use the nested fields \(K''=\mathbb Q(i,\zeta_3,\zeta_{7^{k-3}})\subset K'\). The 343 residue powers are a basis over \(K''\), which contains all coefficient factors, so the unique witness makes the projection of the determinant-square onto its containing modulo-49 component nonzero. Use the 49 basis powers over \(K'\) for the whole quadratic equation: its first two terms do not have that component, and the factors \(u,u^2\in K'\) need not belong to \(K''\). Multiplying the labels by the common unit that turns norm differences into phase differences preserves this exclusion. The nonzero component cannot cancel. Thus \(D\) always gives at least a fifth distinct branch.

The additional sixth branch occurs for a fraction at least \(17/96\) of depth-two clusters. The simple-root case \(\mu=2\bmod7\) has two distinct phases modulo seven from the table in the pinned exact splitting note. For \(\mu=4\bmod7\) the discriminant \(\Delta\) is affine with unit slope in the uniform \(7\mathbb Z_7\) parameter. If \(\Delta=y_*^2\) and \(v_7(y_*)=j\ge1\), the two root balls modulo \(7^{j+1}\) can be selected by the exact shift average with \(h=2j+1\) at conductor \(7^{k-2}\). The quadratic coefficient has valuation \(j\) in both balls; after extracting that valuation the same square-root change of variables gives nonzero contributions for \(k\) sufficiently large at each fixed \(j\). Their critical values differ in valuation exactly \(3j\). Indeed the derivative is \(b_0(y^2-y_*^2)/(y^2-a_0^2/4)\). Expanding the denominator as a geometric series and integrating between \(-y_*\) and \(y_*\) gives a leading term \(-4c y_*^3/3\), \(c=-4b_0/a_0^2\) a unit. Every higher term has valuation \(>3j\): its denominator divides a product of two odd integers \(2l+1,2l+3\), and the powers of \(y_*\) dominate for \(l\ge1\). Equivalently the integrated difference has coefficient \(-4c_l/((2l+1)(2l+3))\) at \(y_*^{2l+3}\); the two odd denominators are coprime and \(7^{2l}>2l+3\). The two phase classes modulo \(7^{3j+1}\) are therefore distinct. Their Gauss and finite character factors lie in the lower conductor field for all sufficiently large \(k\). The resulting off-diagonal entry cannot vanish. First retain finitely many \(j\) and then let the cutoff grow. The fraction of squares of positive valuation in \(7\mathbb Z_7\) is \(1/16\), so the confirmed splitting fraction is at least \((1+1/16)/6\). Multiplication by \(w_\eta(1)\) preserves all these conclusions for \(D\).

## Counting and algebraic time

At fixed depth \(d\) the large cluster density is \(18/7^{d+2}\). The three small lines multiply the number of clusters and the dimension by the same factor. The proven distinct branch counts are \(2,4,5,6,7\) at depths \(0,1,2,3,\ge4\), with the additional depth-two branch in the stated fraction. Counts from different bare full clusters stay disjoint at sufficiently small nonzero real matrix time at each \(k\). For any unitary, the trace Hankel matrix \((\operatorname{tr} U^{a+b})_{0\le a,b<N}\) has rank equal to the number of distinct eigenvalues by the Vandermonde factorization with positive multiplicities. The square submatrix in its first \(d_N\) rows and columns has nonzero determinant. Its entries for \(U(t)=e^{itA_N}M_N\) are finite exponentials with algebraic coefficients and frequencies. Group the distinct frequencies, then Lindemann–Weierstrass preserves every nonzero such minor at real nonzero algebraic \(t=N\tau\). For each fixed depth cutoff this gives the lower density at fixed coupling. Let the cutoff grow after the liminf. The rational sum is
\[
 \frac{18}{49}\left(2+\frac47+\frac{5+17/96}{49}
                      +\frac6{343}+\frac1{294}\right)=\frac{266517}{268912}.
\]
For the real-parameter corollary, choose at each tower dimension a small nonzero real matrix time attaining the branch count used above (or the maximum number of distinct eigenvalues attained at that dimension). Fix its corresponding nonzero trace-Hankel minor. This is an entire function of matrix time, so its real zeros are discrete. Rescaling by \(N\) and taking a countable union over the tower gives one countable exceptional set. Every fixed real coupling outside that set satisfies the same lower bound. No nonzero real algebraic coupling belongs to this exceptional set, by the specialization argument. This does not remove possible exceptional transcendental couplings.

If \(s_N\) counts simple eigenvalues, then \(N\ge s_N+2(d_N-s_N)\), so \(\liminf s_N/N\ge132061/134456\) on this tower under the same coupling quantifiers. This is a multiplicity consequence only. The scale of the separation near zero matrix time is used only to exhibit a Hankel minor. The bound gives no control of nonzero gaps or local spectral statistics.
