# Sylvester/Fitting formulation of exact product-row support

**Status: EXACT for the Sylvester-kernel theorem, the Fitting/compound-matrix support criterion, and its equivalence to the fixed-degree gcd formulation; FINITE-REGRESSION for explicit split anchors.**

This note gives an intrinsic module-theoretic formulation of the exact split
row-intersection support.  It unifies the previously maintained coordinates

```text
Lambda : coefficients of low subresultant polynomials,
Pi     : principal subresultant coefficients,
Omega  : low coefficients of the scalar shift resultant.
```

Those are different coordinates on one determinantal rank-drop locus.

## 1. Sylvester map and its kernel

Let `k` be a field and let `P,Q in k[T]` be nonzero polynomials of the same
degree `m`, with nonzero leading coefficients.  Define the Sylvester map

```text
S_(P,Q): k[T]_<m x k[T]_<m -> k[T]_<2m,
          (U,V) |-> U P + V Q.                           (1)
```

In monomial bases this is the usual `2m x 2m` Sylvester matrix.

### Theorem 1 (kernel dimension equals gcd degree)

Let

```text
G=gcd(P,Q),
g=deg G.
```

Then

```text
dim_k ker S_(P,Q)=g.                                     (2)
```

### Proof

Write

```text
P=G P_1,
Q=G Q_1,
gcd(P_1,Q_1)=1,
deg P_1=deg Q_1=m-g.                                     (3)
```

A kernel vector satisfies

```text
U P_1 = -V Q_1.                                          (4)
```

Coprimality implies

```text
Q_1 | U,
P_1 | V.
```

Thus

```text
U=W Q_1,
V=-W P_1                                                 (5)
```

for a unique polynomial `W`.  The degree restrictions `deg U,deg V<m` are
exactly `deg W<g`, so the kernel is naturally isomorphic to `k[T]_<g`. `□`

Therefore

```text
rank S_(P,Q)=2m-g.                                       (6)
```

## 2. Order-three support as a Fitting ideal

For a square `N x N` matrix `M` over a commutative ring, write

```text
I_j(M)
```

for the ideal generated by all `j x j` minors.  For a presentation matrix of a
module, these are the standard determinantal/Fitting ideals.

Apply this to the `2m x 2m` Sylvester matrix.  Over a field, Theorem 1 gives

```text
deg gcd(P,Q)>=3
iff
rank S_(P,Q)<=2m-3
iff
I_(2m-2)(S_(P,Q))=0.                                    (7)
```

Equivalently, every `(2m-2)x(2m-2)` minor vanishes.

### Theorem 2 (compound-matrix criterion)

Let

```text
C_2(S)
```

denote the `(2m-2)`-nd compound/cofactor matrix of the Sylvester matrix, whose
entries are the signed `(2m-2)`-minors.  Then

```text
deg gcd(P,Q)>=3
iff
C_2(S_(P,Q))=0.                                          (8)
```

For `deg gcd(P,Q)=2`, this compound matrix has rank one.  For gcd degree at
most one, it is nonzero with larger rank.

### Proof

The entries of `C_2(S)` are exactly the maximal minors of size `2m-2`.
Equation (8) is therefore the standard rank criterion.  More generally, the
rank of an exterior power satisfies

```text
rank wedge^j(S)=binomial(rank S,j),                       (9)
```

so at rank `2m-2` the exterior power `wedge^(2m-2)S` has rank one. `□`

## 3. Application to the maximal-real row cofactors

For odd prime `K=2m+1`, fix a normalized row ratio `r!=1`.  Let

```text
P_(K,r),Q_(K,r) in O^+[T],
O^+=Z[tau],                                               (10)
```

be the specialization-safe fixed-degree real-trace cofactors from
`cayley_product_row_real_trace_ideal.md`.

At every split embedding prime `q|p`, their degrees remain `m`, and

```text
I_(1,r)(q)=deg gcd(P_(K,r),Q_(K,r) mod q).                (11)
```

Define the intrinsic order-three Fitting ideal

```text
J^Fitt_(K,r)=I_(2m-2)( Sylv(P_(K,r),Q_(K,r)) )
             subset O^+.                                 (12)
```

Contract it to the rationals:

```text
J^Fitt_(K,r) intersect Z = F_(K,r)^(3) Z.                (13)
```

### Corollary 3 (exact Fitting split support)

For every split rational prime `p==1 mod K`,

```text
p | F_(K,r)^(3)
iff
some embedding prime q|p has I_(1,r)(q)>=3.              (14)
```

Therefore the rational split-prime support of `F_(K,r)^(3)` is exactly the
same as the support of

```text
Lambda_(K,r)^(3),
Pi_(K,r)^(3),
Omega_(K,r)^(3).                                         (15)
```

No equality of the ideals or contracted integer valuations is asserted.

## 4. Principal subresultants are distinguished Fitting coordinates

The principal subresultant coefficients

```text
psc_0,psc_1,psc_2                                        (16)
```

are distinguished nested Sylvester minors.  The exact theorem from
`cayley_principal_subresultant_support.md` says that over every field

```text
deg gcd(P,Q)>=3
iff
psc_0=psc_1=psc_2=0.                                     (17)
```

Hence the three-scalar ideal

```text
J^psc=(psc_0,psc_1,psc_2)                                (18)
```

and the much larger all-minor Fitting ideal `J^Fitt` have the same field-valued
zero locus on the fixed-degree chart.  The Fitting ideal is intrinsic; the PSC
triple is a small canonical coordinate system cutting out the same support.

This distinction is useful:

- `J^Fitt` exposes exterior-power and determinantal identities;
- `J^psc` is computationally small;
- equality of radicals after specialization does not imply equality of
  integral valuations.

## 5. Shift coefficients as contractions of compound cofactors

Let the fixed-degree Sylvester matrix vary in one scalar parameter,

```text
S(Z)=S_0+Z S_1+Z^2 S_2+...,                              (19)
```

as in the row shift resultant.  Then

```text
det S(Z)=c_0+c_1Z+c_2Z^2+....                            (20)
```

The first coefficients are contractions of successive cofactor compounds:

```text
c_0 = det S_0,
c_1 = trace(adj(S_0) S_1),                               (21)
```

and `c_2` is the corresponding bilinear contraction of the second adjugate
(the `(2m-2)` compound of `S_0`) with the first two variation matrices.

At corank one, `adj(S_0)` has rank one and the shift transversality theorem
shows that the contraction in `c_1` is nonzero.  At corank two, the second
adjugate has rank one and the first nonzero shift coefficient is `c_2`.
At corank at least three, the second adjugate vanishes identically.

Thus the three shift coefficients `c_0,c_1,c_2` and the PSC coordinates are two
ways of probing the same nested compound-matrix stratification.

## 6. Determinantal stratification

On the fixed-degree chart, define

```text
D_d={ (P,Q): deg gcd(P,Q)>=d }.                           (22)
```

Then

```text
D_d={ rank Sylv(P,Q)<=2m-d }.                            (23)
```

These are determinantal loci with nested support

```text
D_1 superset D_2 superset D_3 superset ....              (24)
```

The project row-support problem is the arithmetic of the intersection of the
cyclotomic row family with `D_3`.

The characteristic-zero intercept theorem says that the row family avoids
`D_3` in characteristic zero.  Split exceptional primes are exactly the primes
at which the integral row family meets this third determinantal stratum after
reduction.

The inverse-ratio reflection theorem gives a smaller determinantal model:

```text
I_-1=2 deg gcd(E,O),                                     (25)
```

so inverse order-three support is already the corank-two locus of the
half-degree Sylvester matrix `Sylv(E,O)`.

## 7. Exact finite regression

`computations/verification/cayley_sylvester_fitting_support.py` checks:

1. `dim ker Sylv(P,Q)=deg gcd(P,Q)` on exact generic examples;
2. split cyclotomic row anchors with gcd degrees `0,1,2,>=3`;
3. vanishing/non-vanishing of the `(2m-2)` compound rank criterion;
4. agreement of the Fitting rank condition with the already maintained PSC
   criterion.

The verifier deliberately checks rank/nullity over finite fields rather than
contracting the enormous all-minor ideal over `O^+`; the theorem identifies the
support exactly, while `Pi` remains the preferred finite arithmetic
representation.

## 8. Why this matters for the next arithmetic step

The largest-supported-prime problem can now be formulated intrinsically:

> control split primes at which the integral Sylvester presentation acquires
> corank at least three.

This opens several exact algebraic routes that are invisible if one looks only
at one large resultant:

1. **Desnanot--Jacobi / Dodgson identities** among neighboring minors;
2. **subresultant PRS / Habicht identities** coupling consecutive PSCs;
3. **Bezoutian / reduced-resultant** descriptions of the first nonzero Fitting
   layer;
4. **Smith/Fitting invariants** of the Sylvester module;
5. exterior-power identities for the second adjugate.

The arithmetic target itself is unchanged.  A sufficient theorem is still, for
example,

```text
P_split^+( product_[r] Pi'_(K,r)^(3) ) <= K^(4+o(1)).    (26)
```

This proposed bound excludes supported split primes for each fixed
`0<epsilon<1/4` when `K<=p^(1/4-epsilon)`. It does not alone cover all of
`K=o(p^(1/4))`; an `O(K^4)` largest-split-divisor bound would suffice for
that full regime. See the quantifier distinction in
`cayley_product_row_real_trace_ideal.md`, Section 8.

The new result changes the object to attack: the exceptional prime is a
third-corank prime of a highly structured cyclotomic Sylvester module, not just
a divisor of an opaque giant integer.
