# The actual deck action: uniform sensitivity, persistent curvature, and a local differencing barrier

Research continuation, 20 October 2026. This is a further attempt at
the signed pair sum for the original matrices, with fixed
`tau=10^-80`. It proves two additional facts about the actual
long-orbit action, and then records exactly why the local additive
differencing estimate attempted here does not reach the Heisenberg
scale. It does not replace the family or supply a missing estimate
as a hypothesis.

The new proved facts are:

1. The perturbation of the continuous deck action has first and
   second displacement derivatives of order tau, with bounds
   independent of the period m, even though the action itself has
   size of order m tau. A diagonally dominant cyclic Jacobi inverse
   gives the required uniformity.
2. Its mixed second derivative at zero is bounded below by `78 tau`
   for every m. Thus the perturbation is not vanishing in long time;
   its oscillatory multiplier has a mixed second derivative of
   size at least `156 pi N tau` at zero. The tiny fixed kick cannot
   be treated as a uniformly small multiplier in this representation
   at large N.

The signed off-diagonal sum for the fixed test b_* remains
unestimated. The precise expression is retained at the end.

## 2. The continuous deck action and its cyclic Jacobi inverse

For a real displacement `nu=(nu_q,nu_p)` and any m>=1, define

$$
S_{m,\nu}(q_0,\ldots,q_{m-1})
=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}F(q_{j+1},q_j)
 -\nu_p(q_0+\nu_q/2),\qquad q_m=q_0+\nu_q.
\tag{2.1}
$$

This definition agrees with the integer sectors when nu is integer.
Its Hessian is uniformly negative definite, as follows directly
from (1.1) and the unperturbed cyclic Hessian. Therefore it has a
unique critical point and is coercive toward minus infinity.
Let `mathscr S_m(nu)` be its critical value and `mathscr S_m^0(nu)`
the corresponding value with G=0. Put

$$
\Phi_m(\nu)=\mathscr S_m(\nu)-\mathscr S_m^0(\nu).
\tag{2.2}
$$

For the estimates only, interpolate `F_sigma=F_0+sigma G`,
`0<=sigma<=1`. This is an auxiliary differentiation argument for
the fixed original phase; no quantum family is changed or averaged.
Let `q^sigma(nu)` be the unique critical position vector.

Write R for the cyclic shift on its m coordinates, counting the
coincident edges for m=1,2 with their actual multiplicities. Its
Hessian has the form

$$
H_\sigma=-{3\over2}I+{1\over4}(R+R^{-1})+\sigma H_G.
\tag{2.3}
$$

The matrix H_G is symmetric, is supported on cyclic nearest
neighbors including the diagonal, and its absolute row and column
sums are at most `4g_2`. Hence

$$
-H_\sigma={3\over2}(I-B_\sigma),\qquad
B_\sigma={1\over6}(R+R^{-1})+{2\sigma\over3}H_G,
\quad \|B_\sigma\|_1,\|B_\sigma\|_\infty<0.34.
$$

The Neumann series proves the uniform bound

$$
H_\sigma^{-1}=-{2\over3}\sum_{k\ge0}B_\sigma^k,
\qquad \|H_\sigma^{-1}\|_1,\|H_\sigma^{-1}\|_\infty<1.011.
\tag{2.4}
$$

There is also entrywise locality. If `dist_c(i,j)` is cyclic graph
distance, nearest-neighbor support makes the corresponding entry
of `B_sigma^k` zero for `k<dist_c(i,j)`. Therefore

$$
|(H_\sigma^{-1})_{ij}|
\le {2/3\over1-0.34}(0.34)^{\operatorname{dist}_c(i,j)}.
\tag{2.5}
$$

This holds also in the one- and two-coordinate cases. All constants
are independent of m,nu,sigma.

### 2.1 Displacement derivatives of the critical path

Differentiate its stationarity equations with respect to nu_q or
nu_p. The explicit forcing for nu_p is `-e_0`. The forcing for nu_q
is supported at the closing edge: it is

`F_(sigma,QQ) e_0+F_(sigma,Qq) e_(m-1)`.

When m=1 these terms are added at the same coordinate. Its l1 norm
is at most `1+2g_2<1.001`. Equation (2.4) thus gives, for a=q,p,

$$
\|\partial_{\nu_a}q^\sigma\|_1<1.02.
\tag{2.6}
$$

Equation (2.5) also shows that these derivatives decrease
geometrically with distance from the closing edge. For an edge
`Y_j=(q_(j+1),q_j)`, including `q_m=q_0+nu_q`, (2.6) implies

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\partial_{\nu_a}Y_j|_1<4.
\tag{2.7}
$$

For second displacement derivatives, differentiating stationarity
twice leaves H_sigma times `partial_(nu_a nu_b)q` on the left.
Every third derivative of F_sigma on the right is bounded by g_3,
since the third derivatives of F_0 vanish. The l1 norm of the
forcing is at most

`2g_3 sum_j |partial_(nu_a)Y_j|_1 |partial_(nu_b)Y_j|_1`
`<=32g_3`.

The factor 2 counts the two edge components; its embedding back
into the cyclic position vector has l1 operator norm at most one,
even for coincident coordinates. Equations (2.4), (2.7) give

$$
\|\partial_{\nu_a\nu_b}q^\sigma\|_1<33g_3,
\qquad
\sum_j|\partial_{\nu_a\nu_b}Y_j|_1<66g_3.
\tag{2.8}
$$

The smooth dependence used here follows from the ordinary implicit
function theorem at each finite m, since H_sigma is invertible.
The calculations (2.4)--(2.8), not an implicit uniform version of
that theorem, supply the uniform constants.

### 2.2 Uniform action sensitivity

The envelope identity for the sigma derivative gives

$$
\Phi_m(\nu)=\int_0^1\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
       G(q_{j+1}^\sigma(\nu),q_j^\sigma(\nu))\,d\sigma.
\tag{2.9}
$$

Consequently, for a,b in {q,p},

$$
\boxed{
|\Phi_m(\nu)|\le m g_0,\qquad
|\partial_{\nu_a}\Phi_m(\nu)|\le4g_1,
}
\tag{2.10}
$$

$$
\boxed{
|\partial_{\nu_a\nu_b}\Phi_m(\nu)|
\le16g_2+66g_1g_3.
}
\tag{2.11}
$$

Indeed the first differentiated integral uses (2.7). Its second
derivative has one term involving the Hessian of G and two first
path derivatives, bounded by `16g_2`, and one involving its gradient
and a second path derivative, bounded by `66g_1g_3` through (2.8).
These estimates hold for every m>=1 and every real displacement nu.

There is a useful exact periodicity as well:

$$
\Phi_m(\nu+(L^m-I)w)=\Phi_m(\nu)
\quad(w\in\mathbb Z^2).
\tag{2.12}
$$

To verify it, shift the lifted critical path at step j by L^j w.
The exact deck law for F telescopes to the same affine-quadratic
change of critical action as for F_0. Subtracting the two changes
gives (2.12). The telescoping identity is algebraic in nu and
therefore holds for real nu as well as integer nu; integrality was
needed in the odd-trace file only to deduce invariance of the
exponential phase.

## 3. An explicit nonzero curvature for every period

The actual origin is a fixed point. Its Hamiltonian Hessian and
classical derivative, calculated in the pressure file, are

$$
D^2a(0,0)=\operatorname{diag}\left(-{19606\pi^2\over125},0\right),
\qquad
A:=D\Psi(0,0)=
\begin{pmatrix}3&-4\\3s-2&3-4s\end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
s=-{9803\pi\over125}\tau<0,
\qquad T:=\operatorname{Tr}A=6-4s,
\qquad \Lambda_0+\Lambda_0^{-1}=T,quad\Lambda_0>\lambda.
\tag{3.1}
$$

We now compute the displacement Hessian without approximating the
long iterate. At a critical trajectory with
`Psi^m(q_0,p_0)=(q_0+nu_q,p_0+nu_p)`, differentiating its critical
value by the envelope rule gives exactly

$$
\nabla_\nu\mathscr S_m(\nu)
=\bigl(p_0+\nu_p/2,\ -q_0-\nu_q/2\bigr).
\tag{3.2}
$$

At nu=0 the critical trajectory is the fixed point repeated m
times. If

`A^m=((a_m,b_m),(c_m,d_m))`,

implicit differentiation of `Psi^m x-x=nu` there gives
`D_nu x=(A^m-I)^(-1)`. As `det A^m=1` and `tr A^m>2`, (3.2)
therefore yields

$$
D_\nu^2\mathscr S_m(0)
={1\over a_m+d_m-2}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 c_m&(d_m-a_m)/2\\(d_m-a_m)/2&-b_m
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.3}
$$

For G=0, the matrix is L^m and its diagonal entries a_m,d_m are
equal. Thus the mixed derivative of `mathscr S_m^0` is exactly zero
for every m.

Cayley--Hamilton, or direct diagonalization of A, gives

$$
A^m={\sinh(m\ell_0)\over\sinh\ell_0}A
       -{\sinh((m-1)\ell_0)\over\sinh\ell_0}I,
\qquad \ell_0=\log\Lambda_0.
$$

Substitution into the mixed entry of (3.3) gives the exact formula

$$
\boxed{
\partial_{\nu_q\nu_p}\Phi_m(0)
={-2s\over\sqrt{(6-4s)^2-4}}
                  \coth\left({m\log\Lambda_0\over2}\right)>78\tau
\quad(m\ge1).
}
\tag{3.4}
$$

For the explicit lower bound, `-s=(9803 pi/125)tau`, the denominator
in (3.4) is less than 6 for the stated exact tau, and coth is at
least one. Since pi>3, the quotient is greater than
`(9803/125)tau>78tau`. These deliberately coarse inequalities have
large margins.

Both critical actions have zero displacement gradient at nu=0,
by (3.2). Hence for the normalized oscillatory perturbation

$$
B_{m,N}(\nu)
=\exp\bigl(2\pi iN[\Phi_m(\nu)-\Phi_m(0)]\bigr)
$$

one has `B_(m,N)(0)=1`, `grad B_(m,N)(0)=0`, and

$$
\boxed{
|\partial_{\nu_q\nu_p}B_{m,N}(0)|
 =2\pi N\,\partial_{\nu_q\nu_p}\Phi_m(0)
 >156\pi N\tau.
}
\tag{3.5}
$$

This is an actual lower bound, uniform in the period. It excludes
a uniform C2-small-multiplier treatment of the perturbation on a
fixed displacement neighborhood as N tends to infinity. It does
not imply the absence or presence of cancellation in the completed
finite exponential sum.

## 4. What additive deck differencing would have to control

Let

$$
\mathcal G_m=\mathbb Z^2/(L^m-I)\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad D_m=|\mathcal G_m|=\lambda^m+\lambda^{-m}-2.
$$

For each integer class n let x_n be its actual periodic point. The
principal term in the exact quantum trace is

$$
\mathcal P_{m,N}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal G_m}a_{m,N}(n),
$$

$$
a_{m,N}(n)=\zeta_N^m(-1)^{n_qn_p}
 e^{2\pi iN\mathscr S_m(n)}
 \lambda^{-m/2}\Gamma_h(x_n,m).
\tag{4.1}
$$

The phase and amplitude in (4.1) are well-defined on the quotient
by the exact odd deck law. Moreover they satisfy the exact cyclic
invariance

$$
a_{m,N}(Ln)=a_{m,N}(n).
\tag{4.2}
$$

It follows from the actual phase's cyclic identity and the cyclic
product of gamma_h, not from an approximate action relation. Thus
differencing along powers of L leaves this function unchanged.

For additive differences define the full signed autocorrelation

$$
C_{m,N}(d)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal G_m}
                 a_{m,N}(n+d)\overline{a_{m,N}(n)}.
\tag{4.3}
$$

For any nonempty set H of M distinct deck classes, invariance of
summation under translation and Cauchy--Schwarz give the exact
positive inequality

$$
\boxed{
|\mathcal P_{m,N}|^2
\le {D_m\over M^2}\sum_{h,k\in H}C_{m,N}(h-k)
 ={D_m\over M^2}\sum_n\left|\sum_{h\in H}a_{m,N}(n+h)\right|^2.
}
\tag{4.4}
$$

No termwise absolute value has been introduced in (4.4).
The modulus theorem and the actual classical positive trace imply,
uniformly in any fixed range `cN<=m<=CN` with 0<c<C,

$$
C_{m,N}(0)=\sum_n|a_{m,N}(n)|^2=1+O_C(N^{-1})+O(\vartheta^{cN}),
\qquad \vartheta=1-1/8000.
\tag{4.5}
$$

Indeed the summand equals `Lambda_m(x_n)^(-1) exp(O(mh^2))`.
The sum of `Lambda_m^(-1)` differs exponentially little from the
proved `Q_m=1+O(theta^m)` containing the additional factor
`(1-Lambda_m^(-1))^(-2)`; here `Lambda_m>=5^m`.

### 4.1 The limitation of a local decorrelation estimate

If one bounds the off-diagonal autocorrelations in (4.4) individually
in absolute value, even the ideal estimates `C(d)=0` for every
nonzero difference used in H leave the upper bound

$$
{D_m\over M}C_{m,N}(0).
\tag{4.6}
$$

For `m>=cN` and `M<=N^A` with fixed A, (4.5)--(4.6) are
exponentially large in N. Even recovering the trivial unitarity
scale N^2 through this particular estimate would require
`M` of order at least `D_m/N^2`; reaching a pointwise scale N
would require M of order `D_m/N`. Both are exponential in m.

This is a precise limitation of a local, termwise decorrelation
route through (4.4), not a lower bound on the true trace and not
an impossibility theorem for signed differencing. Retaining the
full signed Gram form might be stronger; estimating that form is
exactly the missing step. With only polynomially many shifts it
would have to be of order `M^2 N/D_m` to give a pointwise N bound,
an exponentially small positive quantity.

The derivative information proved above does not supply it.
For a real displacement difference d, the phase splits exactly as

$$
\mathscr S_m(\nu+d)-\mathscr S_m(\nu)
=\mathscr S_m^0(\nu+d)-\mathscr S_m^0(\nu)
                      +\Phi_m(\nu+d)-\Phi_m(\nu).
\tag{4.7}
$$

The first term is affine in nu; the gradient of the last term is
bounded by

`(16g_2+66g_1g_3)|d|_1`

in each coordinate, by (2.11). At quantum frequency N this error
is of order `N tau |d|`. It cannot be treated as a uniformly small
error in the bare linear lattice phase as N tends to infinity.
The lower bound (3.5) shows that the underlying failure of uniform
C2 smallness is real for this map and persists for every m; it is
not merely a poorly chosen upper estimate growing with the period.

## 5. The first still unproved signed quantity

The fixed test is the one already constructed in Section 6 of the
frozen classical-pair file:

$$
\phi(\xi)=e^{-1/(1-\xi^2)}\mathbf1_{|\xi|<1},\quad
I=\int\phi,\quad g=\mathcal F^{-1}\phi,\quad
b_*={|g|^2\over2I^2},\quad
w(u)=|\widehat b_*(u)|^2.
$$

It is admissible, `supp bhat_*=[-2,2]`, and w is positive on
`(-2,2)`. In deck notation the same-orbit terms are precisely
the pairs `n' in <L>n`. Put

$$
O_{m,N}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal G_m}
 \sum_{n'\notin\langle L\rangle n}
                   a_{m,N}(n')\overline{a_{m,N}(n)}.
\tag{5.1}
$$

The previously proved full diagonal calculation, including proper
divisor periods and the exact quantum residual, gives

$$
\Delta_{2,N}(b_*)
={1\over N^2}\sum_{1\le m<2N}w(m/N)O_{m,N}
                  +\eta_*+O(N^{-1}),
$$

$$
\eta_*:=\int_1^2(u-1)w(u)\,du
\ge {e^{-196/15}\over16384}>0.
\tag{5.2}
$$

The first missing estimate remains

$$
\boxed{
{1\over N^2}\sum_{1\le m<2N}w(m/N)O_{m,N}
\longrightarrow-\eta_*.
}
\tag{5.3}
$$

The new uniform sensitivity estimates and exact curvature formula
do not establish (5.3), even for this one fixed test. The determinant
degree cutoff proves cancellation in a different, pseudo-orbit
coefficient sum; no implication from that coefficient cancellation
to the signed Gram form (4.4) or to (5.3) has been justified here.
