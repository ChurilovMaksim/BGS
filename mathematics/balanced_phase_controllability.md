# Balanced-phase regularization: a classical and quantum theorem

Status: self-contained project derivation. The Lie-algebra mechanism is related to standard quantum-control criteria; priority for this explicit specialization is not claimed. Controllability permits varying controls and is not a theorem about eigenphase statistics of one fixed kicked propagator.

## 1. An explicit pair generating su(N)

Let N>=3 be odd. On C^N use the modular-centred Weyl operators

    W(v)W(w)=exp(2 pi i det(v,w)/(2N))W(v+w),

where division by two inside the exponent is interpreted modulo N. Equivalently the scalar is exp(2 pi i (2^(-1)det(v,w) mod N)/N). Then W(v)^N=I, W(v)^*=W(-v), and the N^2 Weyl matrices form a trace-orthogonal basis.

Suppose det(v,w) is a unit modulo N. Put

    H0=(1+i)W(v)+(1-i)W(-v),
    H1=c W(w)+conjugate(c)W(-w), c!=0.

Both matrices are Hermitian and traceless.

**Theorem 1.** The real Lie algebra generated by iH0 and iH1 is su(N). The result includes composite odd N and requires no assumption on a classical map's modular period.

Proof. Write zeta=exp(2 pi i/N), d=det(v,w). The Weyl commutation relation and invertibility of d give an orthonormal basis in which W(v) has simple eigenvalues zeta^j and W(w) moves the jth basis vector to the (j+d)th with a nonzero unit-modulus weight. Thus H1 has a connected cyclic edge graph.

The eigenvalues of H0 are

    E_j=2 sqrt(2) cos(2 pi j/N+pi/4).

They are distinct. A nontrivial cosine equality would require
j+k+N/4 to be an integer multiple of N, impossible for odd N. The edge gaps are

    Delta_j=E_(j+d)-E_j
      =-4 sqrt(2) sin(pi d/N) sin(2 pi j/N+pi d/N+pi/4).

They are nonzero, and their squares are pairwise distinct. Equality of the squared sines implies either

    2(j-k)=0 mod N,

which gives j=k, or

    4(j+k+d)=(2a-1)N for some integer a,

which is impossible because its left side is divisible by four and its right side is odd.

On the skew-Hermitian edge subspace belonging to j, ad(iH0)^2 has eigenvalue -Delta_j^2. Real polynomial interpolation in these N distinct numbers isolates each edge component of iH1. Commuting that component with iH0 supplies its other real quadrature. Hence the Lie algebra contains both standard skew-Hermitian generators on every edge, after a harmless nonzero phase rotation. Their commutators give diagonal differences. Commutators along paths in the connected cycle give every off-diagonal generator. These span su(N), proving the theorem.

The proof does not merely show that the two matrices have no common invariant subspace: it constructs both quadratures on each edge, which is the additional Lie-algebra requirement.

## 2. A cat-map specialization

Let A in SL(2,Z), let U_N implement its Weyl action,

    U_N W(v) U_N^*=W(Av),

and choose an integer v with D=det(v,Av)!=0. Such D is nonzero for every nonzero integer v when A is hyperbolic. For every odd N coprime to D, Theorem 1 gives

    Lie_R(iH0, i U_N H0 U_N^*)=su(N).                      (1)

There is no prime-modulus or regular-semisimple restriction. Scalar phases of U_N have no effect.

For the Pell matrix used in this repository,

    A=(3,2;4,3), v=(1,0), D=4,

(1) holds for every odd N>=3. This matrix is identity modulo two, so the standard physical integer Fourier lift has the same exact covariance along this orbit as the modular-centred convention.

## 3. One fixed scalar perturbation works simultaneously in every admissible dimension

Let K_N be any specified traceless Hermitian background in each admissible dimension, and set

    K_N(epsilon)=K_N+epsilon H0.

**Theorem 2.** For each admissible N, at most (N^2-1)^2 real values of epsilon fail the sufficient determinant certificate for

    Lie_R(iK_N(epsilon),i U_N K_N(epsilon)U_N^*)=su(N).       (2)

In particular, outside one countable exceptional subset of R, the same epsilon satisfies (2) for every admissible odd N simultaneously. Such values exist arbitrarily close to zero.

Proof. Put D_N=N^2-1. A Lie algebra generated by two matrices is spanned by left-nested Lie words. The spaces of words of length at most L strictly increase until the generated algebra is reached: if one space stops increasing, it is invariant under both generator adjoints and all subsequent spaces coincide. Thus, at the pure H0 pair, one may select D_N independent words of lengths at most D_N.

Evaluate exactly these homogeneous words on the perturbed pair. In a fixed real basis of su(N), their coordinate determinant is a real polynomial in epsilon. Its leading term is the nonzero determinant of the selected pure H0 words, times epsilon raised to the sum of their lengths. Its degree is at most D_N^2. Therefore only finitely many real roots can make this certificate fail. Taking their countable union over N proves the assertion. A root of this particular certificate need not be a genuinely uncontrollable parameter; no equality of the exceptional sets is asserted.

A nonzero trace in the original background can be removed by subtracting a scalar identity; it changes only a global quantum phase.

## 4. Simultaneous classical regularization

Let A now be hyperbolic and g any real inversion-even observable with M1(g)<infinity. Define the single balanced harmonic

    b=(1+i)e_v+(1-i)e_(-v), g_epsilon=g+epsilon b.

The real-even part is g+epsilon(e_v+e_(-v)), and the remaining quadrature is epsilon i(e_v-e_(-v)). The exact phase identities in resonance_riesz_regularization.md give

    c_infinity(g_epsilon)
      >=4 pi^2 epsilon^4 D^2/(tr(A)^2-4)>0, epsilon!=0.      (3)

Consequently the full classical family

    lambda^(-r) C^l {g_epsilon,C^r g_epsilon}/(4 pi),
       r>R, l in Z,

has uniform upper and lower Riesz bounds once R is sufficiently large. These bounds are uniform in both window lengths. The starting R may depend on the background and epsilon.

Choose any traceless Hermitian quantization K_N of the background g and the corresponding balanced Weyl perturbation. Combining Theorems 1--2 with (3) shows that outside a countable set, one arbitrarily small nonzero perturbation simultaneously gives the classical Riesz property and full quantum control in every admissible odd dimension.

For the Pell matrix and v above, (3) reads

    c_infinity(g_epsilon)>=2 pi^2 epsilon^4,

and there are no excluded odd dimensions from gcd(N,D). One may take half this bound as a lower Riesz constant after increasing R.

## 5. What has and has not been transferred

The quantum assertion is a finite-dimensional exact Lie-algebra theorem, not a semiclassical approximation. It permits products of exponentials with independently varied real control parameters. It gives no dimension-uniform synthesis time, no quantitative mixing rate for a random control process, and no estimate on Heisenberg-scale traces of a fixed kicked map.

In particular, full controllability is compatible with special control sequences having degenerate or rigid spectra. It cannot be substituted for GOE/GUE/COE/CUE universality. The classical theorem concerns the entire translated bracket-observable family; it does not assert that its Gram matrix approximates the quantum one beyond an exact quantum return.

The exact cyclotomic verifier checks the distinct eigenvalues and distinct squared edge gaps for explicit odd prime and composite dimensions. These finite controls are separate from the all-N trigonometric proof.
