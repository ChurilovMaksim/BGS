# k=2 Cayley energy and cyclotomic exceptional primes

This note isolates the lower-dimensional `C_H` term in the M1 variance problem. The result is exact for odd prime subgroup order outside a finite, explicitly computable set of reduction primes.

## 1. Cayley transform

Let

`c(h)=(h+1)/(h-1)`

for `h!=1`. Direct algebra gives

`d(u,v)=(u-1)(v-1)/(v-u)=2/(c(u)-c(v))`.

Set `A=c(H\{1})`. Since `Delta -> 2/Delta` is a bijection of `F_N^*`, `C_H(t)` is the nonzero additive-difference representation function of `A`, up to relabelling. Hence

`sum_t C_H(t)^2 = E_plus(A)-|A|^2`,

where

`E_plus(A)=#{a-b=c-d : a,b,c,d in A}`.

The active `verify_second_moment_reduction.py` checks this equality directly.

## 2. Central symmetry and the forced baseline

For every `h!=1`,

`c(h^-1)=-c(h)`,

so `A=-A`. If `n=|A|=K-1` and `z=1_{0 in A}`, each nonzero-difference representation `(a,b)` has the forced partner `(-b,-a)`. This involution is fixed exactly when `a=-b`, giving `n-z` fixed ordered pairs. Therefore

`E_forced = 2n(n-1)-(n-z)`.

For odd `K`, `-1 notin H`, hence `z=0` and

`E_forced=(K-1)(2K-5)`.

Any excess above this value is a genuinely new additive collision.

## 3. PGL2 interpretation

If `c(k)=c(h)-x`, then

`k=g_x(h)=((2-x)h+x)/(2+x-xh)`.

The representing matrix

`M_x=[[2-x,x],[-x,2+x]]`

has determinant `4` and satisfies

`M_x M_y = 2 M_(x+y)`.

Thus `g_x` is a one-parameter unipotent subgroup of `PGL_2`, and

`r_(A-A)(x)=#{h in H\{1}:g_x(h) in H\{1}}`.

This explains why Möbius-incidence estimates are relevant, although a generic incidence theorem alone is not sharp enough for the exact near-Sidon energy obtained below.

## 4. Odd-prime K: characteristic-zero collision classification

Assume `K` is an odd prime and let `zeta` be a primitive complex `K`-th root. Write `a_i=c(zeta^i)` for `i=1,...,K-1`.

A difference collision

`a_i-a_j=a_r-a_s`,  `i!=j`, `r!=s`,

clears denominators to `P(zeta)=0`, with

`P=-X^r+X^s+X^i-2X^(s+i)+X^(r+s+i)-X^j+2X^(r+j)-X^(r+s+j)-X^(r+i+j)+X^(s+i+j)`

and exponents read modulo `K`.

Because `K` is prime, the only rational linear relation among `1,zeta,...,zeta^(K-1)` is generated by

`Phi_K(X)=1+X+...+X^(K-1)`.

The coefficients of `P` sum to zero, so `P(zeta)=0` forces equality of the two six-element exponent multisets

`{s,i,r+s+i,r+j,r+j,s+i+j}`
`={r,s+i,s+i,j,r+s+j,r+i+j}`.

The active verifier classifies all `6!=720` possible matchings as exact integer linear systems in `(i,j,r,s)`:

- ranks over `Q`: `30` systems of rank 2, `240` of rank 3, `450` of rank 4;
- gcds of maximal minors show that the rank can drop only in characteristic `2`;
- rank 2: `26` systems force a zero exponent or a diagonal pair, `2` are identity, `2` are inversion-swap;
- rank 3: `236` force a zero exponent or a diagonal pair, and the remaining `4` are identity lines;
- rank 4 has only the zero solution.

Thus no one-dimensional kernel was omitted: for every odd prime `K`, the characteristic-zero Cayley difference set has only the two forced collision mechanisms

`(r,s)=(i,j)`

and

`(r,s)=(-j,-i)`.

This is a small exact computer-assisted linear classification. A shorter hand proof or Lean formalization is still preferable before publication.

## 5. Reduction modulo N and the exact exceptional set

Finite-field collisions may nevertheless appear after reduction. Galois scaling lets us normalize `i=1`. For every non-forced triple `(j,r,s)`, define the nonzero algebraic norm

`R_(j,r,s)=|Res(Phi_K,P_(1,j,r,s))|`.

Let `D_K` be the product of these norms; only its radical matters.

For a rational prime `N == 1 (mod K)`:

- a non-forced collision in the order-`K` subgroup of `F_N^*` implies `N | D_K`;
- conversely, if `N | D_K`, then `Phi_K` and one collision polynomial share a root modulo `N`; because `N == 1 (mod K)`, that root lies in `F_N` and has exact order `K`, producing a genuine non-forced collision.

Hence the exceptional primes are exactly

`E_K={N prime : N == 1 (mod K), N | D_K}`.

Every conjugate of a collision polynomial has absolute value at most `12`, so every exceptional prime also satisfies the crude fixed-`K` bound

`N <= 12^(K-1)`.

This proves finiteness for each fixed odd prime `K`; it is not a useful uniform exclusion when `K` itself grows.

## 6. Exact small-K exceptional sets

The default verifier computes the resultants exactly for two audited subgroup orders:

`E_11={23,67,89}`,

`E_13={53,79,131,157,313}`.

The finite checks behave exactly as predicted: `N=67,K=11` and `N=53,K=13` have excess collision energy, while `N=199,K=11` and `N=521,K=13` have none.

Exploratory calculations also produce exact finite sets for `K=17,19`; they are not part of the routine gate because `K=11,13` already certify the mechanism without making every verification run heavier.

## 7. Exact energy outside the exceptional set

Let `K` be an odd prime and let `N == 1 (mod K)` be prime with `N notin E_K`. Then only the forced representations remain. Therefore

`sum_t C_H(t)^2=(K-1)(2K-5)`

and equivalently

`E_plus(A)=3(K-1)(K-2)`.

Since

`sum_t C_H(t)=(K-1)(K-2)`

and `C_H(0)=0`, the exact population variance over `F_N^*` is

`Var_(t!=0) C_H(t)`
`=(K-1)(2K-5)/(N-1)`
` -(K-1)^2(K-2)^2/(N-1)^2`.

Hence the mixed M1 layer

`M(t)=2(N-2)C_H(t)`

satisfies

`std_(t!=0) M(t)`
`<=2(N-2)sqrt((K-1)(2K-5)/(N-1))`
`=O(K sqrt(N))`.

This is exactly the scale required by the global M1 variance target.

## 8. What remains open

The `k=2` collision-energy component is therefore closed for odd prime `K` outside the explicit cyclotomic exceptional set. It is not yet a uniform theorem through all exceptional primes or for composite `K`.

The dominant unresolved component is the pure off/off elliptic-family term `O(t)` and its covariance with the now-controlled mixed term. The active route is to exploit its non-isotrivial elliptic pencil, classify geometric parameter symmetries, and then apply a trace-function correlation theorem only after the sheaf hypotheses are verified.
