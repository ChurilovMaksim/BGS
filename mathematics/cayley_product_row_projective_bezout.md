# Projective Bezout support and a universal polynomial for Cayley product rows

**Status: EXACT for the universal row polynomial, its differential recurrence, the projective Bezout rank criterion, and the exact split-prime support theorem; FINITE-REGRESSION for the explicit HNF and finite-field anchors below; OPEN for a growing-`K` bound on the exact support integer.**

This note refines `cayley_product_row_subresultants.md` in two ways.

First, it removes a genuine overcount in the order-three subresultant integer.  A
fixed-degree dehomogenized subresultant can vanish because both row forms lose
leading degree modulo a prime, even when the two **projective** rows share fewer
than three parameters.  The ambient Bezout matrix keeps the point at infinity
inside the geometry and gives an exact rank criterion.

Second, all prime-order row polynomials are generated from one elementary
polynomial

```text
P_K(Y)=sum_{j=0}^{(K-1)/2} binom(K,2j) Y^j.
```

It satisfies a second-order differential equation.  Consequently every row
coefficient is generated by a two-step recurrence rather than by a growing
cyclotomic product.  This is intended as the next algebraic input for bounding
the exact determinantal ideal.

No growing-`K` prime bound is proved here.

## 1. Setup

Let `K` be an odd prime, put

```text
m=(K-1)/2,
L=Q(zeta_K),
O_L=Z[zeta_K],
```

and use the Cayley map

```text
c(x)=(x+1)/(x-1).
```

For a split rational prime

```text
p==1 (mod K),
```

let `H<=F_p^*` be the subgroup of order `K`.  The exact fixed-product-row
reduction in `cayley_product_row_intersections.md` gives

```text
Delta_K(p)<=4 I_K(p)(K-1)(K-2),                         (1)
```

where `I_K(p)` is the largest number of common nonzero Cayley parameters
between two distinct noncentral product rows.

The current subresultant note constructs homogeneous degree-`m` forms

```text
R_i(U,V)=prod_{a in A_i}(D_(i,a) U-N_i V),              (2)
N_i=1-zeta_K^(2i),
D_(i,a)=zeta_K^i(zeta_K^a+zeta_K^(-a))-2,
```

whose projective roots are exactly the row parameters in the coordinate
`mu=t/(2-t)`, including `t=2` as `[U:V]=[1:0]`.

For a normalized row pair `(1,r)`, write

```text
f_r(U)=R_1(U,1),
g_r(U)=R_r(U,1).                                         (3)
```

Both have ambient degree `m`; after reduction modulo a prime ideal their actual
affine degrees may be smaller because a projective root has moved to infinity.

## 2. One universal polynomial for all trace classes

Define

```text
P_K(Y)=sum_{j=0}^m binom(K,2j)Y^j.                       (4)
```

Equivalently,

```text
P_K(X^2)=((X+1)^K-(X-1)^K)/2.                           (5)
```

Let `zeta` be a nontrivial `K`-th root of unity and put

```text
C=c(zeta).
```

Since

```text
(C+1)/(C-1)=zeta,
```

we have

```text
(C+1)^K-(C-1)^K=0,
```

and therefore

```text
P_K(C^2)=0.                                              (6)
```

The values `c(zeta^a)^2` are identified exactly under `a~-a`.  There are `m`
such nontrivial classes, while `P_K` has degree `m` and leading coefficient
`K`.  Hence:

> **Theorem 1 (universal trace polynomial).**  In characteristic zero, the
> roots of `P_K` are exactly
>
> ```text
> {c(zeta^a)^2 : a in F_K^*/{+-1}}.                     (7)
> ```
>
> For a split prime `p==1 mod K`, the same identity after reduction gives the
> `m` distinct squared Cayley trace values of the nontrivial elements of `H`.

The distinctness in the split reduction follows directly from the inverse
formula for the Cayley transform and the identification `c(x^-1)=-c(x)`.

## 3. Differential equation and derivative recurrence

The polynomial (4) satisfies the exact differential equation

```text
2Y(Y-1) P_K''(Y)
 +(((3-2K)Y)-1) P_K'(Y)
 +(K(K-1)/2) P_K(Y)=0.                                  (8)
```

### Proof

Write

```text
P_K(Y)=sum_j b_j Y^j,
b_j=binom(K,2j).
```

The coefficient of `Y^j` in the left side of (8) vanishes precisely when

```text
[2j^2+(1-2K)j+K(K-1)/2] b_j
 =(j+1)(2j+1)b_(j+1),                                   (9)
```

which is the elementary binomial-ratio identity obtained from
`b_(j+1)/b_j`.

Now let `a` be a root of `P_K`, and define

```text
d_n(a)=P_K^(n)(a)/P_K'(a),
d_0=0,
d_1=1.                                                  (10)
```

In characteristic zero, and in every split reduction, `P_K'(a)!=0`.  Differentiate
(8) `n` times and evaluate at `a`.  Since the coefficient of `P_K''` is
quadratic and that of `P_K'` is linear, only three neighboring derivative
orders survive.  One obtains

```text
2a(a-1)d_(n+2)
 +(((4n+3-2K)a)-(2n+1))d_(n+1)
 +(2n^2+(1-2K)n+K(K-1)/2)d_n=0.                         (11)
```

Thus all normalized derivatives of `P_K` at a row root are generated by a
second-order recurrence.

## 4. Universal monic row polynomial

Fix a noncentral row square root `w`, set

```text
C=c(w),
a=C^2.                                                   (12)
```

For a second subgroup parameter `z`, put

```text
S=c(z),
s=S^2.
```

The Cayley multiplication identity gives

```text
c(wz)+c(wz^(-1))
 =2C(1-s)/(a-s).                                         (13)
```

Introduce

```text
Z=(1-a)/(s-a).                                           (14)
```

Then (13) becomes simply

```text
t=2C(1-Z).                                               (15)
```

The invalid trace `z=w^(+-1)` is exactly the deleted root `s=a`.  The diagonal
pair `z=1`, for which `c(z)` is infinite, becomes the finite source `Z=0`.

Define

```text
F_a(Z)
 =Z^(m+1) P_K(a+(1-a)/Z) / ((1-a)P_K'(a)).              (16)
```

Taylor expansion at the root `a` shows that `F_a` is a monic polynomial of
degree `m`, divisible by `Z`, with

```text
F_a(Z)
 =sum_{k=1}^m
   [d_k(a)(1-a)^(k-1)/k!] Z^(m+1-k).                    (17)
```

Its roots are

```text
Z=0
```

and

```text
Z=(1-a)/(s-a)
```

for the `m-1` other roots `s` of `P_K`.  Therefore the monic polynomial whose
roots are the `m` valid Cayley parameters in the row is

```text
W_C(T)=(-2C)^m F_(C^2)(1-T/(2C)).                       (18)
```

Equations (11), (17), and (18) give a two-step coefficient generator for every
row polynomial.

Two low coefficients simplify especially strongly.  If `T_w` denotes the
unordered parameter set of the row, then

```text
sum_{t in T_w} t
 =(C-C^(-1))/2,                                          (19)
```

and

```text
sum_{t<u in T_w} tu
 =(K^2-1)/6-(C^2-C^(-2))/2.                              (20)
```

These identities follow from (8) and its first derivative at `a`; no finite
computation is used in their proof.

## 5. Ambient Bezout matrix and projective gcd

Let `F(U,V),G(U,V)` be two nonzero homogeneous binary forms of the same degree
`m` over a field `k`, and put

```text
f(U)=F(U,1),
g(U)=G(U,1),
n=max(deg f,deg g)<=m.                                  (21)
```

Define the **ambient degree-`m` Bezout matrix** `Bez_m(f,g)` by expanding

```text
(f(X)g(Y)-f(Y)g(X))/(X-Y)                                (22)
```

in the basis

```text
1,X,...,X^(m-1)
```

in both variables.  If `n<m`, the final `m-n` rows and columns are zero.

For the ordinary `n x n` Bezout matrix, factor

```text
f=h f_0,
g=h g_0,
```

with `h=gcd(f,g)`.  The Cayley quotient (22) contains the factor `h(X)h(Y)`.
Multiplication by `h` has full column rank, while the Bezout matrix of the
coprime pair `(f_0,g_0)` is nonsingular because its determinant is, up to a
nonzero scalar, their resultant.  Hence

```text
nullity Bez_n(f,g)=deg gcd(f,g).                          (23)
```

Padding from `n` to ambient degree `m` adds `m-n` zero directions.  On the
other hand, homogenization to degree `m` adds exactly the common factor

```text
V^(m-n)
```

at infinity.  Therefore:

> **Theorem 2 (projective Bezout nullity).**  Over any field,
>
> ```text
> nullity Bez_m(f,g)=deg gcd_hom(F,G),                    (24)
> ```
>
> where the right side is the degree of the homogeneous projective gcd.

The proportional case is included: both sides are then `m`.

This is the projective form of the classical Bezout-nullspace theorem.  A
modern reference for the ordinary nullspace/gcd statement is G. M.
Diaz-Toca and M. Fioravanti, *The null space of the Bezout matrix in any basis
and gcd's*, arXiv:1402.4952; equation (24) itself is proved above so no
characteristic-zero external theorem is load-bearing here.

## 6. Exact order-three determinantal ideal

Return to the cyclotomic row forms (2), and fix a normalized ratio class `r`.
Let

```text
B_(K,r)=Bez_m(f_r,g_r) in Mat_m(O_L).                    (25)
```

For `K>=7`, define the ideal

```text
mathfrak B_(K,r)^(3)
 = ideal generated by all (m-2)x(m-2) minors of B_(K,r). (26)
```

The characteristic-zero row forms are coprime, so `B_(K,r)` has full rank and
(26) is a nonzero ideal.  For `K=3,5`, three common row parameters are
impossible for cardinality reasons and may be treated separately.

Let `P` be a prime ideal of `O_L` above a split rational prime `p==1 mod K`.
The reduced row form is a product of `m` **distinct** projective linear factors:
fixed-product row uniqueness gives one unordered subgroup pair per parameter.
Thus its homogeneous gcd degree with another row form is exactly the number of
common row parameters.

By Theorem 2,

```text
I_(1,r)(P)>=3
 iff rank(B_(K,r) mod P)<=m-3
 iff every (m-2)-minor lies in P
 iff mathfrak B_(K,r)^(3) subset P.                      (27)
```

Define the positive rational integer

```text
B_(K,r)^(3)=Norm_(L/Q)(mathfrak B_(K,r)^(3),             (28)
```

and let `B'_(K,r)^(3)` denote (28) after removing factors `2` and `K`.  Finally
put

```text
B'_K^(3)=product_[r] B'_(K,r)^(3)                        (29)
```

over one representative of each unordered ratio class `r~r^(-1)`.

Because a split rational prime divides an ideal norm exactly when one of its
prime ideals divides the ideal, Galois normalization of an arbitrary row pair
gives:

> **Theorem 3 (exact split-prime support).**  For every odd prime `K>=7` and
> every split prime `p==1 mod K`,
>
> ```text
> p | B'_K^(3)  iff  I_K(p)>=3.                          (30)
> ```

This is an equivalence, not merely an obstruction.

Consequently

```text
p does not divide B'_K^(3)
 => I_K(p)<=2
 => Delta_K(p)<=8(K-1)(K-2)
 => E_+(c(H\{1}))<=11(K-1)(K-2).                        (31)
```

## 7. Strict refinement of the coarse subresultant support

The integer `G'_K^(3)` in `cayley_product_row_subresultants.md` is deliberately
safe: if a row pair has at least three common projective roots, its first three
fixed-degree subresultants vanish at the same prime ideal.  Therefore, on split
primes,

```text
support(B'_K^(3)) subset support(G'_K^(3)).              (32)
```

The inclusion can be strict because separate coefficient norms and
fixed-degree dehomogenized subresultants can remember leading-degree loss that
does not amount to three common projective roots.

A concrete exact negative control occurs at

```text
K=11,
r=7.
```

The coarse computation gives

```text
G'_(11,7)^(3)=23.                                        (33)
```

At the split prime `p=23`, however, the exact projective Bezout nullity is at
most one in this ratio class; globally `I_11(23)=2`, so `23` is not in the
order-three support.

Exact HNF computations of the projective determinantal ideals give

```text
Norm mathfrak B_(7,2)^(3)  =7^7,                         (34)
Norm mathfrak B_(11,2)^(3) =11^39,
Norm mathfrak B_(11,3)^(3) =11^39,
Norm mathfrak B_(11,5)^(3) =11^39,
Norm mathfrak B_(11,7)^(3) =11^41,
Norm mathfrak B_(11,10)^(3)=2^30 11^39.                 (35)
```

Hence

```text
B'_11^(3)=1.                                             (36)
```

So the exact projective theorem certifies

```text
I_11(p)<=2
```

for **every** split prime `p==1 mod 11`, not just for primes in a finite search.
This is a fixed-`K` exact computation, not an asymptotic theorem.

As positive controls, the projective Bezout nullity reproduces

```text
(K,p,r)=(17,103,10): max nullity=3,
(K,p,r)=(23,47,18):  max nullity=5,
(K,p,r)=(23,139,18): max nullity=3.                      (37)
```

## 8. Remaining closure problem

The exact support integer (29) is strictly closer to the true geometry than the
coarse subresultant integer.  The pointwise closure target becomes

> **Exact projective divisor problem.**  Bound the largest split prime divisor
> of `B'_K^(3)` as `K` grows; in particular, prove
>
> ```text
> P_split^+(B'_K^(3))<=K^(4+o(1)).                       (38)
> ```

By (30)--(31), (38) closes the pointwise quadratic Cayley-energy target in the
intended regime `K=o(p^(1/4))`.

Equation (38) is **OPEN**.

The universal recurrence (11) gives a concrete next attack.  Instead of
estimating all large cyclotomic minors separately, derive low-height
`O_L`-linear combinations of the determinantal minors from the coefficients of
`W_C`.  A single nonzero rational integer of polynomial size inside each exact
ideal (26), after removing the inert `2,K` factors, would give the required
split-prime bound.  No such polynomial-size generator is claimed here.

No Gaussian law, local eigenphase statistic, or microscopic BGS/GOE theorem
follows from this note.
