# Cyclotomic row-support source audit — 2026-09-05

This file records the source check behind
`mathematics/cayley_trace_intercept_rigidity.md`.  It distinguishes theorem
inputs actually used from adjacent literature and unresolved leads.

## 1. Load-bearing source: Bass 1966

H. Bass, **Generators and Relations for Cyclotomic Units**, *Nagoya
Mathematical Journal* **27** (1966), 401--407,
DOI `10.1017/S0027763000026258`.

The project uses the prime-conductor specialization of Bass's prime-power
Theorem 3.  In Bass's notation the multiplicative group generated by the
numbers `1-zeta^a`, modulo torsion, is free on one Galois orbit modulo complex
conjugation.  For prime `K`, this gives a `Z`-basis represented by

```text
1-zeta^j, 1<=j<=(K-1)/2.
```

Consequently the positive absolute values

```text
|1-zeta|,...,|1-zeta^((K-1)/2)|
```

are multiplicatively independent: a relation among their absolute values,
after squaring and replacing `1-zeta^(-j)` by a root of unity times
`1-zeta^j`, becomes a relation in Bass's torsion-free group.

This is the external theorem used in the characteristic-zero affine-intercept
classification.  The rest of that classification is derived explicitly in the
project note.

## 2. Closely related characteristic-zero source: Fu 2022

H. Fu, **Projective Equivalence for the Roots of Unity**, *Journal of
Algebraic Combinatorics* **56** (2022), 823--871, arXiv:1905.01510.

Relevant mechanisms:

- equality of cross-ratios of roots of unity is converted into products of
  cyclotomic differences;
- the paper states the Bass/Ennola/Washington description of multiplicative
  relations among `e_k=|zeta_n^k-1|` (Theorem 15 in the arXiv text);
- those relations are then used for projective-equivalence classification.

The present row-intercept theorem is more special: prime conductor and one
affine trace map.  It is proved directly in the project rather than imported
from Fu.  Fu is cited as nearby mechanism/prior art, not as the theorem that
closes the row fibres.

## 3. Unresolved lead: Dvornicich--Zannier 2002

R. Dvornicich and U. Zannier, **Sums of roots of unity vanishing modulo a
prime**, *Archiv der Mathematik* **79** (2002), 104--108,
DOI `10.1007/S00013-002-8291-4`.

The abstract states that a Conway--Jones type inequality for vanishing sums of
roots of unity has an analogue for congruences modulo a prime.  This is
potentially relevant to sparse row relations.

**Current status: LEAD ONLY.**

The project needs relations in one chosen residue embedding / prime ideal of a
cyclotomic field.  The exact hypotheses of the 2002 theorem have not yet been
matched to that setting from the full theorem text.  No consequence is used in
`claim_ledger.md` or the manuscript.

A naive strengthening is definitely false: the maintained Fibonacci collision
slice supplies short finite-field root-of-unity relations for arbitrarily large
prime `K` at suitable finite reductions.  Therefore no statement of the form

```text
short relation modulo one finite embedding => K is bounded
```

may be imported without its exact arithmetic hypotheses.

## 4. What the literature check changes in the active route

The characteristic-zero part is now closed by a verified primary theorem
(Bass) plus an explicit project derivation.  The positive-characteristic
order-three problem remains a simultaneous divisibility problem:

```text
q | (c_0,c_1,c_2)
```

for the first three coefficients of the scalar shift resultant, equivalently a
collision problem between the two explicit cyclotomic-unit lists

```text
W_r(b), V_r(a).
```

The highest-value literature search is therefore no longer generic
root-of-unity linear independence.  It is for **uniform gcd / S-unit / norm
bounds in varying prime cyclotomic fields** that can control a common prime
ideal dividing several explicit differences of cyclotomic units.

Any future theorem is to be promoted only after checking:

1. whether it applies to one prime ideal or only to a rational congruence;
2. coefficient/height and support hypotheses;
3. dependence of constants on the varying field degree `K-1`;
4. whether it controls the largest rational split prime, not merely total norm;
5. compatibility with the existing Fibonacci and inverse-row finite
   counterexamples.
