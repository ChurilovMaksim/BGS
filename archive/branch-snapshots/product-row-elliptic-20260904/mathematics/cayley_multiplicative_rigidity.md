# Multiplicative rigidity of the prime-order Cayley set

**Status: EXACT for every odd prime `K`.  FINITE-REGRESSION for the maintained small reductions.**

This note proves the multiplicative analogue of the characteristic-zero
additive collision classification.  It is useful for the torus--unipotent /
Minkowski reformulation of the Cayley-energy problem: the same Cayley set that
has highly structured additive fibers is also an exact multiplicative
near-Sidon object in characteristic zero.

Let `K` be an odd prime, let `zeta` be a primitive `K`-th root of unity, and
put

```text
c(z)=(z+1)/(z-1),
A_K={c(zeta^a): a in F_K^*}.                              (1)
```

Thus `|A_K|=K-1`, `A_K=-A_K`, and `0 notin A_K`.

## 1. A trace parametrization of an ordered pair

Because `2` is invertible modulo `K`, every ordered pair

```text
(h_1,h_2)=(zeta^a,zeta^b),  a,b!=0,                     (2)
```

has a unique representation

```text
h_1=z/v,
h_2=z v,                                                (3)
```

with `z,v in mu_K`.  In exponent coordinates,

```text
z=zeta^((a+b)/2),
v=zeta^((b-a)/2).                                        (4)
```

The conditions `h_1,h_2!=1` are exactly

```text
z!=v,
z!=v^(-1).                                              (5)
```

Write

```text
T_x=x+x^(-1).                                            (6)
```

A direct calculation gives

```text
c(z/v)c(zv)=(T_z+T_v)/(T_z-T_v).                        (7)
```

The denominator is nonzero precisely because of (5).

## 2. A four-point trace-product lemma

### Lemma 1
Let `x,y,u,v in F_K`.  If the multisets

```text
{ x+y, x-y, -x+y, -x-y }
```

and

```text
{ u+v, u-v, -u+v, -u-v }
```

are equal, then `(u,v)` is obtained from `(x,y)` by independent sign changes
and possibly a swap.

### Proof
Put

```text
r=x+y,  s=x-y.
```

The first multiset is simply

```text
{r,-r,s,-s}.                                             (8)
```

Since `2` is invertible, `(x,y)=((r+s)/2,(r-s)/2)`.
Equality of the two multisets says that the corresponding pair `(r',s')` is
obtained from `(r,s)` by independent sign changes and a swap.  Conjugating
these signed permutations by the invertible Hadamard map

```text
(x,y) -> (x+y,x-y)
```

again gives exactly independent sign changes and a swap of `(x,y)`.  This
also covers the degenerate cases `x=0`, `y=0`, and `x=+-y` because the
statement is multiset-valued.

## 3. Characteristic-zero product collision classification

### Theorem 2 (multiplicative Cayley rigidity)
For `a,b,c,d in F_K^*`,

```text
c(zeta^a)c(zeta^b)=c(zeta^c)c(zeta^d)                   (9)
```

if and only if the ordered pair `(c,d)` is one of

```text
(a,b),
(b,a),
(-a,-b),
(-b,-a).                                                 (10)
```

### Proof
Write the first pair as `(z/v,zv)` and the second as `(u/w,uw)` as in
(3).  Equation (7) gives

```text
(T_z+T_v)/(T_z-T_v)=(T_u+T_w)/(T_u-T_w),
```

and hence

```text
T_z T_w=T_u T_v.                                        (11)
```

Write

```text
z=zeta^r, v=zeta^s, u=zeta^q, w=zeta^t.
```

Expanding (11) gives a rational linear relation among the `K`-th roots of
unity.  Its coefficient sum is `4-4=0`.  Since the minimal polynomial of
`zeta` is

```text
Phi_K(X)=1+X+...+X^(K-1),                                (12)
```

any rational relation of degree at most `K-1` is a scalar multiple of
`Phi_K`; the zero coefficient sum forces that scalar to vanish.  Therefore
(11) is equivalent to equality, with multiplicity, of the exponent multisets

```text
{r+t,r-t,-r+t,-r-t}
 = {q+s,q-s,-q+s,-q-s}.                                 (13)
```

Lemma 1 says that `(q,s)` is a signed permutation of `(r,t)`.

The swapped alternative would force

```text
s=+-r,                                                   (14)
```

which is forbidden by (5) for the first Cayley pair.  Hence

```text
q=+-r,
t=+-s.                                                   (15)
```

The four sign choices translate back through

```text
a=r-s,
b=r+s,
c=q-t,
d=q+t
```

exactly to the four pairs in (10).

The converse follows from commutativity and

```text
c(z^(-1))=-c(z).                                         (16)
```

## 4. Exact characteristic-zero multiplicative energy

Put

```text
n=K-1.
```

The four transformations in (10) form the Klein four-group generated by

```text
(x,y)->(y,x),
(x,y)->(-x,-y).                                          (17)
```

On `A_K^2`, a generic orbit has size four.  The fixed loci of the two
reflections are

```text
x=y,
x=-y,                                                    (18)
```

with `n` ordered pairs each; they are disjoint because `0 notin A_K`.
Thus `2n` ordered pairs lie in orbits of size two and the remaining
`n^2-2n` lie in orbits of size four.

Therefore:

### Corollary 3
The multiplicative energy of the characteristic-zero Cayley set is exactly

```text
E_x(A_K)
 =2(2n)+4(n^2-2n)
 =4n(n-1)
 =4(K-1)(K-2).                                          (19)
```

Equivalently, every product collision is forced by swap and central
inversion.

## 5. Relation to the trace orbit

Equation (7) gives another useful exact consequence.  If

```text
h_1=z/v, h_2=zv,
h_3=u/w, h_4=uw,
```

then

```text
c(h_1)c(h_2)=c(h_3)c(h_4)
 iff
T_z/T_v=T_u/T_w.                                        (20)
```

Thus multiplicative Cayley energy is a weighted multiplicative-ratio energy of
the trace orbit

```text
T_H={h+h^(-1):h in H}.                                  (21)
```

Moreover

```text
T_z T_v=T_(zv)+T_(z/v),                                  (22)
```

and, for odd `K`, the map

```text
(z,v)->(zv,z/v)                                          (23)
```

is a bijection of `mu_K^2`.  Hence the indexed trace multiset has an exact
self-duality between its weighted multiplicative and additive energies.  This
identity is one reason the trace-coordinate formulation is more structured
than a generic Mobius-incidence problem.

## 6. Finite-field boundary

Theorem 2 is a characteristic-zero statement.  After reduction at a split
prime `p==1 (mod K)`, additional product collisions can occur, just as for
additive Cayley differences.  For example,

```text
K=11, p=23:
E_x(A)=552,
4(K-1)(K-2)=360.
```

On the other hand, in tested large split primes the exact baseline often
persists; e.g. the first split primes above `K^4` for
`K=11,13,17,19,23,29,31,37,43` all satisfy (19) after reduction.
That observation is finite evidence only.

The companion note `cayley_product_discriminant.md` compresses all additional
finite-field product collisions into a single discriminant and supplies the
exact valuation criterion.
