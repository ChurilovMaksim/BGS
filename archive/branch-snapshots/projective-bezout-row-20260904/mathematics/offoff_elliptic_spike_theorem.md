# Off/off elliptic correlation after the reducible-fiber spike

**Status: EXACT modulo explicitly cited standard theorems (Igusa/open image and Deligne RH for curves); FINITE-REGRESSION for the symbolic discriminant, spike factorization and promoted finite designs.**

This note repairs the historical off/off elliptic draft.  The decisive correction is
that every generic parameter class has one reducible fiber with about `2p` valid
points.  Those spikes are coherent when the Cayley difference parameter agrees,
so they cannot be hidden inside a generic square-root trace-correlation error.
After extracting them explicitly, the remaining elliptic trace functions are
uniformly quasi-orthogonal.

The resulting unconditional bound is

```text
Var_{t!=0} O(t)
 << p E_+(c(H\{1})) + K^2 p + K^4 sqrt(p) + K^4.       (1)
```

Thus the elliptic/sheaf layer is no longer an independent pointwise barrier.
In the intended `K=o(p^(1/4))` regime, the only superquadratic arithmetic term
in (1) is exactly the Cayley additive-energy problem already isolated in the
mixed `k=2` layer.

Throughout, `p>3` is prime, `H<=F_p^*` has odd order `K`, and

```text
c(x)=(x+1)/(x-1),
A=c(H\{1}).                                              (2)
```

The off/off notation is that of `fourth_moment_reduction.md`.

## 1. Parameter classes and Cayley coordinates

For an off-diagonal pair key `(u,v)`,

```text
d(u,v)=(u-1)(v-1)/(v-u).                                (3)
```

For `lambda=(h1,h2) in H^2`, let `O_lambda(t)` count valid off-diagonal
`(u,v)` for which the second key `(h1/u,h2/v)` is also off-diagonal and

```text
d(u,v)+d(h1/u,h2/v)=t.                                  (4)
```

Then

```text
O(t)=sum_{lambda in H^2} O_lambda(t).                    (5)
```

If `h1,h2!=1`, put

```text
alpha=c(h1),  beta=c(h2),
s=alpha+beta, d=alpha-beta.                              (6)
```

With `a=c(u)`, `b=c(v)` and

```text
T_alpha(a)=(alpha*a-1)/(a-alpha),                        (7)
```

(4) becomes

```text
2/(a-b)+2/(T_alpha(a)-T_beta(b))=t.                      (8)
```

The generic parameter set is

```text
Lambda_g={
 (h1,h2) in H^2:
 h1!=1, h2!=1, h1!=h2, h1*h2!=1
}.                                                       (9)
```

Because `K` is odd, the four excluded one-dimensional strata meet only at
`(1,1)`. Hence

```text
|H^2\Lambda_g|=4K-3,
|Lambda_g|=(K-1)(K-3).                                  (10)
```

On `Lambda_g`, `sd!=0` and `alpha,beta notin {+-1}`.

## 2. Exact branch-quartic discriminant

Clear denominators in (8) and view the resulting plane cubic as a quadratic
in `b`.  Its discriminant in `b` is a quartic in `a`.  The discriminant of
that quartic factors, up to a nonzero scalar independent of `t,s,d`, as

```text
Disc_a(q)
 =d^2 (d*t-2)^4
  (s-d-2)^2(s-d+2)^2(s+d-2)^2(s+d+2)^2
  R_(s,d)(t),                                            (11)
```

where

```text
R_(s,d)(t)
 =d^2*t^4
  +2d(s^2-d^2-4)t^3
  +4(5d^2-s^2+4)t^2
  -64d*t+64.                                             (12)
```

The residual quartic has the exact discriminant

```text
Disc_t R_(s,d)
 =2^16 d^2 s^2
  (s-d-2)^2(s-d+2)^2(s+d-2)^2(s+d+2)^2.                (13)
```

Equation (13) is symbolically regenerated by
`computations/verification/offoff_elliptic_spike_regression.py`; it corrects
an earlier transcription that had lost the factor `d^2` and the squares on the
four linear factors.

All factors in (13) are nonzero on `Lambda_g`, so `R_(s,d)` has four simple
roots.  Moreover

```text
R_(s,d)(2/d)=16/d^2 !=0.                                (14)
```

Thus the generic family has five distinct finite bad fibers:

```text
t0=2/d,
and the four roots of R_(s,d).                          (15)
```

For the primitive normalization of the binary quartic, the classical invariant
`I` satisfies

```text
I(2/d)=256 !=0.                                          (16)
```

(The raw quartic before removing its common content has `I=65536`; only
non-vanishing is load-bearing.)  Consequently the distinguished fiber has
multiplicative type `I_4`, while the four simple residual roots have type
`I_1`.  In particular all five finite points have nontrivial tame local
monodromy in the rank-two `R^1` sheaf.  The pole of `j` at `t0` proves that the
Jacobian over `F_p(t)` is non-isotrivial.

## 3. Exact reducible spike

At `t0=2/d`, the cleared cubic factors into two distinct affine lines.  Up to a
nonzero scalar they are

```text
L_-(a,b)=(beta-1)(a-1)-(alpha-1)(b-1),
L_+(a,b)=(beta+1)(a+1)-(alpha+1)(b+1).                  (17)
```

Their unique intersection is `(alpha,beta)`.

The valid off/off domain excludes

```text
a,b in {+-1},
a=b,
a=alpha,
b=beta,
T_alpha(a)=T_beta(b).                                    (18)
```

On `L_-` these exclusions collapse to four points: `(1,1)`, its unique
`a=-1` point, its unique `b=-1` point, and `(alpha,beta)`.  On `L_+` they are
`(-1,-1)`, its unique `a=1` point, its unique `b=1` point, and
`(alpha,beta)`.  The two lists meet only at `(alpha,beta)`.

Two distinct `F_p`-lines have `2p-1` affine points in their union.  Removing
those seven distinct invalid points gives the exact count

```text
O_lambda(t0)=2p-8                                        (19)
```

for every `lambda in Lambda_g`.

This is the load-bearing feature missing from the historical off/off target.

## 4. Trace decomposition after spike extraction

Let `E_lambda/F_p(t)` be the Jacobian of the generic genus-one pencil and let
`F_lambda` denote the rank-two middle-extension sheaf from `R^1`.  Write
`a_lambda(t)` for the unnormalized Frobenius trace on smooth fibers.
Then

```text
|a_lambda(t)|<=2 sqrt(p).                                (20)
```

At the four `I_1` fibers the projective curve is irreducible nodal and has
`p+O(1)` points.  At the `I_4` fiber, subtracting the explicit extra `p` from
(19) again leaves `p+O(1)`.  Affine/projective and valid-domain boundary
corrections have bounded degree.  Hence an absolute constant `C` exists such
that for all generic `lambda` and all `t in F_p`,

```text
O_lambda(t)
 =p+p*1_{t=t_lambda}-a_lambda(t)+e_lambda(t),            (21)

|e_lambda(t)|<=C,
t_lambda=2/(alpha-beta).                                 (22)
```

At singular fibers `a_lambda(t)` means the middle-extension trace; this is
uniformly bounded after the usual weight normalization.

## 5. Geometric irreducibility and conductor

For a non-isotrivial elliptic curve over a global function field,
Igusa's open-image theorem implies that the geometric image is open in the
expected `SL_2` factor.  In particular the rank-two geometric `R^1` local
system is irreducible.  A convenient modern source is Bandini--Longhi--Vigni,
*Torsion points on elliptic curves over function fields and a theorem of
Igusa*, Theorem 1.3 (equivalently Theorem 3.23), together with the determinant
sequence displayed there.

After the half-Tate normalization, the sheaf is pointwise pure of weight zero.
The five finite singularities are tame multiplicative; there is at most one
additional place at infinity.  Since the family has fixed degree and rank and
`p>3`, its conductor is bounded by an absolute constant independent of
`p,K,lambda`.

## 6. Finite singular support recovers the generic parameter class

The finite singular support is the root set of the squarefree polynomial

```text
S_(s,d)(t)=(d*t-2)R_(s,d)(t).                            (23)
```

Expanding,

```text
S_(s,d)(t)
 =d^3*t^5
  +(-2d^4+2d^2s^2-10d^2)t^4
  +(24d^3-8ds^2+32d)t^3
  +(-104d^2+8s^2-32)t^2
  +192d*t-128.                                           (24)
```

If two generic middle-extension sheaves are geometrically isomorphic, their
local-monodromy loci agree, hence their five finite singular points agree.
Therefore their support polynomials are proportional.  The constant
coefficient in (24) is always `-128`, so the proportionality constant is one.
The coefficient of `t` gives `d'=d`; then the coefficient of `t^2` gives
`s'^2=s^2`.

Thus either

```text
(s',d')=(s,d)
```

or

```text
(s',d')=(-s,d).                                         (25)
```

Returning to `(alpha,beta)`, these are exactly

```text
(alpha',beta')=(alpha,beta)
```

or

```text
(alpha',beta')=(-beta,-alpha).                          (26)
```

Since `c(h^(-1))=-c(h)`, the second case is the exact involution

```text
lambda*=(h2^(-1),h1^(-1)).                              (27)
```

Hence every generic geometric isomorphism class contains at most these two
parameter classes.  No separate isogeny classification is needed for the
trace-correlation step.

## 7. Source-exact quasi-orthogonality

Put

```text
K_lambda(t)=a_lambda(t)/sqrt(p).                         (28)
```

These are traces of geometrically irreducible, weight-zero, uniformly bounded
conductor middle-extension sheaves.

The exact input is the Grothendieck--Lefschetz/Deligne argument written as
Lemma 3.5 in Fouvry--Kowalski--Michel, *Counting sheaves using spherical codes*
(arXiv:1210.0851): for geometrically irreducible weight-zero sheaves of bounded
conductor that are not geometrically isomorphic, the normalized complete
correlation is `O(p^(-1/2))`.

Their printed lemma assumes a common primitive lisse open.  Our sheaves have
parameter-dependent bad points, so we use the proof rather than silently
forcing that hypothesis: restrict both sheaves to the common good open `U`.
Schur's lemma kills `H_c^2` when the two geometric representations are
non-isomorphic; Grothendieck--Lefschetz expresses the correlation through
`H_c^1`; Deligne RH bounds every eigenvalue there by `sqrt(p)`; and the
Grothendieck--Ogg--Shafarevich formula gives `dim H_c^1=O(1)` because rank,
conductor and the number of deleted points are uniformly bounded.  Restoring
the boundedly many omitted points changes the complete sum by `O(1)` in the
normalized scale.

Therefore, for generic `lambda,mu` outside the same/involution relation,

```text
sum_t a_lambda(t) conjugate(a_mu(t))=O(p^(3/2)),         (29)
```

with an absolute implied constant.  For the `O(K^2)` same/involution ordered
pairs, the Weil bound gives `O(p^2)`.

With

```text
A_g(t)=sum_{lambda in Lambda_g} a_lambda(t),             (30)
```

therefore

```text
sum_t |A_g(t)|^2
 =O(K^2 p^2+K^4 p^(3/2)).                                (31)
```

## 8. The spike multiplicity is Cayley difference energy

Define

```text
D(t)=#{lambda in Lambda_g:t_lambda=t}.                   (32)
```

Since `d -> 2/d` permutes `F_p^*` and `d=alpha-beta`,

```text
sum_{t!=0} D(t)^2
 <= sum_{x!=0} r_(A-A)(x)^2
 <= E_+(A).                                              (33)
```

Summing (21) over generic parameters gives

```text
O_g(t)=|Lambda_g|p+pD(t)-A_g(t)+E_g(t),
|E_g(t)|=O(K^2).                                         (34)
```

Using (31), (33), and variance bounded by the corresponding second moments,

```text
Var_{t!=0} O_g(t)
 =O(p E_+(A)+K^2p+K^4 sqrt(p)+K^4).                     (35)
```

## 9. Special parameter strata

The complement `Lambda_s=H^2\Lambda_g` has size `4K-3`.  No pairwise sheaf
independence is needed for it.

### 9.1 `h1=h2!=1`

Putting `d=0` in the cleared cubic factors out the forbidden diagonal `a-b`.
The residual valid equation is bilinear:

```text
8ab+A(t)a+B(t)b+8=0,                                    (36)
A(t)=s^2t-4s-4t,
B(t)=-s^2t-4s+4t.                                       (37)
```

Equivalently

```text
(8a+B(t))(8b+A(t))=A(t)B(t)-64.                         (38)
```

For all but at most two `t` it has `p-1` affine points before bounded domain
corrections; at the exceptional values it has `O(p)` points.  Hence each class
has variance `O(p)`.

### 9.2 `h1*h2=1`, `h1!=1`

Here `s=0,d!=0`.  Outside boundedly many `t`, the projective fiber is smooth
genus one, hence has `p+O(sqrt(p))` points.  Singular fibers have `O(p)`
points by the fixed degree bound.  Thus the variance is `O(p)` per class.

### 9.3 Exactly one of `h1,h2` equals `1`

For example `h1=1`, `h2!=1`.  Writing `beta=c(h2)`, the branch-quartic
discriminant of

```text
2/(a-b)+2/(-a-T_beta(b))=t                              (39)
```

is, up to a nonzero scalar,

```text
2^16 t^6 (beta-1)^2(beta+1)^2(t^2+8 beta t+16).         (40)
```

Since `beta!=+-1`, on `t!=0` there are at most two singular fibers.  The same
Hasse/fixed-degree argument gives variance `O(p)` per class.  The case
`h2=1` is symmetric.

### 9.4 `(h1,h2)=(1,1)`

Both transformed Cayley coordinates change sign, so the two differences cancel
identically:

```text
O_(1,1)(t)=0, t!=0.                                     (41)
```

Cauchy--Schwarz across the `O(K)` special classes yields

```text
Var_{t!=0} O_s(t)=O(K^2p).                              (42)
```

## 10. Corrected off/off variance theorem

For every prime `p>3` and odd-order subgroup `H<=F_p^*`,

```text

    +K^4 sqrt(p)
```

Proof: decompose `O=O_g+O_s`, use (35), (42), and
`Var(X+Y)<=2Var(X)+2Var(Y)`.
