# Round 8: original-family symmetry and powers-of-seven audit

Date: 21 October 2026. This note records the obstruction branches checked
before proving the new Weyl self-overlap theorem. It does not claim an
infinite sequence violating $H_{\rm diag}$.

The actual new quantitative result from this branch is in
`r8_original_uniform_weyl_self_overlap.md`. It proves, for the original
fixed $\tau=10^{-80}$, that the largest normalized one-step Weyl
self-overlap tends to

$$
 \Gamma=\frac{8\pi\sqrt{101}}{25}\tau+O(10^8\tau^2),
$$

with eventual maximizing labels exactly $\pm(4,-8)$. Thus approximate
pure-Weyl symmetries are excluded uniformly over all odd dimensions and all
nonzero labels. The spectral-statistical cancellation estimate remains open.

## 1. What previous symmetry results already cover

The full proofs were not replaced by symmetry-class intuition:

* `research_round2_2026_10_20/arithmetic_classical_symmetry_rigidity.md`
  proves that the relevant perturbed classical map has no $C^1$ reverser,
  and that its $C^1$ centralizer contains only its iterates. The proof
  uses exact periodic-orbit multiplier markers after classifying the
  possible lifted topological actions.
* `research_round3_2026_10_21/arithmetic_monomial_reversal_closure.md`
  excludes arbitrary-phase monomial quantum reversers, and nontrivial
  monomial projective commuting symmetries, for every odd $N\ge11$.
  This includes arbitrary permutations and real-algebraic split kicks,
  not merely a fixed diagonal gauge or a classical affine map.
* `research_round3_2026_10_21/arithmetic_seven_chain_cut_stability.md`
  proves that the seven weak Jacobi boundaries do not by themselves
  distinguish $7\mid N$: cutting the nearest six edges in every odd
  dimension costs $O(\tau/N)$ in kick trace norm and changes the full
  pair statistic by $O_b(\tau/\log N)$. When $N=7n$, the seven
  coordinate paths are not invariant sectors of $U_N$; each cross block
  satisfies $\|P_tU_NP_s\|_{\rm HS}^2=n^2/N>0$.

These results do not exclude an arbitrary nonmonomial quantum symmetry.
In particular, an eigenbasis-dependent antiunitary is not a usable
symmetry-class test: if $U=Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_j)Q^*$, then the
antiunitary $C=QKQ^*$, with $K$ coordinate conjugation, always satisfies
$CUC^{-1}=U^{-1}$. This elementary construction works for every unitary
matrix and by itself has no implication for COE versus CUE statistics.

## 2. A published perturbation-surviving symmetry mechanism does not apply

Primary source: M. Degli Esposti and B. Winn,
*The quantum perturbed cat map and symmetry*, J. Phys. A 38 (2005),
5895--5912, DOI 10.1088/0305-4470/38/26/005.
Author-hosted full text:
<https://www.dm.unibo.it/fismat/pub/a5_26_005.pdf>.

Their Theorem 1 constructs a monomial symmetry when $4\mid N$, for
Hamiltonians with the specified half-period reflection. Our dimensions
are odd. Their Theorem 2, for a single momentum shear and even cat entry
$A_{12}$, requires an integer $s>1$ with
$2s\mid\gcd(A_{12},A_{22}-1)$. For
$S_c=\begin{pmatrix}3&4\\2&3\end{pmatrix}$, the gcd is 2;
there is no such $s$. The original kick also has genuine mixed
$(q,p)$ dependence. Thus neither published construction yields a
symmetry of the family in the question. Their random-matrix interpretations
do not constitute a universality theorem that could be transferred here.

The checks above are negative applicability checks. They do not claim that
these two theorems classify every possible quantum symmetry.

## 3. Exact renormalization changes both the family and dimension parity

Primary source: I. Dana,
*Renormalization of Quantum Anosov Maps: Reduction to Fixed Boundary
Conditions*, arXiv:nlin/0005038 (2000):
<https://arxiv.org/pdf/nlin/0005038>.

The transformation in equations (5)--(8) has scale
$k=|2-\operatorname{tr}A|$, changes $N$ to $kN$, and rescales the
nonlinear propagator; its exact spectral equivalence combines all
admissible boundary conditions before fixing a new one.

For our cat,

$$
 K=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 K^2=S_c,\qquad \det K=-1,
$$

and direct multiplication gives

$$
 k=4,\qquad (S_c-I)^{-1}K=\tfrac12I.
$$

Hence the coordinate scaling in that transformation is $z=2z'$, and
$N'=4N$. At the Hamiltonian level the rescaled kick is generated by
$a'(z')=a(2z')/4$, as is also checked directly from
$\dot z'=\tfrac12J\nabla a(2z')=J\nabla[a(2z')/4]$. This is not the
fixed symbol in the question: every Fourier frequency of $a'$ is even,
whereas $a_{1,0}=1/20-i/200\ne0$. Also every rescaled dimension is even.
The exact renormalization therefore supplies no recurrence inside the
specified all-odd, fixed-symbol family.

## 4. The actual linear cat has a long exact recurrence at $N=7^r$

This elementary calculation was checked as a possible arithmetic route.
Let

$$
 S_f=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\4&3\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its order modulo $7$ is 3, and

$$
 S_f^3=\begin{pmatrix}99&70\\140&99\end{pmatrix}
      =I+7C,\qquad
 C=\begin{pmatrix}14&10\\20&14\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C\not\equiv0\pmod7.
$$

For every $r\ge1$,

$$
 \boxed{\operatorname{ord}_{7^r}(S_f)=3\cdot7^{r-1}.}          \tag{4.1}
$$

Here is a full lifting proof. The binomial formula for a single matrix
shows inductively that

$$
 (I+7C)^{7^j}=I+7^{j+1}C_j,\qquad C_j\equiv C\pmod7.
$$

Indeed the first nontrivial term in the seventh power is
$7^{j+2}C_j$; all subsequent terms contain at least one additional
factor of 7 after division by $7^{j+2}$. The congruence of the leading
coefficient is therefore preserved. If an exponent has the form
$7^j u$, $7\nmid u$, its leading coefficient is congruent to
$uC\not\equiv0\pmod7$, so its order modulo $7^r$ is exactly
$7^{r-1}$. An exponent killing $S_f$ must first be divisible by 3
because its reduction modulo 7 has order 3. This proves (4.1).

By the exact covariance of the original $M_N$, equation (4.1) implies

$$
 M_{7^r}^{L_r}=\zeta_r I,
 \qquad L_r=3\cdot7^{r-1}=\frac37N,
 \qquad |\zeta_r|=1.                                         \tag{4.2}
$$

For completeness, the scalar conclusion uses no representation theorem:
an operator commuting with every $W_v$ first commutes with all diagonal
characters, hence is diagonal, and then commutes with the cyclic shift,
hence its diagonal entries are all equal.

## 5. The first missing step in transferring that recurrence to $U_N$

The kick destroys the implication from (4.2) by a quantitatively large
potential error. The exact interaction-picture product is

$$
 U_N^{L_r}=
 \left[\prod_{j=0}^{L_r-1}
    e^{i\tau N M_N^jA_NM_N^{-j}}\right]M_N^{L_r},              \tag{5.1}
$$

with factors in the displayed order. The conjugated generators generally
do not commute. Removing them or replacing their ordered product by the
exponential of their sum is not justified.

The Weyl coefficient one-norm of the actual symbol is below 2, so
$\|A_N\|\le2$. Telescoping the product of unitaries gives the rigorous
but insufficient estimate

$$
 \|U_N^{L_r}-\zeta_rI\|
 \le\min\{2,\,2\tau NL_r\}
 =\min\{2,\tfrac67\tau N^2\}.                                \tag{5.2}
$$

This upper bound ceases to be small along the infinite tower. In particular,
the numerical size $10^{-80}$ creates a very long finite perturbative
window, but does not create an infinite subsequence on which (5.2) tends
to zero. No estimate controlling (5.1) at its actual fixed parameter, or
forcing a spectral cluster there, has been proved in this branch.

The new theorem in `r8_original_uniform_weyl_self_overlap.md` excludes an
approximate one-step pure-Weyl symmetry uniformly also on this tower. It
does not exclude a higher-time arithmetic recurrence such as a hypothetical
special behavior of (5.1).

## 6. Honest endpoint of this branch

No fixed admissible $b$, $\varepsilon>0$, and infinite sequence of the
actual $U_N$ with $|\Delta_{k,N}(b)|\ge\varepsilon$ has been found.
Conversely, none of these symmetry checks proves the CUE limit. The exact
first unresolved original-family estimate remains the fixed weighted
pair form-factor convergence displayed in the companion theorem file,
followed by the still stronger all-order $H_{\rm diag}$ requirements.
