## 1. Deletion of the zero orbit

The signed generating function after deleting the fixed orbit is
$\mathcal Z_N^-=\mathcal Z_N/(1-a_{0,N}z)$. The positive geometric
product instead divides by $1+\Lambda^{-1}z$. These two signs
must not be confused. The input handles them separately.

From
$$
 F_0(z)=e^{H_F(z)}/(1-z)
$$
and analyticity beyond the unit circle, division by the positive
factor gives coefficient limit
$$
 C_-=\frac{C_F}{1+\Lambda^{-1}}>0.
$$
There is no new singularity before $|z|=\Lambda>5$.
The amplitude error for a whole orbit set is bounded by
$e^{\pm Cmh^2}$, because the component lengths sum to $m$.
Positivity permits the same comparison after summation. Thus
the positive quantum coefficients tend uniformly to $C_-$
on $m\asymp N$, and remain uniformly bounded for $0\le m\le BN$.

## 2. The marked-orbit count

If a nonconstant primitive code of period $d\le k$ had $k$
successive zero edges, it would contain no nonzero edge in a
whole period. This is impossible. The only constant zero
orbit has been deleted. The periodic boundary point has at
most four itineraries, each of period at most four; for
$k>4$ none can contain the zero run, since the all-zero
code represents the different interior fixed point.

Every occurrence in a remaining primitive orbit of period
$d\ge k+1$ gives one midpoint-cut code
$0^Ru0^R$, with $k=2R$. Its sum is bounded by
$$
 [J^RT^{d-k}J^R1](o)\le C\Lambda^{-2R}.
$$
The left zero power evaluates exactly and the right normalized
zero power and all middle transfer powers are uniformly bounded.
The periodic/open comparison has a uniform $1+O(\theta^R)$
factor. Allowing imprimitive codes only increases the positive
sum. This proves the asserted marked-point estimate without
an extra factor $d$; the occurrences already count the
distinguished starting points.

The quantum modulus error is uniformly $1+o(1)$ for $d\le BN$.
The Euler union bound can then remove one distinguished marked
primitive orbit and bound the remaining coefficient by the full
positive coefficient of degree $m-d$. It gives a total lost
weight at most $Cm\Lambda^{-2R}$.
Choosing one sufficiently large fixed buffer offset $R_0$
leaves a fixed positive marker-free weight. This choice changes
no parameter of the quantum family.

## 3. The cutoff remains valid for the deleted series

The fixed-point amplitude satisfies
$|a_{0,N}|^2=\Lambda^{-1}e^{O(h^2)}$, so
$|a_{0,N}|\le .48$ eventually. On $|z|=2$,
$(1-a_{0,N}z)^{-1}$ is therefore bounded by $25$.
The scalar correction $e^{G_N}$ is analytic and bounded there,
since $2<.421^{-1}$.

The all-time trace bound splits at $\lfloor\log_aN\rfloor$
to give
$$
 \sum_{d\ge1}\frac{|\operatorname{Tr}U_N^d|}{d2^d}
       =O(N^\beta/\log N)=o(N),\qquad
 \beta=1-\log2/\log a\in(0,1).
$$
Both the growing initial geometric sum and the decaying tail
have been accounted for. Unitarity gives the exterior
determinant estimate $2^N e^{o(N)}$. Cauchy's formula for
$\mathcal Z_N^-$ consequently yields exponentially small
coefficients uniformly on $6N/5\le m\le3N/2$.
This is a cutoff for the series actually being summed, not
an application of the original cutoff to a different subset.

## 4. Classes and their signed cross terms

Every permitted nontrivial reconnection cuts with at least
$R$ zeros on both sides. Every reconnected cut still has
at least $2R$ zeros; the cells contain a nonzero edge, so
this marker cannot disappear into a deleted zero component.
Thus both endpoints of every permitted nontrivial move contain
a marker. Taking the equivalence relation generated by the
moves cannot connect a marker-free set to anything else.

More generally, for any partition retaining all marker-free
sets as singleton classes,
$$
 \sum_C|B_N(C)|^2\ge Q_{m,N}^{\rm free}.
$$
The exact finite square identity is
$$
 \sum_{C\ne C'}B_N(C)\overline{B_N(C')}
 =|[z^m]\mathcal Z_N^-|^2-\sum_C|B_N(C)|^2.
$$
Combining the cutoff with the positive singleton weight proves
the negative upper bound, uniformly in the degree band and in
the choice of such a partition. No estimate of a nonsingleton
class, and no phase-randomness assumption, is used.

The theorem asserts no upper bound on the magnitude of the
negative cross-class sum. Its conclusion is precisely that the
putative cross-class $o(1)$ step fails for this actual Euler
expression. It does not rule out an approach that evaluates
these phases, and it does not supply a counterexample to
$H_{\rm diag}$.
