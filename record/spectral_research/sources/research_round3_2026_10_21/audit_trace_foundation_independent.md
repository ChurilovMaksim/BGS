## 1. The actual quantum operator and the exact one-step amplitude

Let $c_{kl}$ be the Fourier coefficients defining $A_N=\sum c_{kl}T_N(l,k)$. Direct substitution at $h=(2\pi N)^{-1}$ gives

$$
\operatorname{Op}_h^W(e^{2\pi i(kq+lp)})\psi(Q)
=e^{2\pi ik(Q+\pi hl)}\psi(Q+2\pi hl).
$$

On a comb supported at $j/N$, this descends to $(-1)^{kl}T_N(l,k)$. Indeed, the modular inverse of $2$ is represented by $(N+1)/2$, and the additional $N/2$ gives precisely $(-1)^{kl}$. Consequently $a_{kl}=(-1)^{kl}c_{kl}$. For $l=\pm1$, the half-period change $r(q)\mapsto r(q+1/2)$ is exactly this coefficient correction. The principal symbol in the report is forced by the matrix definition.

The time and sign also check:

$$
t/h=\tau N,\qquad F_t=a(Q,F_Q),\qquad
\dot Q=-a_p,\quad\dot P=a_q.
$$

Thus the plane operator is $V=e^{itA_h/h}\mu_L$ and the generated map is $\Phi_{-\tau}S^{-1}$ in the report's convention.

To verify that the amplitude is exact, set $F=F_0+G$ and substitute $K=C_he^{iF/h}B_h$ into the **actual finite-shift equation** $K_t=(i/h)A_hK$. With $c=2\pi l$, this yields

$$
\partial_tB_h=i\sum a_{kl}
\left[E_h\frac{B_h(Q+ch)-B_h(Q)}h
+\frac{E_h-E_0}hB_h(Q)\right].
$$

No shift has been replaced by a formal Taylor series. The identity

$$
\theta_h=2\pi((k-3l/4)Q+(l/4)q)
+h\kappa+c\int_0^1G_Q(Q+uch,q)\,du
$$

with $\kappa=2\pi^2kl-\tfrac32\pi^2l^2$ is obtained by expanding only the quadratic $F_0$. For real $h$, every shifted argument remains in the same horizontal strip. Cauchy estimates therefore yield the analytic-scale bounds on $L_h$ and its first two real-$h$ derivatives without a constraint $|ch|<\delta$.

The $n$-fold Dyson term has bound

$$
\|\mathcal B_n\|\le
\frac{|t|^n}{n!}(Cn/\Delta)^n\le q^n,
\qquad q=eC|t|/\Delta<1.
$$

When $r\le2$ derivatives in $h$ are taken, the ordered product rule has $n^r$ terms, and the additional derivative loss contributes another $n^r\Delta^{-r}$. Thus $\|\partial_h^r\mathcal B_n\|\le\Delta^{-r}n^{2r}q^n$, which is summable. This is genuine real-$h$ regularity. It makes no claim about a fixed complex-$h$ disk.

For exact identification at fixed nonzero real $h$, put $D_h=e^{iG/h}B_h$. Its equation is

$$
\partial_tD_h=P_hD_h,\qquad D_h(0)=1,
$$

$$
P_hf=\frac ih\sum a_{kl}
e^{i[2\pi((k-3l/4)Q+(l/4)q)+h\kappa]}
f(Q+2\pi hl,q).
$$

This is a bounded operator on each fixed strip for the fixed $h$, hence $D_h=e^{tP_h}1$. Independently applying the actual Weyl operator to the quadratic kernel gives

$$
((i/h)A_h)^n(C_he^{iF_0/h})
=C_he^{iF_0/h}P_h^n1.
$$

The finite-translation word coefficients have finite absolute exponential mass, so this equality passes through the convergent operator exponential against Schwartz tests. Therefore the exact kernel really is $C_he^{iF/h}B_h$. This closes the formal-versus-actual issue, rather than presupposing it.

Finally, the limiting transport equation gives $B_0=2\sqrt{F_{Qq}}$. The positive branch is well defined because $F_{Qq}$ is uniformly close to $1/4$. Reindexing the real Fourier symbol gives $B_{-h}=\overline{B_h}$ on the real domain, so $\partial_hB_h|_{h=0}$ is imaginary. These are the exact amplitude properties needed later.

## 2. Finite torus to full flat sectors

For integer $n=(n_q,n_p)$, define

$$
R_n=(-1)^{n_qn_p}\mathcal T_n.
$$

The physical Weyl product has scalar $e^{i(n_pm_q-n_qm_p)/(2h)}$, which, at odd $N$, is $(-1)^{n_pm_q+n_qm_p}$. The displayed character cancels it, giving $R_nR_m=R_{n+m}$. Since $L\equiv I\pmod2$, $\mu_LR_n=R_{Ln}\mu_L$. Periodicity of $a$ gives $VR_n=R_{Ln}V$.

The joint fixed distributions are exactly the combs

$$
\mathcal Cu=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}u_{j\bmod N}\delta(q-j/N).
$$

The distributional identity $\sum_b e^{2\pi iNbq}=N^{-1}\sum_j\delta(q-j/N)$ gives the periodization map

$$
(\Theta\psi)_j=N^{-1/2}\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}\psi(j/N-a),
\qquad \langle\Theta\psi,\Theta\chi\rangle
=\langle\psi,P\chi\rangle.
$$

The metaplectic scalar can be checked without an abstract convention choice. Splitting an input comb coordinate as $\ell=a+8z$ gives the distributional factor $1/(2N)$. At $Q=j/(2N)$, the eight-term sum vanishes for odd $j$, and at $j=2x$ equals

$$
\sum_{a=0}^7e_8(-3Na^2+2(x-3y)a)
=4e_8(-3N+3N(x-3y)^2).
$$

Combining it with $C_h=e_8(1)\sqrt N/2$ gives

$$
\mu_L|_{\rm comb}=\zeta_N^{-1}M_N,
\qquad \zeta_N=e_8(3N-1).
$$

The kick descends to $e^{i\tau NA_N}$, so $V|_{\rm comb}=\zeta_N^{-1}U_N$.

For normalized coherent states $|z\rangle$, the finite frame is

$$
N\int_{[0,1]^2}|\Theta z\rangle\langle\Theta z|\,dz=I_N.
$$

Integration in the momentum coordinate kills every term except equal grid representatives and equal summation indices; the remaining position integrals tile the real line and integrate a normalized Gaussian. This checks the $N$ factor.

Expanding $V^m$ gives a summable series $\sum_\nu d_\nu\mathcal T_{w_\nu}\mu_L^m$, with $\sum|d_\nu|<\infty$ for every fixed $m,N$. For $G=L^m$, each word has

$$
|\langle z|\mathcal T_w\mu_L^m|z\rangle|
=c_G\exp\left[-\frac{(w+(G-I)z)^T(I+GG^T)^{-1}(w+(G-I)z)}{2h}\right].
$$

Because $G-I$ is invertible, the full-plane integral of this absolute value is finite and independent of $w$. The lattice sum on a fixed unit cell is bounded independently of its center by the supremum of a periodic Gaussian sum. These two bounds justify the rearrangements, not merely convergence of the operator series on $L^2$.

Let $g_n(z)=\langle z|R_{-n}V^m|z\rangle$. The honest integer representation gives the exact identity

$$
g_{n_0}(z+w)=g_{n_0-(L^m-I)w}(z).

Grouping $n$ by $\mathcal Q_m=\mathbb Z^2/(L^m-I)\mathbb Z^2$, the unit cells tile the plane **once**. Hence

$$
\zeta_N^{-m}\operatorname{Tr}U_N^m
=\sum_{[n]\in\mathcal Q_m}\mathfrak T_h(R_{-n}V^m),
\qquad
\mathfrak T_h(B)=\frac1{2\pi h}\int_{\mathbb R^2}\langle z|B|z\rangle dz.
$$

There is no additional determinant multiplier. Each sector is an ordinary absolutely convergent coherent integral, although $V^m$ itself is not trace class on the plane.

## 3. Abel path integrals and exact critical-point indexing

For each translated-metaplectic word, both its coherent integral and its Abel-regularized kernel diagonal reduce to the same elementary Gaussian. The relevant one-variable phase is $Dq^2/(2b)+\ell q+c$, where $D=\operatorname{tr}(L^m)-2>0$. Positive damping reduces the determinant modulus and gives a nonpositive real part after completing the square. The damped integral is therefore bounded independently of the real translation, by $D^{-1/2}$.

The $m$-variable word calculation is equally direct. With $q_m=q_0+n_q$, the quadratic Hessian is

$$
H_m=-\frac14(6I-\mathcal R-\mathcal R^{-1}).
$$

Its eigenvalues lie in $[-2,-1]$. This formula includes the coincident-edge multiplicities at $m=1$ and $m=2$. A real linear translation of a damped Gaussian cannot increase its modulus: if $D_\varepsilon=\varepsilon I-iH_m/(2h)$, then $\Re D_\varepsilon^{-1}\ge0$. Thus the entire translated word has a damping-independent, translation-independent determinant bound. Summability of the word coefficients permits taking the common Abel limit termwise.

It follows that the exact sector is

$$
(-1)^{n_qn_p}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\int_{\mathbb R^m}e^{-\varepsilon\sum q_j^2}
e^{-in_p(q_0+n_q/2)/h}
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}K_V(q_{j+1},q_j)\,d\mathbf q.
$$

This uses the full $\mathbb R^m$ domain, rather than a fundamental cube. Constants in this exact identification may depend on $m,N$; uniformity is not needed for an equality at each fixed pair.

The sector's real action has precisely one critical point. Its unperturbed Hessian is $H_m$. Each edge perturbation has all second derivatives bounded by $\eta$, so the perturbation of the cyclic Hessian has row sums at most $4\eta$, including $m=1,2$. Therefore

$$
D^2\mathcal S_n\le-(1-4\eta)I.
$$

The bounded perturbation of a negative quadratic polynomial tends to $-\infty$ at infinity, so it attains a unique maximum. Its critical equations are exactly $\Psi^m x=x+n$. Changing the initial lift by $w\in\mathbb Z^2$ changes $n$ by $(L^m-I)w$. Thus sectors and torus fixed points are in bijection, and their number is

$$
|\det(L^m-I)|=\lambda^m+\lambda^{-m}-2<\lambda^m,
\qquad \lambda=3+2\sqrt2.
$$

This supplies the fixed-point count directly from the raw phase. No separate structural-stability theorem is needed for this count.

## 4. Bounded contour and one uniform operator neighborhood

Center each variable at the sector critical point. The boundary momentum term cancels the telescoping linear edge jets exactly, so the remaining phase is $\sum_j\widetilde F_{x_j}(z_{j+1},z_j)$.

Choose an odd smooth $\psi$ with $\psi(u)=u$ near zero, $0\le\psi'\le1$, and constant nonzero tails strictly inside the spatial strip. Write $z=u-i\psi(u)$. For one edge, put $v=\psi(u)$, $v'=\psi(u')$, and $D=uv+u'v'\ge0$. Direct expansion of the quadratic phase gives

$$
\Re(iF_0(z',z))=-\frac{3D-(u'v+uv')}{4}.
$$

Monotonicity implies $u'v+uv'\le D$, hence this is at most $-D/2$. For the centered $G$, reality on the real plane and integration along the vertical segment give

$$
|\Im \widetilde G(U-iV)|
\le\eta(|U|_1+|V|_1)|V|_1\le4\eta D.
$$

The last inequality follows from $|v_i|\le|u_i|$ and

$$
(a+b)(\psi(a)+\psi(b))\le2(a\psi(a)+b\psi(b)),\qquad a,b\ge0,
$$

which is exactly the monotonicity inequality $(a-b)(\psi(a)-\psi(b))\ge0$. Therefore the deformed phase obeys

$$
\Re(i\widetilde F_x/s^2)\le-D/(4s^2),\qquad s=\sqrt h.
$$

This remains coercive on the saturated tails. Near zero it is Gaussian in $y=u/s$; beyond $|u|=a$ it supplies $e^{-a|y|/(4s)}$, which is sufficient for integrability and all required weighted derivatives.

For fixed $\varepsilon>0,h,m$, use the entire regulator $e^{-\varepsilon\sum z_j^2}$. On a vertical side at real distance $R$, bounded imaginary displacement allows at most $e^{C_{h,m}R}$ phase growth, while the regulator supplies $e^{-\varepsilon R^2}$. Thus the side integrals vanish. On the final contour, the regulator's modulus is at most one in centered coordinates. Coercivity supplies an integrable majorant independent of $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This proves equality with the full Abel integral. A shifted regulator has the same conclusion because $\psi$ is bounded.

After $u=sy$, assign the Jacobian to the input variable and normalize the kernel by $\lambda_0^{-1}$, where $\lambda_0=2\sqrt\pi/\sqrt\lambda$. The outside factor is exactly

$$
C_h\sqrt h(1-i)\lambda_0=\lambda^{-1/2}.
$$

The weighted kernel criterion using $A=1-\partial_y^2+y^2$ is valid:

$$
\|K\|_1\le\|A^{-1}\|_2^2\|A_{y'}A_yk\|_2
=\frac{\pi^2}{24}\|A_{y'}A_yk\|_2.
$$

In the straight part of the contour, Taylor expansion gives the uniform limit

$$
k_0(x;Y)=\lambda_0^{-1}B_0(x)e^{Y^TD^2F(x)Y}.
$$

For a fixed positive distance from the straight region, the above tail bound contains $e^{-c/s^2}$, which absorbs every fixed polynomial weight, every fixed inverse power of $s$, and the finitely many kernel derivatives required by the criterion. Therefore convergence holds uniformly in trace norm. Uniformity in $x$ follows from the global strip bounds; no orbit length enters.

The reference kernel is

$$
k_G(y',y)=\lambda_0^{-1}e^{-3(y'^2+y^2)/4+y'y/2}.
$$

Gaussian integration against the Hermite generating function gives eigenvalues $1,\rho,\rho^2,\ldots$, where $\rho=\lambda^{-1}=3-2\sqrt2$. The normalized ground state is the displayed $e_0$, so for $P=e_0e_0^*$,

$$
\|K_G-P\|=\rho,\qquad
\|K_G-P\|_1=\rho/(1-\rho)=(\sqrt2-1)/2.
$$

The source's coarse Gaussian-segment estimate gives $\sup_x\|K_0(x)-K_G\|_1\le10^{12}\eta<10^{-60}$. Its numerical margin is enormous: the required neighborhood is $10^{-4}$. The weighted convergence then yields **one** $h_0>0$ such that

$$
\sup_x\|K_h(x)-K_G\|_1\le10^{-4},\qquad 0<h<h_0.
$$

No power $m$ was used in constructing $h_0$. This is the load-bearing uniform threshold.

## 5. Exact graph split and summed residual

With $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$, $r=2.5\times10^{-4}$, and $\delta=\varepsilon(1+r)$, the graph map

$$
g_x(w)=\frac{(I-P)K_h(x)(e_0+w)}{e_0^*K_h(x)(e_0+w)}
$$

maps the radius-$r$ ball into itself and has Lipschitz constant below (0.172). The denominator is at least $1-\delta$. The Lipschitz bound follows by writing the quotient difference with one common denominator and using $\|g_x(w)\|\le r$; no missing second denominator is needed.

The backward graph transform over $\Psi^{-1}$ produces $v_h(x)$ with $e_0^*v_h(x)=1$ and

$$
K_h(x)v_h(x)=\gamma_h(x)v_h(\Psi x),\qquad
|\gamma_h(x)-1|\le\delta.
$$

The future dual transform, normalized by $u_h(x)e_0=1$, gives a dual functional. Dividing it by $u_h(x)v_h(x)$, whose distance from one is at most $r^2$, gives $\ell_h(x)v_h(x)=1$ and

$$
\ell_h(\Psi x)K_h(x)=\gamma_h(x)\ell_h(x).
$$

Consequently, if

$$
R_h(x)=\gamma_h(x)v_h(\Psi x)\ell_h(x),\qquad
\mathcal B_h(x)=K_h(x)-R_h(x),
$$

then $R_h(\Psi x)\mathcal B_h(x)=0$ and $\mathcal B_h(\Psi x)R_h(x)=0$. Every nonconstant word in the two factors contains an adjacent change of type and therefore vanishes. For a periodic point,

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\prod K_h
=\Gamma_h(x,m)+\operatorname{Tr}\prod\mathcal B_h,
\qquad \Gamma_h=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\gamma_h(\Psi^jx).
$$

The stated norm bounds follow directly from the graph ball:

$$
\|R_h-P\|_1<0.000601,
\quad\|\mathcal B_h\|<0.174,
\quad\|\mathcal B_h\|_1<0.209.

Using one trace norm and $m-1$ operator norms gives, also at $m=1$,

$$
\left|\operatorname{Tr}\prod\mathcal B_h\right|
\le0.209(0.174)^{m-1}.

Each sector has outside modulus $\lambda^{-m/2}$, and there are fewer than $\lambda^m$ sectors. Hence

$$
|\mathcal E_{m,N}|

since $0.209/0.174=209/174<2$ and

$$

=0.42007315985291853849\ldots<0.421.
$$

Choosing $N_0$ so that $(2\pi N_0)^{-1}<h_0$ proves the exact order of quantifiers claimed.

## 6. Repetition of the action and the odd-lattice sign

For a primitive orbit of length $d$ with winding $n$, set $G=L^d$. The repeated winding is

$$
n_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}G^jn,\qquad n_0=0.
$$

Apply the exact deck law to each edge in the $k$-th block and telescope. If $\mathcal S_r$ denotes the sector action of the $r$-fold repeat, the resulting identity is

$$
\mathcal S_r-r\mathcal S_1
=\frac12\sum_{k=1}^r
\bigl(n_q(n_k)_p-n_p(n_k)_q\bigr).
$$

This formula follows by subtracting the repeated boundary term $ (n_r)_p(q_0+(n_r)_q/2)$ after telescoping the edge actions; in particular it includes the half-winding terms.

Because $G\equiv I\pmod2$, $n_k\equiv kn\pmod2$. Every summand on the right is even, so $\mathcal S_r-r\mathcal S_1\in\mathbb Z$. At $h=(2\pi N)^{-1}$, the exponential phase therefore repeats exactly. Also

$$
(-1)^{(n_r)_q(n_r)_p}
=(-1)^{r^2n_qn_p}=((-1)^{n_qn_p})^r.
$$

The centered edge kernel is invariant under its integer deck law, so the normalized graph section and $\Gamma_h$ repeat as well. The metaplectic scalar $\zeta_N^d$ is already multiplicative. Consequently the exact weight can be written

$$
u_{p,N}=\zeta_N^d(-1)^{n_qn_p}
e^{i\mathcal S_1/h}\lambda^{-d/2}\Gamma_h(x,d),
$$

and each repeat contributes its corresponding power. Cyclic invariance of the action and the scalar product identifies the $d$ starting points on the primitive orbit, giving the factor $d$ in the asserted trace sum.

## 7. Uniform modulus to second order in $h$

The exact amplitude's uniform real-$h$ expansion and spatial analyticity give the trace-norm expansion

$$
K_s=K_0+sK_1+s^2K_2+s^3K_3+O_{\mathcal S_1}(s^4),
\qquad h=s^2.
$$

On the straight portion, Taylor's formula for the phase through degree five and for the amplitude through the necessary spatial orders has a polynomial-times-Gaussian remainder. The phase stays uniformly negative under the Taylor interpolation. The saturated part is flat to every required order by $e^{-c/s^2}$. Thus this expansion requires only the stated two real-$h$ amplitude derivatives, not an unjustified fourth derivative of $B_{s^2}$ away from $s=0$.

\operatorname{Tr}\prod K_0

$$

The Jacobi recurrence is

$$

$$

Its transfer product is conjugate to $D\Psi^m$: the change of variables is $(u_j,u_{j-1})\mapsto(u_j,F_{QQ}(x_{j-1})u_j+b_{j-1}u_{j-1})$.
