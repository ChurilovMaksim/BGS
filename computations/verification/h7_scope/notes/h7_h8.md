## 1. The sequence and the factor \(K_p\)

Let \(\varepsilon = 1+\sqrt{2}\) and \(\lambda = \varepsilon^2 = 3+2\sqrt{2}\). The NSW sequence is \(u_0 = 1\), \(u_1 = 7\), \(u_{n+1} = 6u_n - u_{n-1}\). Write \(h_p = u_{(p-1)/2}\) and \(K_p = 4h_p^2 + 3\).

**Lemma 1 [P].** \(u_n = \bigl(\varepsilon^{2n+1} - \varepsilon^{-(2n+1)}\bigr)/2\).

The characteristic polynomial of the recurrence is \(r^2 - 6r + 1\), with roots \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda^{-1}\). The general term is \(A\lambda^n + B\lambda^{-n}\). The initial conditions give \(A+B = 1\) and \(A\lambda + B\lambda^{-1} = 7\). Since \(\lambda - \lambda^{-1} = 4\sqrt{2}\) and \(7 - \lambda^{-1} = 4 + 2\sqrt{2}\),

\[
A = \frac{4+2\sqrt{2}}{4\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\varepsilon}{2}, \qquad B = 1 - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \frac{1-\sqrt{2}}{2}.
\]

Then \(B\lambda^{-n} = -\varepsilon^{-(2n+1)}/2\), which is the stated formula. The cases \(n = 0\) and \(n = 1\) recover \(u_0 = 1\) and \(u_1 = 7\).

**Lemma 2 [P].** \(u_{3n+1} = u_n(4u_n^2 + 3)\). In particular \(h_{3p} = h_p K_p\).

Let \(\gamma = \varepsilon^{2n+1}\), so \(2u_n = \gamma - \gamma^{-1}\). Then

\[
(\gamma - \gamma^{-1})^3 = \gamma^3 - \gamma^{-3} - 3(\gamma - \gamma^{-1}),
\]

hence \(\gamma^3 - \gamma^{-3} = 2u_n(4u_n^2 + 3)\). The left side is \(2u_{3n+1}\). For \(n = (p-1)/2\) one has \(3n+1 = (3p-1)/2\), so \(h_{3p} = u_{3n+1} = h_p K_p\).

The identity is prior: BGS-TRACE-265, proved there from \(V_{3n} = V_n^3 + 3V_n\) with \(V = 2h\). The argument above is a closed-form re-proof, the same closed form already used for the span law in this directory. The rank statement in TRACE-265 — every prime factor of \(K_p\) other than 7 has rank \(3p\) — is cited and not re-proved. TRACE-265 checks \(7 \mid K_p\) for the finite range \(p = 5,\ldots,23\). Lemma 4 extends that divisibility to every prime \(p \ge 5\).

**Lemma 3 [P].** \(\gcd(h_p, K_p) = 1\) for every integer \(p \ge 2\) at which \(h_p\) is defined, and in particular for every prime \(p\). A prime divisor of \(K_p\) is congruent to \(1\) modulo \(3\).

Any common prime divisor of \(h\) and \(4h^2+3\) divides \(3\). Modulo \(3\) the recurrence is \(u_{n+1} \equiv -u_{n-1}\), because the coefficient \(6\) vanishes. From \((u_0, u_1) \equiv (1, 1)\) the sequence is \(1,1,2,2,1,1,\ldots\), which is never \(0\). Thus \(3 \nmid h_p\), so \(3 \nmid K_p\) and the gcd is \(1\).

Now let \(q\) be a prime divisor of \(K_p\). Then \(4h_p^2 + 3 \equiv 0 \pmod q\), so \((2h_p)^2 \equiv -3 \pmod q\). The prime \(q = 2\) cannot occur, because \(K_p = 4h_p^2 + 3\) is odd. The prime \(q = 3\) is already excluded. For an odd prime \(q \ne 3\),

\[
\Bigl(\frac{-3}{q}\Bigr) = \Bigl(\frac{-1}{q}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{3}{q}\Bigr) = (-1)^{(q-1)/2}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{q}{3}\Bigr)(-1)^{(q-1)/2} = \Bigl(\frac{q}{3}\Bigr),
\]

by quadratic reciprocity for the distinct odd primes \(3\) and \(q\). The residue symbol \((q/3)\) equals \(1\) precisely when \(q \equiv 1 \pmod 3\). Hence \(q \equiv 1 \pmod 3\).

N8b records the stronger congruence \(r \mid K_p\), \(r \ne 7\), implies \(r \equiv 1 \pmod{6p}\), from the order of \(\varepsilon\) and the relation \(\varepsilon^6 - 1 = 14\varepsilon^3\). That order computation is not repeated here. Lemma 3 is the quadratic constraint only.

**Lemma 4 [P].** For every prime \(p \ge 5\), \(7 \mid K_p\) and \(7 \nmid h_p\). At \(p = 3\), \(h_3 = 7\) and \(K_3 = 199 \equiv 3 \pmod 7\).

Modulo \(7\), the coefficient \(6\) is \(-1\), so \(u_{n+1} \equiv -u_n - u_{n-1}\). From \((u_0, u_1) \equiv (1, 0)\) the terms from index \(1\) are \(0, -1, 1\), and then the same three residues again. So \(u_n \equiv 0 \pmod 7\) if and only if \(n \equiv 1 \pmod 3\). The index \((p-1)/2\) is congruent to \(1\) modulo \(3\) precisely when \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 6\). The only prime in that class is \(p = 3\). Every prime \(p \ge 5\) is \(1\) or \(5\) modulo \(6\), so \(h_p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod 7\), \(h_p^2 \equiv 1\), and \(4h_p^2 + 3 \equiv 0 \pmod 7\).

The script checks the same two congruences, together with \(3 \nmid h_p\) and \(3 \nmid K_p\), for every prime \(p\) with \(5 \le p \le 200\). At integration, `code/kp_arith.py` checks \(7 \mid K_p\), \(7 \nmid h_p\), \(3 \nmid h_p\), \(K_p\) odd, \(\gcd(h_p, K_p) = 1\) and \(u_{(3p-1)/2} = h_p K_p\) in exact integers for every prime \(5 \le p < 2000\) (301 primes; the referee's `ints.py` independently, same range). That is a finite check of the lemmas, not a substitute for them.

Level \(d = 7\) is therefore a \(K_p\)-level for every prime \(p \ge 5\). It lies under the inclusion cutoff of Section 2, because \(h_p K_p \ge 41 \cdot 6727 > 49\). The signed bound at this single level is prior and is not re-opened: TRACE-223 gives \(|\mu_{1:3}| \le 1.2837144 < T'\). Lemma 4 explains why that one covered level is present for every such \(p\). It does not cover \(7^2\), nor any other divisor of \(K_p\).

## 3. A close return is shorter than the trace

The product in the trace is \(\widehat B_m(y) = \prod_{j < m} g(\mathrm{Tr}\,\lambda^j y)\) with \(m = 3p\) (BGS-TRACE-263). That is a product of \(3p\) factors in the \(\lambda\)-step.

**Lemma E [P, external].** BGS-TRACE-268. For \(0 < \varepsilon \le 1/16\) and \(\beta \ne 0\) in \(\mathcal{O}/q\), if \(\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{Tr}(\beta\lambda^j)/q,\ \mathbb{Z}) \le \varepsilon\) for \(k \ge 3\) consecutive indices \(j\), then \(\lambda^{k-1} \le 264\varepsilon^2 q\) when \(\beta\) is isotropic, and \(\lambda^{k-1} \le 264\varepsilon^2 q^2\) for every \(\beta \ne 0\).

The second bound is the one that applies without a class hypothesis. At \(\varepsilon = 1/16\), which is the largest \(\varepsilon\) the lemma allows and therefore the longest run it permits,

\[
\lambda^{k-1} \le \frac{264}{256}\, q^2 = \frac{33}{32}\, q^2,
\]

so \(k \le 1 + 2\log_\lambda q + \log_\lambda(33/32)\).

**Proposition 6 [P].** Let \(p \ge 5\) be prime and let \(q \le \sqrt{h_p K_p}\). Any run of consecutive \(\lambda\)-steps on which a nonzero point modulo \(q\) stays within \(1/16\) of the integers has length \(k\) satisfying \(k < 3p - (p + 7/4)\). The product has \(3p\) factors. The excess of the product over this run is greater than \(p + 7/4\).

The closed form drops a positive term, so \(h_p < (\varepsilon/2)\,\lambda^{(p-1)/2}\). Then

\[
K_p = 4h_p^2 + 3 < \lambda^p + 3.
\]

The companion sequence \(v_n = \lambda^n + \lambda^{-n}\) has \(v_0 = 2\), \(v_1 = 6\), and the same recurrence, so \(v_5 = 6726\). Hence \(\lambda^5 > 6725\) and, for \(p \ge 5\),

\[
\lambda^p + 3 < \lambda^p\Bigl(1 + \frac{3}{6725}\Bigr) = \lambda^p\cdot\frac{6728}{6725}.
\]

Combining these,

\[
h_p K_p < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{6728}{6725}\cdot\lambda^{(3p-1)/2}.
\]

Also \(\sqrt{2} < 99/70\), because \((99/70)^2 = 9801/4900 > 2\), so \(\varepsilon/2 < 169/140\). For \(q \le \sqrt{h_p K_p}\),

\[
k < 1 + \frac{3p-1}{2} + \log_\lambda\Pi, \qquad \Pi < \frac{169}{140}\cdot\frac{33}{32}\cdot\frac{6728}{6725}.
\]

The integer comparison \(2\cdot 169\cdot 33\cdot 6728 < 3\cdot 140\cdot 32\cdot 6725\) says that the product on the right is strictly less than \(3/2\). And \(\lambda > 81/16\), because \(\lambda^2 = 17 + 12\sqrt{2}\) exceeds \((81/16)^2\) exactly when \(2\cdot 3072^2 > 2209^2\), which holds. Thus \(\lambda^{1/4} > 3/2 > \Pi\), so \(\log_\lambda\Pi < 1/4\). Therefore

\[
3p - k > \frac{3p-1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}, \qquad (3p - k) - p > \frac{p-1}{2} - \frac{1}{4} \ge \frac{7}{4}.
\]

Both integer comparisons are asserted in `code/h7h8.py` and are small enough to recompute by hand. The same program checks the sharper inequality “exact \(\log_\lambda(h_p K_p)\), plus the Lemma E constant at \(\varepsilon = 1/16\), is less than \(2p\)” for every prime \(p\) with \(5 \le p \le 79\), by an arb enclosure of the exact integers \(h_p\) and \(K_p\). The lower end of that excess is greater than \(6.875\) at \(p = 5\) and greater than \(117.875\) at \(p = 79\). Those are checks of the closed form. Proposition 6 is the proof for every prime \(p \ge 5\).

The isotropic clause of Lemma E has a single factor \(\log_\lambda q\) rather than two, so it gives a shorter run. The non-isotropic clause is the one that determines the excess.

Proposition 6 says that one close return, at the weakest \(\varepsilon\) Lemma E permits, cannot be the whole trace product on any included level \(d \le \sqrt{h_m}\). N17b §3 described the same gap as “separation time \(2\log_\lambda d \le p \ll 3p\)”. The inequality \(2\log_\lambda d \le p\) holds for \(d \le \lambda^{p/2}\), which is the scale of \(h_p\). Up to the inclusion cutoff the run reaches about \(3p/2\), and the excess over the product is still larger than \(p\).

Two clocks stay separate. N14b’s separation length \(n = p - \log_\lambda d\) is the diameter of the \(h_p\)-lattice, where \(h_p^2\) has size \(\lambda^p\). Proposition 6 counts factors in the product of length \(3p\). The excess is a comparison inside the second clock.

Proposition 6 does not determine the sign of \(\mathrm{Dev}_d\). A trace that is not one long close return can still be large, and it can still be negative. H7* asks for a rate below \(T'\) on each included level. Lemma E does not supply that rate.

## 4. Floors, under an explicit shape hypothesis

N17b Q1, already marked **[P]** there and used here as an input: the only a-priori bound is

\[
\varphi^{-1}\mathrm{Dev}_d \ge -\varphi^{-1} n_d |Z_\rho|^2/d^2 \ge -\frac{h_p}{d}\, S_\rho,
\]

because \(n_d \le d\) and \(S_\rho = \varphi^{-1}|Z_\rho|^2/h_p\). In units of \(S_\rho\) the floor of level \(d\) is \(h_p/d\).

From Lemma 1, \(h_p > (\varepsilon/2)\bigl(1 - \lambda^{-(p-1)-1}\bigr)\lambda^{(p-1)/2}\). For \(p \ge 5\) the factor in parentheses is greater than \(1 - \lambda^{-5} > 1 - 1/6725\), so

\[
\ln(h_p/7) = (p-1)\ln\varepsilon + O(1).
\]

The coefficient \(\ln\varepsilon\) is the limit of \(\ln(h_p/7)/p\). The log column in the script is a survey of that ratio; the closed form is the proof of the limit.

**Proposition 7 [P, conditional on a shape].** Assume the positive contribution that H7* is being compared with is at most \(e^{0.05 p}\) in the same \(S_\rho\) units. Then every included level \(d\) with \(h_p/d > e^{0.05 p}\) has a floor larger than that contribution. The comparison \(h_p/d > e^{0.05 p}\) was decided by arb at precision \(200\). Among the included \(K_p\)-divisors other than \(7\), the ones that pass are:

| \(p\) | divisors whose floor beats \(e^{0.05 p}\) | included others that do not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | \(31\) | \(217\) (and \(217 > h_5\), so the floor is \(< 1\)) |
| 7 | \(49\) | \(4663\) |
| 19 | \(457,\ 3199,\ 14593,\ 102151\) | the six larger included divisors in the log |
| 29 | \(13921,\ 97447,\ 5806729,\ 40647103\) | the six larger included divisors in the log |

Level \(7\) itself satisfies the same size comparison for every prime \(p \ge 5\) in the census, and level \(7\) is the covered level. The first open prime power that passes is \(49\) at \(p = 7\).

The shape \(e^{0.05 p}\) is a hypothesis of this proposition. The v5 gas is a lower bound of size \((2(1+a)^p - 1)^2\) with \(a\) of order \(10^{-3}\). A lower bound that sits under the floor does not show that the gas fails to pay the floor: the true positive term could be larger than every upper shape one writes down. Proposition 7 says what follows when the positive term is assumed to have at most that shape. It is the obstruction to discharging H7* by comparing the gas’s lower bound with these floors. It is not a proof that \(\mathrm{Lin}_d\) attains the floor.

## 5. The wrap budget and the delivered exponent \(c_B\)

Gap (a), N14 §3: a point of the level-\(h_p\) circle whose bad cluster has span \(\ell \ge \ell_0(p)\), with the design value \(\ell_0 = p/2\) in \(\varepsilon\)-steps, which is \(p/4\) in \(\lambda\)-steps. The window model does not apply, because the window wraps. Once a second-moment equidistribution statement is available, the per-site wrap activity is at most a constant times \((\lambda\bar\rho^2)^{3\ell/2}\).

N14 certifies \(\bar\rho \le 0.28\) and, separately, the looser value \(0.30\). The draft’s H8 threshold quotes \(\bar\rho_{\mathrm{true}} = 0.28028\). This note uses \(\bar\rho \le 0.28\) as the proved certificate. The value \(0.28028\) is recomputed only as a sensitivity check and is not treated as proved here.

Write \(\alpha(\rho) = -\ln\bigl((\lambda\rho^2)^{3/2}\bigr)\). An amplified weight of loss exponent \(1\), which is N16b (c2) under the hypothesis that the points carry weights \(\asymp d/h\), meets the factor \((\lambda\rho^2)^{3\ell/2}\) when \(c\ln d < \alpha(\rho)\,\ell\). At exponent \(c = 1\), \(\ell_0 = p/4\) and \(\rho = 0.28\),

\[
\theta = \frac{\log_\lambda d}{p} < \frac{\alpha(0.28)}{4\ln\lambda}.
\]

**Proposition 8 [P, arithmetic on cited caps].** Substitute the certificate caps \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{free}}^{8V} \le 1.9656353\) (BGS-TRACE-295) and \(r_2^{8V}(4000) \le 1.0529012\) (BGS-TRACE-290) into the exponent that N14b Lemma 2 writes down,

\[
c_{\mathrm{del}} = \frac{2\ln\Lambda_{\mathrm{cap}} - \ln r_{2,\mathrm{cap}}}{\ln\lambda}.
\]

This is the exponent produced by substituting those caps into \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{free}}^{3\ell}/(\Lambda r_2)^\ell = (\Lambda_{\mathrm{cap}}^2/r_{2,\mathrm{cap}})^\ell\). It is a number attached to that substitution. It is not a lower bound for the joint spectral radius.

At arb precision \(200\):

- \(c_{\mathrm{del}}\) lies in the ball of centre \(0.73753151868331175037\) and radius \(4\cdot 10^{-60}\).
- \(\tfrac32 c_{\mathrm{del}} > 1.10629727 > 1\).
- \(\alpha(0.28)\) lies in the ball of centre \(1.17477626638003325659\) and radius \(3\cdot 10^{-59}\). The resulting wrap threshold at \(\ell_0 = p/4\) satisfies \(\theta < 0.16661157\).
- The same threshold at \(\rho = 0.28028\) satisfies \(\theta < 0.16618631\), and at \(\rho = 0.30\) it satisfies \(\theta < 0.13725702\).
- The mixed-class budget \(-\ln(\varepsilon\cdot 0.2945)\) of N14b, with \(0.2945\) moved by one unit in the last printed place in the direction that shrinks the budget, is at most the ball of centre \(0.34093292399489768147\). Dividing that shrunk budget by \(c_{\mathrm{del}}\ln\lambda\) still gives a first-moment range \(\theta > 0.26223963\).
- These two intervals are disjoint: \(0.26223963 > 0.16661157\). Every \(\theta\) between them lies inside the range the substituted first-moment factor advertises and outside the amplified wrap budget at \(\ell_0 = p/4\).

The factor \(\tfrac32\) is the power in \(\sum |w| \le \bigl(\sum |r_A r_B|^2\bigr)^{3/2}\) (N14, \(\ell^3 \le \ell^2\)). Raising the delivered first-moment exponent by that power produces an exponent strictly larger than \(1\). N16b (c2) already charges exponent \(1\) for \(d\)-amplified weights, and it records that the weight sits outside the \(\ell^3 \le \ell^2\) power, so the loss is \(d^1\) and not \(d^{3/2}\). Proposition 8 says that forcing the delivered \(c_{\mathrm{del}}\) through the extra power is strictly worse than that exponent \(1\). A later argument that produces a smaller exponent is not touched by this comparison.

**Proposition 9 [P, arithmetic].** Covering every divisor of \(h_p\), in the limit \(\ln h_p \sim p\ln\varepsilon\), at loss exponent \(1\) and \(\rho = 0.28\), forces

\[
\frac{\ell_0}{p} \ge \frac{\ln\varepsilon}{\alpha(0.28)} > 0.75024803.
\]

Inserting \(c_{\mathrm{del}}\) in place of the exponent \(1\) multiplies this length by \(c_{\mathrm{del}}\). The product lies between \(0.55333157\) and the same number up to \(10^{-59}\). A window of that length is a positive fraction of the \(3p\)-step trace. It is not a short KP window. The trace is long enough to contain \(0.75p\) steps. Containing them is a design choice about gap (a), which is the choice N16b (c2) already named.

Proposition 9 does not use Lemma 2 as a proved theorem. Lemma 2 remains **[P-sketch]** in N14b: the residue-class Hölder step on the shortened window is not written line by line. The arithmetic above is what that sketch delivers when the certified caps are substituted, set next to the amplified wrap budget. The two quantities do not meet at \(\ell_0 = p/4\).

On prime \(h_p\), H8 reduces to H1 in the draft, as a sketch, and this note does not touch H1. On composite \(h_p\), N16b (c1) gives loss exponent \(0\) when the deep points carry the same per-point weight as in the prime case. The obstruction in this section is the amplified case, which is the case in which a subgroup exponent of size \(c_B\) would have been the relevant input.
