# Working bibliography

The entries below were rechecked against scholarly metadata on 2026-09-03. They are selected because they constrain what this project can honestly claim or provide candidate tools for a precisely stated open step.

1. P. Kurlberg and Z. Rudnick, **On the distribution of matrix elements for the quantum cat map**, *Annals of Mathematics* 161 (2005), DOI `10.4007/annals.2005.161.489`. Gives rigorous second/fourth-moment information for Hecke matrix elements and points toward a non-Gaussian SU(2)-trace law.
2. P. Kurlberg and Z. Rudnick, **Value distribution for eigenfunctions of desymmetrized quantum maps** (2001). Important for arithmetic desymmetrization and finite-field character-sum methods. DOI `10.1155/S1073792801000472` resolves to the expected 2001 IMRN volume/page and authors, but the resolver did not return a title; retain this title with a metadata caution until checked against the paper itself.
3. P. Kurlberg, A. Ostafe, Z. Rudnick and I. E. Shparlinski, **On Quantum Ergodicity for Higher Dimensional Cat Maps**, *Communications in Mathematical Physics* (2025), DOI `10.1007/s00220-025-05350-1`. Shows how substantially harder higher-dimensional cat-map equidistribution becomes and uses additive-combinatorial/number-theoretic input.
4. L. Rosenzweig, **On the fluctuations of matrix elements of the quantum cat map**, arXiv:0909.1410.
5. P. Kurlberg, L. Rosenzweig and Z. Rudnick, **Matrix elements for the quantum cat map: fluctuations in short windows**, arXiv:math/0701685.
6. D. Kelmer, **On matrix elements for the quantized cat map modulo prime powers**, DOI `10.1007/s00023-008-0394-4`.
7. A. Gamburd, J. Lafferty and D. Rockmore, **Eigenvalue spacings for quantized cat maps**, *Journal of Physics A* 36 (2003), DOI `10.1088/0305-4470/36/12/336`. Particularly relevant because it studies spacing numerically and exhibits arithmetic/non-generic behavior rather than furnishing a universal local-statistics theorem.
8. É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski and P. Michel, **A study in sums of products**, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 373 (2015), 20140309, DOI `10.1098/rsta.2014.0309`. Candidate framework for square-root cancellation in correlations/products of finite-field trace functions. It is not yet an applied theorem here: the active cubic family still needs a sheaf construction, bounded conductor, monodromy and geometric-isomorphism/duality classification.
9. É. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel, C. S. Raju, J. Rivat and K. Soundararajan, **On short sums of trace functions**, *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* 67 (2017), 423–449, DOI `10.5802/aif.3087`. Useful trace-function/Fourier background for incomplete or structured sums; not a substitute for the missing correlation hypotheses in M1.
10. N. Kaplan and I. Petrow, **Elliptic curves over a finite field and the trace formula**, *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society* 115 (2017), 1317–1372, DOI `10.1112/PLMS.12069`. Relevant background for moments of point counts in elliptic-curve families if the generic M1 cubic is organized as an elliptic family; no direct theorem from this paper is currently asserted for our family.
11. W. Sawin, A. Forey, J. Fresán and E. Kowalski, **Quantitative sheaf theory**, *Journal of the American Mathematical Society* 36 (2023 issue; published online 2022), 653–726, DOI `10.1090/jams/1008`. Particularly relevant now: complexity is controlled under the six operations, and pure irreducible perverse sheaves that are not geometrically isomorphic satisfy an effective quasi-orthogonality estimate. This is a candidate theorem for the generic off/off elliptic-family cross-correlations after the actual sheaf and its irreducibility/non-isomorphism are proved.
12. P. Michel, **Rang moyen de familles de courbes elliptiques et lois de Sato-Tate**, *Monatshefte für Mathematik* 120 (1995), 127–136. Classical one-parameter elliptic-family input for second moments/Sato–Tate behavior when the `j`-invariant is nonconstant. The current repository uses it as guidance for the self-correlation scale, not as a blanket proof of mixed-family independence.
13. A. Bandini, I. Longhi and S. Vigni, **Torsion points on elliptic curves over function fields and a theorem of Igusa**, *Expositiones Mathematicae* 27 (2009), 175–209, DOI `10.1016/j.exmath.2008.06.001`. Provides a modern account of Igusa's monodromy theorem for non-isotrivial elliptic curves over function fields; relevant to geometric irreducibility/big monodromy of the rank-two elliptic sheaf after the family is put in the required form.
14. S. Lvovski, **On monodromy in families of elliptic curves over C**, *Moscow Mathematical Journal* 19 (2019), 597–613, DOI `10.17323/1609-4514-2019-19-3-597-613`. Characteristic-zero monodromy support for non-isotrivial genus-one families; useful conceptually, while the finite-field proof should ultimately use an arithmetic/étale statement.
15. I. Shkredov, **On a girth-free variant of the Bourgain–Gamburd machine**, *Finite Fields and Their Applications* 90 (2023), 102225, DOI `10.1016/j.ffa.2023.102225`. Contains finite-field Möbius/PGL2 incidence bounds. These bounds help organize large-multiplicity level sets of the Cayley intersection problem, but their direct generic form is weaker than the exact cyclotomic near-Sidon statement proved for the current `k=2` component.

This bibliography distinguishes eigenfunction/matrix-element equidistribution from local eigenphase statistics, and also distinguishes a plausible theorem technology from a theorem whose hypotheses have actually been verified. Success on the former or merely identifying the latter does not by itself prove BGS spacing laws.
