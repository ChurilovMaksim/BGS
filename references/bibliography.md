# Working bibliography

Only references that constrain the active proof program or provide a theorem technology used in the current notes are kept here. A citation being listed does not mean its hypotheses have automatically been verified for this project.

1. P. Kurlberg and Z. Rudnick, **On the distribution of matrix elements for the quantum cat map**, *Annals of Mathematics* 161 (2005), DOI `10.4007/annals.2005.161.489`.
2. P. Kurlberg and Z. Rudnick, **Value distribution for eigenfunctions of desymmetrized quantum maps** (2001), DOI `10.1155/S1073792801000472` (metadata/title should still be checked against the paper itself before publication).
3. P. Kurlberg, A. Ostafe, Z. Rudnick and I. E. Shparlinski, **On Quantum Ergodicity for Higher Dimensional Cat Maps**, *Communications in Mathematical Physics* (2025), DOI `10.1007/s00220-025-05350-1`.
4. L. Rosenzweig, **On the fluctuations of matrix elements of the quantum cat map**, arXiv:0909.1410.
5. P. Kurlberg, L. Rosenzweig and Z. Rudnick, **Matrix elements for the quantum cat map: fluctuations in short windows**, arXiv:math/0701685.
6. D. Kelmer, **On matrix elements for the quantized cat map modulo prime powers**, DOI `10.1007/s00023-008-0394-4`.
7. A. Gamburd, J. Lafferty and D. Rockmore, **Eigenvalue spacings for quantized cat maps**, *Journal of Physics A* 36 (2003), DOI `10.1088/0305-4470/36/12/336`.
8. E. Fouvry, E. Kowalski and P. Michel, **A study in sums of products**, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 373 (2015), DOI `10.1098/rsta.2014.0309`.
9. E. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel, C. S. Raju, J. Rivat and K. Soundararajan, **On short sums of trace functions**, *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* 67 (2017), DOI `10.5802/aif.3087`.
10. N. Kaplan and I. Petrow, **Elliptic curves over a finite field and the trace formula**, *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society* 115 (2017), DOI `10.1112/PLMS.12069`.
11. W. Sawin, A. Forey, J. Fresan and E. Kowalski, **Quantitative sheaf theory**, *Journal of the American Mathematical Society* 36, 653-726, DOI `10.1090/jams/1008`.
12. P. Michel, **Rang moyen de familles de courbes elliptiques et lois de Sato-Tate**, *Monatshefte fuer Mathematik* 120 (1995), 127-136.
13. A. Bandini, I. Longhi and S. Vigni, **Torsion points on elliptic curves over function fields and a theorem of Igusa**, *Expositiones Mathematicae* 27 (2009), DOI `10.1016/j.exmath.2008.06.001`.
14. S. Lvovski, **On monodromy in families of elliptic curves over C**, *Moscow Mathematical Journal* 19 (2019), DOI `10.17323/1609-4514-2019-19-3-597-613`.
15. I. Shkredov, **On a girth-free variant of the Bourgain-Gamburd machine**, *Finite Fields and Their Applications* 90 (2023), DOI `10.1016/j.ffa.2023.102225`.
16. Yu. G. Zarhin, **Homomorphisms of abelian varieties over geometric fields of finite characteristic**, arXiv:1012.5083. This is the geometric-field Tate bridge used in the no-isogeny/local-system argument.
17. R. S. Maier, **Algebraic hypergeometric transformations of modular origin**, *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* 359 (2007), DOI `10.1090/S0002-9947-07-04128-1`. Useful modular-curve/cusp background; the actual no-isogeny note uses only standard `X_0(n)` degree and cusp-width formulas.

The bibliography deliberately distinguishes eigenfunction/matrix-element equidistribution from local eigenphase statistics and distinguishes an identified theorem technology from a theorem whose hypotheses have actually been checked.
