# What the classical mixing comparison does and does not say

TRACE-081 and TRACE-082 are proved from their displayed finite-dimensional
definitions. No external theorem is a load-bearing leaf. Their purpose is
to separate a fixed unitary's microscopic spectrum from its one-step
intensity transition and from a full phase-averaged ensemble.

The primary text of Gregor Tanner, *Unitary stochastic matrix ensembles
and spectral statistics*, arXiv:nlin/0104014v1 (6 April 2001), was read
at the abstract and Sections II.B--III.B. Equation (9), printed page 5,
defines the phase ensemble average. Equations (10)--(11) explain the
replacement by an ergodic phase trajectory and the rational-independence
condition. The conjecture in Section III.B concerns the averaged ensemble,
not every fixed phase tuple. Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/nlin/0104014v1

The primary text of Sven Gnutzmann and Alexander Altland, *Universal
spectral statistics in quantum graphs*, arXiv:nlin/0402029v1
(18 February 2004), was read at printed pages 1--2. Printed page 2
and equation (1) explicitly use rationally independent bond lengths
to replace a spectral average by independent phase averaging.
Primary source:
https://users.physik.fu-berlin.de/~vonoppen/papers/nlin.CD_0402029.pdf

The abstract matrices in TRACE-081 are not asserted to define that
graph quantization, and their small generic column phases are not
sampled over a full phase torus. Its obstruction therefore contradicts
neither referenced ensemble formulation. The source comparison supplies
no missing deterministic-map universality theorem.

Fourier chirps, tensor spectra, Gauss orthogonality, generic discriminants,
block resolvent elimination and observability dimension counting are
prior mechanisms. No world-priority claim is established for the
particular conjunction or norm statement here. No external dependency
registry entry, new applicability audit, or synthetic source hash is
created merely because these sources provide context.
