# Bibliography and theorem-technology map

A paper appears here only if it constrains the active argument or the interpretation of the results. Listing a source does not mean that its hypotheses have automatically been verified for this project.

## Quantum cat maps and arithmetic quantum ergodicity

1. P. Kurlberg and Z. Rudnick, **On the distribution of matrix elements for the quantum cat map**, *Annals of Mathematics* 161 (2005), DOI `10.4007/annals.2005.161.489`.
2. P. Kurlberg, L. Rosenzweig and Z. Rudnick, **Matrix elements for the quantum cat map: fluctuations in short windows**, arXiv:math/0701685.
3. L. Rosenzweig, **On the fluctuations of matrix elements of the quantum cat map**, arXiv:0909.1410.
4. D. Kelmer, **On matrix elements for the quantized cat map modulo prime powers**, *Annales Henri Poincare* 9 (2008), DOI `10.1007/s00023-008-0394-4`.
5. P. Kurlberg, A. Ostafe, Z. Rudnick and I. E. Shparlinski, **On Quantum Ergodicity for Higher Dimensional Cat Maps**, *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 406 (2025), DOI `10.1007/s00220-025-05350-1`.
6. S. Bhakta and I. E. Shparlinski, **On quantum ergodicity for higher-dimensional cat maps modulo prime powers**, *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* 58 (2026), DOI `10.1112/blms.70300`.

These works concern eigenfunctions/matrix elements and quantum ergodicity. They do not by themselves prove microscopic BGS eigenphase statistics.

## Spectral-spacing evidence

7. A. Gamburd, J. Lafferty and D. Rockmore, **Eigenvalue spacings for quantized cat maps**, *Journal of Physics A* 36 (2003), DOI `10.1088/0305-4470/36/12/336`.

This paper supplies numerical random-matrix spacing evidence for generic choices and arithmetic Poisson behavior in special cases; it is not a general local-law theorem.

## Trace functions and sheaf complexity

8. E. Fouvry, E. Kowalski and P. Michel, **A study in sums of products**, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 373 (2015), DOI `10.1098/rsta.2014.0309`.
9. E. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel, C. S. Raju, J. Rivat and K. Soundararajan, **On short sums of trace functions**, *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* 67 (2017), DOI `10.5802/aif.3087`.
10. W. Sawin, A. Forey, J. Fresan and E. Kowalski, **Quantitative sheaf theory**, *Journal of the American Mathematical Society* 36 (2023), 653--726, DOI `10.1090/jams/1008`.

The current M1 route needs a precise corollary with the project's geometric irreducibility, non-isomorphism, ramification and complexity hypotheses checked explicitly.

## Elliptic families and independence

11. P. Michel, **Rang moyen de familles de courbes elliptiques et lois de Sato--Tate**, *Monatshefte fuer Mathematik* 120 (1995), 127--136.
12. B. Grove and H. Saad, **Hypergeometric distributions and joint families of elliptic curves**, *Research in the Mathematical Sciences* 12, 74 (2025), DOI `10.1007/s40687-025-00554-2`.
13. Yu. G. Zarhin, **Homomorphisms of abelian varieties over geometric fields of finite characteristic**, arXiv:1012.5083.

Grove--Saad is especially relevant as a modern worked example: their joint-family theorem explicitly separates nonconstant `j`, reduction conditions, and non-isogeny/non-isomorphism hypotheses before applying cohomological square-root cancellation. It is a methodological template, not a plug-in proof for the present family.

## Citation discipline

For publication, every load-bearing use of a theorem must cite the exact theorem/corollary and verify its hypotheses in the notation of this project. Abstract-level relevance is not enough.
