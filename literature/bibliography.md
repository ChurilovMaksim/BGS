# Bibliography and theorem-technology map

Only sources that constrain the active argument or its interpretation are listed here. A citation is not a claim that its hypotheses have been verified for this project.

## Arithmetic quantum cat maps

1. P. Kurlberg and Z. Rudnick, **Value distribution for eigenfunctions of desymmetrized quantum maps**, arXiv:math-ph/0101008 (2001). For split primes this concerns value distributions of Hecke eigenfunctions; it is not an eigenphase-spacing theorem.
2. P. Kurlberg and Z. Rudnick, **On the distribution of matrix elements for the quantum cat map**, *Annals of Mathematics* **161** (2005), 489–507, DOI `10.4007/annals.2005.161.489`. The paper studies fluctuations of matrix elements and computes the second and fourth moments in agreement with an SU(2)-trace distribution conjecture.
3. P. Kurlberg, L. Rosenzweig and Z. Rudnick, **Matrix elements for the quantum cat map: fluctuations in short windows**, arXiv:math/0701685 (2007). This treats sums of matrix coefficients in shrinking eigenphase windows, not microscopic eigenvalue correlation statistics.
4. L. Rosenzweig, **On the fluctuations of matrix elements of the quantum cat map**, arXiv:0909.1410 (2009).
5. S. Gurevich and R. Hadani, **Proof of the Kurlberg--Rudnick rate conjecture**, *Annals of Mathematics* **174** (2011), 1–54, DOI `10.4007/annals.2011.174.1.1`. This is a Hecke quantum-unique-ergodicity rate result.
6. P. Kurlberg, A. Ostafe, Z. Rudnick and I. E. Shparlinski, **On Quantum Ergodicity for Higher Dimensional Cat Maps**, *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **406**, 174 (2025), DOI `10.1007/s00220-025-05350-1`.
7. S. Bhakta and I. E. Shparlinski, **On quantum ergodicity for higher-dimensional cat maps modulo prime powers**, *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* **58** (2026), DOI `10.1112/blms.70300`.

Items 1–7 concern eigenfunctions, matrix elements or quantum ergodicity. None supplies the microscopic GOE/GUE eigenphase law needed to close BGS for the present family.

## Spectral-spacing evidence

8. A. Gamburd, J. Lafferty and D. Rockmore, **Eigenvalue spacings for quantized cat maps**, *Journal of Physics A* **36** (2003), 3487–3499, DOI `10.1088/0305-4470/36/12/336`.

This paper contains numerical random-matrix spacing evidence for generic choices and arithmetic Poisson behaviour in special cases. It is useful context, not a general local-law theorem.

## Trace functions and quantitative sheaf theory

9. E. Fouvry, E. Kowalski and P. Michel, **A study in sums of products**, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* **373** (2015), DOI `10.1098/rsta.2014.0309`.
10. E. Fouvry, E. Kowalski, P. Michel, C. S. Raju, J. Rivat and K. Soundararajan, **On short sums of trace functions**, *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* **67** (2017), DOI `10.5802/aif.3087`.
11. W. Sawin, A. Forey, J. Fresán and E. Kowalski, **Quantitative sheaf theory**, *Journal of the American Mathematical Society* **36** (2023), 653–726, DOI `10.1090/jams/1008`.

These papers supply technology for controlling trace-function correlations and complexity. The present off/off draft still has to identify the actual sheaves and verify lissity, purity, geometric irreducibility/non-isomorphism, ramification and uniform complexity before invoking a quantitative theorem.

## Elliptic families, Sato--Tate and isogeny detection

12. P. Michel, **Rang moyen de familles de courbes elliptiques et lois de Sato--Tate**, *Monatshefte für Mathematik* **120** (1995), 127–136.
13. Yu. G. Zarhin, **Homomorphisms of abelian varieties over geometric fields of finite characteristic**, *Journal of the Institute of Mathematics of Jussieu* **12** (2013), 225–236, DOI `10.1017/S1474748012000655` (arXiv:1012.5083).
14. B. Grove and H. Saad, **Hypergeometric distributions and joint families of elliptic curves**, *Research in the Mathematical Sciences* **12**, 74 (2025), DOI `10.1007/s40687-025-00554-2`.

Grove--Saad is a useful modern worked example because independence is obtained only after the relevant geometric hypotheses for a pair of families are separated and verified. Zarhin-type Tate/isogeny results can turn suitable Tate-module isomorphisms into isogenies under their own hypotheses. Neither is a plug-in proof for the current pencil.

## Citation discipline

Any publication-grade use of an external theorem must cite the exact theorem/corollary and verify its assumptions in the notation of this project. Abstract-level similarity, nonconstant `j`, or a successful finite computation is not enough.
