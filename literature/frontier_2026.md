# Frontier check — 3 September 2026

The literature was rechecked during the reconstruction and again during the full editorial audit. The purpose is to keep three different questions separate: eigenfunction equidistribution, matrix-element fluctuations, and microscopic eigenphase statistics.

## What the established cat-map literature gives

Kurlberg--Rudnick's arithmetic cat-map work gives strong Hecke-eigenfunction and matrix-element results. Their 2005 Annals paper computes second and fourth moments of normalized matrix elements and compares them with a conjectural SU(2)-trace distribution. The short-window work of Kurlberg--Rosenzweig--Rudnick studies sums of matrix coefficients in shrinking eigenphase windows. These are highly relevant precedents for moment calculations, but they are not microscopic GOE spacing theorems.

The 2025 higher-dimensional quantum-ergodicity theorem of Kurlberg--Ostafe--Rudnick--Shparlinski and the 2026 prime-power result of Bhakta--Shparlinski advance eigenfunction equidistribution/localization questions. They do not provide the local eigenphase-correlation bridge needed here.

Gamburd--Lafferty--Rockmore remains relevant as numerical spectral-spacing context: their experiments show random-matrix-like spacing for generic choices and arithmetic Poisson behaviour in special cases. It reinforces the need to track arithmetic symmetry/desymmetrization carefully; it does not replace a local-law proof.

## What the elliptic/sheaf literature does and does not give

Michel and more recent joint-family work such as Grove--Saad show the right architecture for obtaining square-root cancellation from families of elliptic curves: one first identifies the family and its bad locus, then proves the required monodromy/independence or non-isogeny statements, and only then applies cohomological trace estimates.

Quantitative sheaf theory of Sawin--Forey--Fresán--Kowalski is relevant for uniform complexity bounds once the actual sheaves and their singularities are controlled. Zarhin's Tate/isogeny results are relevant to detecting isogeny from suitable Galois-module information under their hypotheses. None of these results turns “nonconstant j” by itself into the correlation theorem required by the present off/off pencil.

## Defensible frontier position

1. The corrected pair-key reduction, fixed-`K` Cayley classification and audited finite certificates are genuine results.
2. The off/off elliptic-family organization is substantial enough to justify a focused theorem proof, but the sheaf/isogeny hypotheses remain open obligations.
3. The target restricted M1 estimate remains a theorem draft until those obligations are closed.
4. Even a completed M1 estimate would still require a separate local-law/comparison argument to reach microscopic BGS/GOE eigenphase statistics.
5. No source found in the September 2026 check makes the historical claims “general BGS solved”, “M1 proved by Möbius fibers”, or “finite GOE fit proves a limit law” defensible.
