Exact finite-field reductions and several finite combinatorial identities below are proved and independently verified.  A restricted fourth-moment concentration theorem in the regime $K=o(N^{1/4})$ has a complete proof \emph{route} assembled from exact algebraic reductions and standard $\ell$-adic inputs, but is not promoted to a final theorem until the algebraic computer-assisted lemmas, projective models, and the cited sheaf-theoretic hypotheses have passed a line-by-line publication audit.}

\begin{abstract}
We study arithmetic moment structures arising from quantized hyperbolic cat maps at split prime levels.  The main technical contribution is a replacement of an earlier, invalid M\"obius-fiber argument by an exact weighted pair-key geometry.  For a multiplicative subgroup $H\leq\F_N^\times$ of order $K$ and its annihilator family of characters, the complete fourth-moment twist sum collapses to one finite-field difference distribution $B(t)$.  This gives exact formulas for the total $k=4$ constraint variety, the fourth-moment Fourier transform, and an exact mean/variance duality.  The lower-dimensional mixed term is transformed by a Cayley map into an additive-energy problem.  For odd prime $K$, outside an explicit finite cyclotomic exceptional set of primes $N$, its energy is exactly $(K-1)(2K-5)$.  The remaining off/off term is organized as a non-isotrivial elliptic pencil.  Its $j$-map has degree $12$, its generic parameter symmetries are classified, and the only $N$-scale reducible-fiber spikes occur at the already-controlled Cayley differences.  These facts reduce a first rigorous concentration theorem to standard quasi-orthogonality for bounded-complexity elliptic trace sheaves, yielding the natural preliminary range $K=o(N^{1/4})$.  We also record the computational certificates, failed historical routes, and the precise gap between fourth-moment concentration and a local BGS spacing theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and relation to BGS}
The BGS conjecture predicts random-matrix local spectral statistics for quantizations of classically chaotic systems after all relevant symmetries are removed.  Arithmetic cat maps are unusually delicate because Hecke symmetries and finite-field algebra can dominate spectral and eigenfunction statistics.  Consequently, neither quantum ergodicity nor a successful matrix-element moment calculation automatically implies GOE/GUE local spacing statistics.

The current project therefore separates three logical layers:
\begin{enumerate}
\item exact finite-field character and moment identities;
\item asymptotic concentration of the resulting finite-field moment objects;
\item a later local spectral-law step connecting those estimates to unfolded eigenphase statistics.
\end{enumerate}
This paper concerns primarily the first two layers.  The third remains open.

\section{Finite-field setup and character conventions}
Let $N$ be an odd prime and let
\[
H=\langle a\rangle\leq\F_N^\times,
\qquad |H|=K,
\qquad m=\frac{N-1}{K}.
\]
Let $S=H^\perp$ be the annihilator subgroup of multiplicative characters, so $|S|=m$.  If $g$ is a primitive root modulo $N$ and $\chi_j(g)=\exp(2\pi i j/(N-1))$, then
\[
S=\{\chi_{Kj}:j\bmod m\}.
\]
In particular the natural generator $\chi_K$ has order $m$, not $K$.  This distinction matters in every perfect-power or divisor argument involving the twist family.

For $\chi,\psi\in S$ and additive frequency $q\in\F_N$, define the twisted Jacobi-type sum
\[
J(\chi,\psi;q)
 =\sum_{x\in\F_N\setminus\{0,1\}}
 \overline{\chi(x)}\,\psi(x-1)\eN{qx},
\qquad
\eN{z}=\exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i z}{N}\right).
\]
The exact moment reductions below arise by expanding powers of $J$ and using orthogonality over $S$.

\section{The exact pair-key geometry}
\subsection{Pair coordinates}
For ordered pairs $x,y\in\F_N\setminus\{0,1\}$ define
\[
u=\frac{x}{y},\qquad
v=\frac{x-1}{y-1},\qquad
d=x-y.
\]
The diagonal $x=y$ collapses to one key $(u,v)=(1,1)$ with multiplicity $N-2$ and $d=0$.  Off the diagonal, the map is a bijection onto
\[
D_{\rm off}=\{(u,v)\in(\F_N^\times\setminus\{1\})^2:u\neq v\},
\]
with inverse
\[
y=\frac{1-v}{u-v},\qquad
x=\frac{u(1-v)}{u-v},
\]
and exact difference
\begin{equation}\label{eq:key-difference}
d(u,v)=\frac{(u-1)(v-1)}{v-u}.
\end{equation}
Thus the collapsed table has $(N-2)(N-3)+1$ records: one weighted diagonal record and $(N-2)(N-3)$ unit-weight off-diagonal records.

\subsection{The second-moment constraint}
The second-moment constraint requires the off-diagonal key itself to lie in $H\times H$.  Hence the number of constrained off-diagonal pairs is exactly
\[
(K-1)(K-2).
\]
This count is independent of all later $k=4$ asymptotics and is checked directly in the active verification suite.

\section{The fourth-moment difference distribution}
Let a collapsed key record be $r=(u_r,v_r,d_r,n_r)$, where $n_r$ is its multiplicity.  Define the ordered coupled difference distribution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-def}
B(t)=\sum_{r,s}n_rn_s\,
\1_H(u_ru_s)\,\1_H(v_rv_s)\,
\1_{d_r+d_s=t}.
\end{equation}
Write
\[
V_4=\sum_t B(t),\qquad Z_0=B(0),\qquad E_B=\sum_t B(t)^2.
\]
These are respectively the total four-variable constraint count, the zero-difference count, and the coupled difference energy.

\subsection{Exact total variety formula}
There are four ordered coupling classes.  The diagonal/diagonal class contributes $(N-2)^2$.  Each ordered diagonal/off-diagonal class contributes
\[
(N-2)(K-1)(K-2).
\]
For the off/off class, a partner of $(u,v)$ is $(h_1/u,h_2/v)$ with $(h_1,h_2)\in H^2$.  Failure to remain in $D_{\rm off}$ occurs only through
\[
h_1=u,\qquad h_2=v,\qquad h_1v=h_2u.
\]
Inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
V_{\rm off}
=K^2(N-2)(N-3)-3K(K-1)(N-3)+2(K-1)(K-2).
\]
Therefore
\begin{theorem}[Exact $k=4$ total]\label{thm:v4}
For every admissible prime $N$ and subgroup order $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4}
V_4=(K^2+1)N^2-(6K^2+3K)N+13K^2-3K.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
This is a finite combinatorial identity, not an asymptotic statement.

\section{Exact collapse of the twist assembly}
Define the correct weighted key amplitude
\[
A_{\chi,\psi}(d)
=\sum_{r:d_r=d}n_r\chi(u_r)\psi(v_r).
\]
Double character orthogonality yields, for every $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twist-collapse-time}
\sum_{\chi,\psi\in S}(A_{\chi,\psi}*A_{\chi,\psi})(t)=m^2B(t).
\end{equation}
With the unnormalized Fourier transform
\[
\widehat f(\xi)=\sum_df(d)\eN{-\xi d},
\]
this becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twist-collapse-fourier}
\sum_{\chi,\psi\in S}\widehat A_{\chi,\psi}(\xi)^2
=m^2\widehat B(\xi).
\end{equation}
Thus the previously mysterious ``cross-twist'' contribution is not an uncontrolled sum of unrelated energies; the full twist assembly is exactly one object, $B$.

Expanding $J^2\overline{J}^2$ gives the fourth-moment factorization
\begin{theorem}[Exact fourth-moment reduction]\label{thm:fourth}
For $q\in\F_N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourth}
\sum_{\chi,\psi\in S}|J(\chi,\psi;q)|^4
=m^2\Sigma_4(q),
\qquad
\Sigma_4(q)=\sum_t B(t)\eN{qt}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The same orthogonality argument extends formally to every fixed even moment, although estimating the corresponding higher-dimensional varieties is a separate problem.

\section{Exact mean/variance duality}
Let $V=\sum_tB(t)$ and $Z=B(0)$.  Averaging the finite Fourier transform over $q\neq0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-exact}
\E_{q\neq0}\Sigma_4(q)=\frac{NZ-V}{N-1}.
\end{equation}
Let $\overline B_{\ne0}=(V-Z)/(N-1)$.  Orthogonality of additive characters yields the exact population-variance identity
\begin{theorem}[Fourier variance duality]\label{thm:variance-duality}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-duality}
\Var_{q\neq0}\Sigma_4(q)=N\,\Var_{t\neq0}B(t).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
Hence the desired scale
\[
\operatorname{std}_{q\neq0}\Sigma_4(q)=O(NK)
\]
is equivalent to
\[
\operatorname{std}_{t\neq0}B(t)=O(K\sqrt N).
\]
The M1 problem is therefore a concrete finite-field $L^2$ estimate.

\section{Structural decomposition of the key distribution}
Let
\[
C_H(t)=\#\{(u,v)\in(H\setminus\{1\})^2:u\neq v,\ d(u,v)=t\},
\]
and let $O(t)$ be the pure off/off contribution.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-decomp}
B(t)=O(t)+2(N-2)C_H(t)+(N-2)^2\1_{t=0}.
\end{equation}
Because \eqref{eq:key-difference} never vanishes on the off-diagonal domain,
$C_H(0)=0$.  Thus the variance problem separates into a generic off/off family, a lower-dimensional mixed term, and a deterministic diagonal spike.

\section{Cayley transform and exact second-moment energy}
\subsection{Additive-energy form}
For $h\neq1$ define the Cayley transform
\[
c(h)=\frac{h+1}{h-1}.
\]
A direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-d}
d(u,v)=\frac{2}{c(u)-c(v)}.
\end{equation}
Let $A=c(H\setminus\{1\})$.  Since $\Delta\mapsto2/\Delta$ is a bijection on $\F_N^\times$, $C_H$ is exactly the nonzero additive-difference representation function of $A$, with its argument relabelled.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-energy}
\sum_tC_H(t)^2=E^+(A)-|A|^2.
\end{equation}
Moreover
\[
c(h^{-1})=-c(h),
\]
so $A=-A$.

\subsection{Forced central-symmetry baseline}
For odd $K$, $-1\notin H$, hence $0\notin A$.  The involution
\[
(a,b)\mapsto(-b,-a)
\]
pairs representations of every nonzero difference.  With $n=|A|=K-1$, the forced nonzero energy is
\[
2n(n-1)-n=(K-1)(2K-5).
\]
Any excess is a genuinely new additive collision.

\subsection{Cyclotomic classification for odd prime subgroup order}
Assume $K$ is an odd prime and let $\zeta$ be a primitive complex $K$th root of unity.  A collision
\[
c(\zeta^i)-c(\zeta^j)=c(\zeta^r)-c(\zeta^s)
\]
clears denominators to a sparse cyclotomic relation $P(\zeta)=0$.  Since
\[
\Phi_K(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{K-1}
\]
is the only rational linear relation among the $K$ powers, the collision reduces to a six-term multiset matching.  The current exact audit classifies all $6!=720$ matching systems as integer linear systems in $(i,j,r,s)$: 30 have rank 2, 240 rank 3, and 450 rank 4.  Maximal minors can lose rank only in characteristic 2.  The admissible positive-dimensional families are exactly
\[
(r,s)=(i,j),\qquad (r,s)=(-j,-i),
\]
with all remaining rank-2/rank-3 systems forcing a zero exponent or diagonal pair.

For a non-forced normalized collision define the algebraic norm
\[
R_{j,r,s}=\left|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_K,P_{1,j,r,s})\right|,
\qquad
D_K=\prod R_{j,r,s}
\]
(up to radical).  For primes $N\equiv1\pmod K$, non-forced finite-field collisions occur exactly when $N\mid D_K$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact nonexceptional $k=2$ energy]\label{prop:k2-energy}
Let $K$ be an odd prime and $N\equiv1\pmod K$ a prime not dividing $D_K$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:k2-exact}
\sum_t C_H(t)^2=(K-1)(2K-5),
\qquad
E^+(A)=3(K-1)(K-2).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
\Var_{t\neq0}C_H(t)
=\frac{(K-1)(2K-5)}{N-1}
-\frac{(K-1)^2(K-2)^2}{(N-1)^2}.
\]
\end{proposition}
The exact exceptional sets computed by the verifier begin with
\[
\mathcal E_{11}=\{23,67,89\},
\qquad
\mathcal E_{13}=\{53,79,131,157,313\}.
\]
Thus the mixed term $2(N-2)C_H(t)$ has standard deviation $O(K\sqrt N)$ outside the explicit exceptional set.

\section{The mean of the fourth-moment transform}
Two deterministic $N^2$-scale spikes explain the observed mean.  The diagonal self-coupling contributes $(N-2)^2$ to $B(0)$.  In the off/off layer, $(h_1,h_2)=(1,1)$ sends
\[
(u,v)\mapsto(u^{-1},v^{-1}),
\qquad
d(u^{-1},v^{-1})=-d(u,v),
\]
so every off-diagonal key contributes, giving $(N-2)(N-3)$.

For $h_1=h_2=h\neq1$, the zero-difference equation reduces to $uv=h$ and has valid count $N-4-\eta(h)$, where $\eta$ is the quadratic character.  The one-sided strata $h_1=1,h_2\neq1$ and its transpose contribute no valid zero-difference off/off points.  For $h_1,h_2\neq1$ and $h_1\neq h_2$, the projective completion is generically a smooth plane cubic.  A direct resultant calculation gives a singularity obstruction proportional to
\[
h_1^5h_2^6(h_1-1)^{14}(h_1-h_2)^4(h_2-1)^{18}.
\]
Thus Hasse--Weil gives a uniform $N+O(\sqrt N)$ count on the generic stratum after a bounded boundary correction.

Writing
\[
S_\eta=\sum_{h\in H\setminus\{1\}}\eta(h),
\]
one obtains the deterministic part
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B0det}
B_{0,\rm det}=2N^2+K^2N-2KN-8N-4K+14-S_\eta
\end{equation}
and the conservative bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B0err}
|B(0)-B_{0,\rm det}|
\le (K-1)(K-2)(2\sqrt N+28).
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:mean-exact}, \eqref{eq:v4}, and \eqref{eq:B0err} yields the draft asymptotic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-asymp}
\E_{q\neq0}\Sigma_4(q)
=2N^2+O(KN+N+K^2\sqrt N),
\end{equation}
so the mean is asymptotic to $2N^2$ whenever $K=o(N^{3/4})$.  The displayed resultant factorization has independent symbolic checks, but is still queued for a publication-quality hand/formal certificate.

\section{The off/off elliptic pencil}
\subsection{Cayley coordinates}
Set
\[
a=c(u),\quad b=c(v),\quad
\alpha=c(h_1),\quad\beta=c(h_2).
\]
Then
\[
T_\alpha(a)=c(h_1/u)=\frac{\alpha a-1}{a-\alpha},
\qquad d(u,v)=\frac{2}{a-b}.
\]
The fiber equation for fixed $(h_1,h_2)$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-fiber}
\frac{2}{a-b}+
\frac{2}{T_\alpha(a)-T_\beta(b)}=t.
\end{equation}
After clearing denominators this is a plane cubic and, quadratically in one variable, a double cover branched at a quartic.

Let
\[
p=\alpha+\beta,\qquad d=\alpha-\beta.
\]
For the binary-quartic invariants $I,J$, the discriminant factors, up to a nonzero scalar away from characteristics 2 and 3, as
\begin{align}\label{eq:quartic-discriminant}
\Delta(t)
&=d^2(dt-2)^4
(p-d-2)^2(p-d+2)^2(p+d-2)^2(p+d+2)^2R_{p,d}(t),\\
R_{p,d}(t)
&=d^2t^4+2d(p^2-d^2-4)t^3
+4(5d^2-p^2+4)t^2-64dt+64.
\end{align}
The discriminant of $R_{p,d}$ is proportional to
\[
d^2p^2(p-d-2)^2(p-d+2)^2(p+d-2)^2(p+d+2)^2.
\]
For genuine Cayley parameters the last four factors never vanish.  Thus the residual quartic is squarefree off the two $O(K)$ parameter strata
\[
h_1=h_2\quad(d=0),
\qquad
h_1h_2=1\quad(p=0).
\]

\subsection{Degree and parameter symmetry of the j-map}
On the generic locus, $I(2/d)=256$ and $\gcd_t(I,\Delta)=1$.  Since $\deg_tI=4$ and $\deg_t\Delta=8$, the $j$-map has degree exactly 12 and pole divisor
\[
(4,4,1,1,1,1).
\]
In particular the family is non-isotrivial.

The key involution
\[
(u,v)\mapsto(v^{-1},u^{-1})
\]
preserves $d(u,v)$ and sends
\[
(h_1,h_2)\mapsto(h_2^{-1},h_1^{-1}),
\]
so the corresponding class arrays are identical.  In Cayley coordinates this is $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(-\beta,-\alpha)$, which fixes $d$ and negates $p$.

Conversely, the unique finite order-4 pole of $j(t)$ recovers $d$, and the ratio of the $t^3$ and $t^4$ Laurent coefficients at infinity recovers $p^2$:
\[
\frac{L_3}{L_4}=\frac{4(p^2-d^2-2)}{d}.
\]
Hence equality of generic same-base $j$-functions gives only the identity parameter or the exact involution above.

\subsection{No nontrivial generic isogenies}
Identical $j$ is stronger than needed for local-system isomorphism: isogenous elliptic curves also have isomorphic rational Tate modules.  The degree-12 pole structure nevertheless rules out nontrivial generic cyclic isogenies.

If a primitive separable cyclic $n$-isogeny existed, the two $j$-maps would lift through $X_0(n)$.  Since
\[
\psi(n)=[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z):\Gamma_0(n)]
=n\prod_{q\mid n}(1+q^{-1})
\]
must divide 12, the only possibilities are
\[
n\in\{2,3,4,5,6,8,9,11\}.
\]
At $t=\infty$, both $j$-maps have pole order 4.  For a cusp of denominator $c\mid n$, the relevant width is
\[
w(c)=\frac{n}{\gcd(c^2,n)}.
\]
The two degeneracy maps are exchanged by the Fricke involution, so a lift of ramification index $e$ would require
\[
ew(c)=ew(n/c)=4.
\]
Exact enumeration of the eight candidate levels leaves equal Fricke-paired widths only at the middle cusps of $n=4$ and $n=9$, where $w=1$.  These would require $e=4$, but the lift degrees are respectively $2$ and $1$.  Thus no nontrivial separable cyclic isogeny exists.  A nontrivial inseparable Frobenius factor would multiply the degree of the source $j$-map by a power of $N$, contradicting degree 12 for every member of the family.

Zarhin's geometric-field Tate theorem then implies that isomorphism of the generic rank-two $\ell$-adic local systems would produce an isogeny.  Hence generic local systems are geometrically non-isomorphic outside the exact involution orbit.

\section{Bad fibers and trace-to-affine bookkeeping}
The order-4 bad fiber occurs at
\[
t_0=\frac{2}{\alpha-\beta}=d(h_1,h_2).
\]
At this value the cleared cubic factors into two lines.  Thus it can contribute an $N$-scale point-count spike.  Crucially its location is exactly the $k=2$ Cayley difference.  If $R(t)$ denotes the number of generic parameter pairs whose reducible fiber occurs at $t$, then
\[
R(t)\le C_H(t)
\]
(up to already removed special strata), and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spike-bound}
\frac1N\sum_t|NR(t)|^2
\le N\sum_t C_H(t)^2=O(K^2N)
\end{equation}
outside the cyclotomic exceptional set.

The other four finite bad fibers are simple discriminant zeros and give irreducible nodal/rational fibers.  The open key domain is obtained from the projective cubic by removing a boundary divisor of total degree 10.  By B\'ezout, an irreducible cubic meets this boundary in at most 30 geometric points.  Thus, for a generic parameter $\lambda=(h_1,h_2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-affine}
O_\lambda(t)
=N-a_\lambda(t)
+N\1_{t=d(h_1,h_2)}+e_\lambda(t),
\end{equation}
where $a_\lambda(t)$ is the elliptic Frobenius trace represented by the middle-extension sheaf, $e_\lambda(t)=O(1)$ on good fibers, and $e_\lambda(t)=O(\sqrt N)$ on the bounded set of remaining bad fibers.  Summing the bookkeeping errors over $O(K^2)$ parameter classes contributes $O(K^4)$ to the variance.

\section{Sheaf correlation input and a restricted M1 theorem draft}
For a generic parameter $\lambda$, let $\pi_\lambda:E_\lambda\to U_\lambda$ be the smooth elliptic family over the good $t$-locus.  The standard inputs line up as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item Smooth proper base change and Deligne purity give a rank-two pure local system, weight zero after normalization.
\item Nonconstant $j$ and Igusa open monodromy give geometric irreducibility.
\item The equations, compactification, and bad-fiber degrees are uniformly bounded; quantitative sheaf theory therefore gives absolute bounded complexity after excluding characteristics 2 and 3.
\item The preceding isogeny argument and Zarhin's theorem give geometric non-isomorphism outside the exact involution orbit.
\item Quantitative quasi-orthogonality then gives normalized complete cross-correlations $O(\sqrt N)$ for non-equivalent parameter pairs, hence unnormalized elliptic-trace correlations $O(N^{3/2})$.
\end{itemize}
There are $O(K^2)$ self/involution ordered pairs and $O(K^4)$ non-equivalent pairs.  After division by $N$ to convert a complete $L^2$ sum into population variance, the generic elliptic traces contribute
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offoff-var}
\Var_t O(t)
\ll K^2N+K^4\sqrt N+K^4,
\end{equation}
where \eqref{eq:spike-bound} absorbs the reducible-fiber spikes.  The diagonal and inverse-pair parameter strata contain only $O(K)$ classes and are no larger than the target scale under direct one-family $L^2$ bounds.

\statusbox{\textbf{Restricted M1 theorem draft.} Let $K$ be an odd prime, let $N\equiv1\pmod K$ be a split prime outside the explicit cyclotomic collision set, and assume the fixed-characteristic exclusions needed by the elliptic/sheaf model.  Once the algebraic model and cited sheaf-theoretic hypotheses above are certified line by line, \eqref{eq:offoff-var}, Proposition~\ref{prop:k2-energy}, and Cauchy--Schwarz for the covariance imply
\[
K=o(N^{1/4})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Var_{t\neq0}B(t)=O(K^2N).
\]
Then Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-duality} gives
\[
\operatorname{std}_{q\neq0}\Sigma_4(q)=O(NK),
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{std}\Sigma_4}{\E\Sigma_4}=O(K/N),
\]
using \eqref{eq:mean-asymp}.  This statement is not yet promoted as a final published theorem; the remaining work is verification/formalization, not a missing cancellation mechanism.}

\section{Historical falsifiers and negative results}
A reliable research workspace must preserve failed routes without allowing them to masquerade as current proofs.

\subsection{The historical M\"obius-fiber assembly}
Eliminating $(x,y)$ from the off-diagonal inverse formulas produces
\[
v_d(u)=\frac{(d-1)u+1}{d+1-u}.
\]
Without saturation this eliminated equation contains $(u,v)=(1,1)$ for every $d$, although the inverse pair map is undefined there.  The historical graph therefore contains a phantom point.  Exact comparison with the true key table gives:
\begin{itemize}
\item for $d\neq0$, $A_{\rm hist}=A_{\rm key}+1$ for every twist pair;
\item for $d=0$ with first twist trivial, both equal $N-2$;
\item for $d=0$ with first twist nontrivial, $A_{\rm hist}=-1$ while $A_{\rm key}=N-2$.
\end{itemize}
Thus the old proof cannot be repaired merely by adding cross terms or strengthening a Weil estimate.

\subsection{Ordered-coupling bookkeeping bug}
An archived large-$m$ producer included diagonal$\to$off coupling but omitted the reverse ordered off$\to$diagonal class.  At $N=53,K=13$ the missing class has exact size
\[
(N-2)(K-1)(K-2)=51\cdot132=6732,
\]
which explains the historical difference $423879-417147=6732$.  Current verifiers compare the entire $B(t)$ distribution and permanently guard against this recurrence.

\subsection{Other quarantined claims}
The workspace also quarantines a historical spacing test that compared consecutive-gap ratio statistics with an ordinary GOE spacing CDF, a word-trace program that terminated with an unresolved word, and a Hecke-centring numerical line whose selected subspace and random-centering construction did not represent the claimed Hecke eigenspace mechanism.  These failures are research information, not evidence for BGS.

\section{Reproducible computation and language architecture}
The current implementation deliberately avoids a one-script-per-run layout.
\begin{itemize}
\item Python is used for symbolic/exact research glue, finite-field analysis, deterministic probes, and result assembly.
\item C++20 is used for expensive finite-field loops, exact dynamic programming, and independent exhaustive verification.
\item A second language implementation is kept only when independence is itself part of a scientific certificate.
\item Historical Julia sources remain once in the immutable provenance archive.  They are mapped to consolidated supported components rather than mechanically translated one for one.
\end{itemize}
The migration audit found, among other things, that an old Perron-transfer script had an impossible spectral-radius bracket because a zero-cost symbol induces a weight-one self-loop, and that a helper named ``projective order'' actually tested return to the identity matrix.  These are examples of why language migration is treated as a scientific audit rather than syntax conversion.

\section{What remains before a local BGS theorem}
Even a fully certified restricted M1 theorem is not a local spacing theorem.  The next stages are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item finish publication-level certification of the $K=o(N^{1/4})$ fourth-moment concentration statement;
\item identify the precise spectral observable/local window for which the moment information is sufficient;
\item control higher moments or a Stieltjes/resolvent object strongly enough to obtain a local law;
\item remove residual arithmetic symmetries and state the unfolding procedure exactly;
\item prove the relevant random-matrix comparison or universality step;
\item use numerical GOE controls only as falsifiers and calibration, never as substitutes for the proof.
\end{enumerate}
The general BGS conjecture remains outside the current theorem set.

\section{Conclusion}
The central methodological change is the replacement of a fragile twist-by-twist M\"obius argument by an exact pair-key distribution.  This turns the fourth moment into a single finite Fourier transform with an exact variance duality.  The mixed contribution is solved in a substantial arithmetic regime by Cayley energy and cyclotomic collision classification, while the remaining off/off contribution is an explicit bounded-complexity elliptic family.  The first plausible asymptotic concentration range is therefore no longer speculative: it reduces to a short list of certifiable algebraic and sheaf-theoretic steps.  The project nevertheless keeps a strict distinction between exact finite identities, theorem drafts, numerical evidence, and the still-open local BGS conclusion.
