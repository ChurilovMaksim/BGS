\section{Quantitative exclusion of arithmetic translation algebras}
\label{sec:arithmetic-translation-exclusion}
Keep the unchanged odd-dimensional operator
$U_N=\exp(i\tau N A_N)M_N$, with $\tau=10^{-80}$, and the physical
midpoint symbol, including its shifted momentum coefficient. Put
$\kappa=\tau/(2\pi)$ and equip matrices with
$\langle X,Y\rangle_N=N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(X^*Y)$.
For an odd divisor $d\ge3$ of $N$, let
\[
 E^0_{d,N}=\operatorname{span}\{T_N((N/d)v):
 v\in(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z)^2\setminus\{0\}\},
 \qquad \Pi^0_{d,N}:\mathcal H_N\longrightarrow E^0_{d,N}.
\]
The $d^2-1$ displayed raw Weyl modes are exactly orthonormal. No
coprimality of $d$ and $N/d$ or CRT factorization is assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic translation-algebra exclusion]
There is $C_\tau$, independent of $N,d$ and the grid labels, such that
\[
 \bigl\|\Pi^0_{d,N}\operatorname{Ad}(U_N)\Pi^0_{d,N}\bigr\|
 \le\min\left\{1,C_\tau\left(\frac{d^{262}}N\right)^{1/129}\right\}.
\]
For every fixed $\delta<1/262$, uniformly when $d\mid N$ and
$d\le N^\delta$, the compression is
\[ O_\tau\bigl(N^{-(1-262\delta)/129}\bigr). \]
In particular, for $d\le N^{1/524}$,
\[
 \sup_{\substack{X\in E^0_{d,N}\\\|X\|_N=1}}
 \left|\|U_N XU_N^*-X\|_N^2-2\right|
 \le 2C_\tau N^{-1/258}.
\]
\end{theorem}
The fixed-$d$ statement follows immediately. Since the cat propagator
permutes these labels, the conclusion applies to normalized arithmetic
cat orbit sums. If a projection $P_N$ lies in
$\mathbb CI\oplus E^0_{d,N}$ and
$0<\alpha_N=\operatorname{rank}(P_N)/N<1$, its nonzero centered part
$X_N=(P_N-\alpha_N I)/\sqrt{\alpha_N(1-\alpha_N)}$ satisfies the same
unit-vector estimate. The projections $0,I$ are excluded from this
normalization. Arbitrary spectral projections are not covered.

\paragraph{Uniform finite type at fixed coupling.}
Write the kick flow as $\Psi=\Phi_g^{-\kappa}=\mathrm{Id}+u$.
Complex-strip bounds give $u=-\kappa X_g+R$. Crucially, for a translation
$\xi$ and $r=\|\xi\|_\infty$ in its centered representative,
\[
 \|D^\alpha(R(\cdot+\xi)-R)\|_\infty
 \le C\kappa^2 r \qquad (|\alpha|\le16),
\]
using one extra derivative. Degree-16 differential filters isolate the
primitive $(\!-1,0)$ and $(0,\!-1)$ Fourier modes of the actual symbol.
Their coefficient norms are $6409325/1143072<6$, and their surviving
multipliers have moduli $4/3$ and $2$. Explicit Cauchy estimates bound the
filtered remainder by $10^{55}\kappa^2r$, smaller than the leading
$|\kappa|r$ signal at the literal coupling. A selected real component of
$\xi-\eta+u(x+\xi)-u(x)$ has finite type at most 16, uniformly in the
translations. Directional slicing and the one-dimensional sublevel lemma
therefore give coincidence-sublevel area
$O_\tau((d\varepsilon)^{1/16})$ for a nonzero $d$-grid translation.

\paragraph{Quantum overlap and dimension loss.}
Exact Weyl multiplication gives the actual kick Egorov error
$O_\tau(N^{-2}\|a\|_{H^5})$. It remains uniform after inserting a
translation, since translations preserve Sobolev norms. In the trace
intertwining argument, division by squared map separation costs
$\varepsilon^{-4}$ in $C^3$. Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz on the bad
set retains the \emph{square root} of its area, while the aliased Fourier
tail costs $N^{-2}\varepsilon^{-3}$. Thus every matrix overlap is bounded
by
\[
 C_\tau\left((d\varepsilon)^{1/32}
       +N^{-1}\varepsilon^{-4}+N^{-2}\varepsilon^{-3}\right).
\]
Choosing $\varepsilon=N^{-32/129}d^{-1/129}$ gives
$C_\tau(d^4/N)^{1/129}$ per entry. Passing to the entire
$(d^2-1)$-dimensional span costs a further factor $d^2$, yielding the
stated $262/129$ exponent. The residual estimate follows from unitarity
and $\|\operatorname{Ad}(U_N)X-X\|_N^2
=2-2\operatorname{Re}\langle X,\operatorname{Ad}(U_N)X\rangle_N$.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This is a project proof with independent internal review and exact finite
controls, not an external referee certificate or analytic formalization.
The exponents are not claimed optimal; the endpoint $\delta=1/262$ is not
a decay statement. Constants depend on the fixed coupling, with no useful
numerical startup or uniform shrinking-coupling estimate supplied.
Faster-growing divisors, general quantum commutants, coherent returns and
the signed Heisenberg joint-trace comparison remain open. The theorem
does not establish microscopic BGS universality.
