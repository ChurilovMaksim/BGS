\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolic diagonal form factor, BGS-TRACE-131]
\label{thm:hyperbolic-diagonal-form-factor}
Let \(S=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb Z)\)
with \(|\alpha+\delta|\ge 3\), and let \(N\ge 3\) be odd with \(\gcd(\beta,N)=1\).
The position-basis matrix \(M_N(S)\) with phase
\(\kappa(\delta x^2-2xy+\alpha y^2)\), \(\kappa\equiv-(2\beta)^{-1}\),
and prefactor \(N^{-1/2}\) is unitary and flat. It is symmetric if and only if
\(\alpha\equiv\delta\pmod N\). For every \(m\ge 1\),
\(\lvert\operatorname{Tr} M_N(S)^m\rvert^2=\lvert\ker(S^m-I)\rvert\).
The sequences \(u_n=\operatorname{tr}(S^n)\) and \(v_n\) depend only on the trace.
For an odd prime \(p\nmid\beta\), the kernel has order \(1\), \(p\), or \(p^2\)
according as \(p\nmid(2-u_m)\), the matrix is a nontrivial unipotent, or
\(S^m\equiv I\). The last test is \(v_m\equiv 0\) and \(v_{m-1}\equiv-1\), and
it depends only on the trace. If \(p\nmid 2\Delta\), the order divides
\(p-(\Delta/p)\). If \(p\mid\beta\), equal traces need not agree: the two
matrices of trace \(-16\) in the note have kernels \(9\) and \(3\) modulo \(3\).

For \(f=\sum_k a_k\cos(2\pi k q/N+\varphi_k)\) with independent uniform phases,
the quantum average of \(\lvert\operatorname{Tr} U^m\rvert^2\) is the Bessel
path sum of the note. If \(\gcd(2-t,N)=1\), the time-one value is \(1\) for
every real coupling. In general
\[
\mathbb E_\varphi\lvert\operatorname{Tr} U^m\rvert^2
=\lvert\ker(S^m-I)\rvert+\eta^2 D_2(S,a,m,N)+\rho,
\]
with \(\lvert D_2\rvert\le 2m^2\sigma\lvert\ker\rvert\),
\(\sigma=\sum a_k^2/2\), and the remainder of the note.
At an identity revival, \(D_2=-m(\sigma-\sigma_0(p))p^2\). This is not the
all-order revival law. The fixed-time limit is not claimed.
Let \(E=\mathrm{diag}\,e_N(\kappa(\delta-\alpha)x^2)\). Then \(M_N=EM_N^{\mathsf T}E^{-1}\),
and \(\Theta'=DEK_0\) satisfies \((\Theta')^2=I\) and reverses \(DM_N\) for every such
\(S\) and every diagonal unitary \(D\). It reduces to \(DK_0\) when
\(\alpha\equiv\delta\pmod N\). The orthogonal-class name is a heuristic for
\(\Theta'\), in the sense of Keating--Mezzadri, Nonlinearity 13 (2000), 747.
\end{theorem}
