\section{Cat-map order and the Abel statistic}
\label{sec:order-ladder-abel}
The one-term disk and the positive-time expansion stop at individual Pell
dimensions. The Abel statistic of the pure cat map is instead decided by
the multiplicative order of \(C\) modulo \(N\). The canonical proof is
\path{mathematics/fixed-map/order_ladder_abel.md}.
The integer comparisons are certified by
\path{formalization/Bgs/OrderLadderRational.lean} and recomputed by the

\begin{theorem}[Order ladder, BGS-TRACE-120]
\label{thm:order-ladder-abel}
Let \(N\ge3\) be odd and let \(t(N)\) be the least positive integer with
\(C^{t(N)}\equiv I\pmod N\), for the frequency matrix \(C\) of
Section~\ref{sec:kick-blind-arithmetic-spike}. If \(12\,t(N)\le5N\), then
\(L_N(M_N;1)>3/4\), and the distance from this value to
\((1-e^{-2})/4\) is greater than \(1/2\). If \(p\) is an odd prime, the
order is \(t(3)=4\). For \(p=3\), and for every prime \(p\ge5\) with
\(2\,t(p)\ge p+1\), one has \(L_p(M_p;1)<5/7\), and the distance from
this value to \((1-e^{-2})/4\) is less than \(1/2\).

For the fixed kick \(\tau=10^{-80}\), the same strict gap above \(3/4\)
holds whenever \(8\,t(N)\le3N\) and \(N\le10^{39}\). Every odd prime
\(p\le10^{26}\) on the long-order side satisfies \(L_p(U_p;1)<5/7\), so
the kicked statistic lies below \(3/4\) and within distance \(1/2\) of the
CUE value. The odd dimension \(N=35\) has order \(6\), is not a Pell
number, and its first return alone lies below \(3/4\). The prime \(p=7\)
meets neither order hypothesis, and the estimate does not decide
\(L_7(M_7;1)\). It does not decide the kicked map at \(u_{207}\), the
limit, or a generic odd dimension.
\end{theorem}
