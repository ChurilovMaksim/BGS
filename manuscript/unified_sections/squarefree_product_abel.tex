\section{Square-free products of at least three primes}
\label{sec:squarefree-product-abel}
Every square-free odd modulus with at least three distinct prime factors
has pure Abel value strictly above \(3/4\). The canonical proof is
\path{mathematics/fixed-map/squarefree_product_abel.md}.
The integer comparisons are certified by
\path{formalization/Bgs/SquarefreeProductRational.lean} and recomputed by the
Python and C++ verifiers named there. No external refereeing is asserted.
BGS-TRACE-005 and BGS-BGS-001 remain open.

\begin{theorem}[Square-free products above \(3/4\), BGS-TRACE-123]
\label{thm:squarefree-product-abel}
Let \(N\) be square-free and divisible by at least three distinct odd
primes. Then \(L_N(M_N;1)>3/4\), so the distance from this value to
\((1-e^{-2})/4\) is greater than \(1/2\).

For the fixed kick \(\tau=10^{-80}\), the same gap holds for
\(L_N(U_N;1)\) in three windows. It holds when \(N\) has at least four
distinct prime factors and \(N\le10^{39}\). It holds when \(N\) is a
product of three distinct odd primes, \(15\) does not divide \(N\), and
\(N\le10^{39}\). It holds when \(N=15r\) with \(r\) an odd prime other
than \(5\) and \(N\le10^{26}\). For \(N=15r>10^{26}\) the pure sum remains
above \(3/4\) and the kick is not claimed.

A prime square is not covered. The modulus \(315=3^2\cdot5\cdot7\) has
three distinct prime factors and is not square-free. Products of two
distinct primes remain the dichotomy of BGS-TRACE-122. No density is
proved. The kick at the \(79\)-digit dimension \(u_{207}\), the signed
off-diagonal sum, the limit, and every \(k>2\) stay open.
\end{theorem}
