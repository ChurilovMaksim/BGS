\section{Global rotated-box representation at finite Heisenberg bandwidth}
\label{sec:global-rotated-box-trace}
Keep the prescribed odd-dimensional quantization and $\tau=10^{-80}$.
For each fixed transport order $J$, let $V_{N,J}$ be the explicit finite
four-branch kernel. Write $h=(2\pi N)^{-1}$; its amplitude polynomial is
$\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}N^{-\ell}a_{r,\ell}
=\sum_{\ell=0}^{J}(2\pi h)^\ell a_{r,\ell}$.
The two expansion parameters must not be conflated.

Put $B_m=6I-P_m-P_m^{\mathsf T}$, where $P_m$ is the cyclic shift,
and $D_m=\det B_m$. In particular $B_1=4$, while $B_2$ has diagonal
entries $6$ and off-diagonal entries $-2$.
For each branch vector $r\in\{0,1,2,3\}^m$ and winding representative
$\nu\in\mathbb Z^m/B_m\mathbb Z^m$, let $x^*_{r,\nu}$ be the unique
real stationary point of the exact lifted phase
$\Psi_{r,\nu}(x)=\sum_j S_{r_j}(\tau,x_j,x_{j+1})+\nu\cdot x$.
Let $A_{r,J}$ be the product of the finite one-step amplitudes and set
\[
 \mathcal B_{N,m,J}=
 \frac{e(3mN/8)N^{m/2}}{8^m}
 \sum_r\sum_{\nu\bmod B_m}
 \int_{x^*_{r,\nu}+(1-i)[-1,1]^m}
 e\!\left(N\Psi_{r,\nu}(x)\right) A_{r,J}(x)\,dx.
\]
The integrals retain the full nonlinear phase and their product orientation.
The class list is not asserted to consist of distinct physical orbits.

\paragraph{Conservative finite-band theorem.}
For every fixed $J\geq1$, uniformly for $1\le m\le2N$,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}U_N^m
 =\mathcal B_{N,m,J}
 +O_J(N^{3/2-J})+O_J\!\left(\sqrt N e^{-4N/5}\right).
\]
The first error is the separate operator-telescoping error; the second is
an absolutely summed contour-tail error. Negative traces use conjugation.
More generally, the latter error is
$O_{J,\beta}(\sqrt N\exp[-(8\pi/5-\beta\log7)N])$ for
$m\le\beta N$ and $\beta<8\pi/(5\log7)$, with a strict endpoint.
No coprimality condition is imposed on $N$ and $D_m$.

\paragraph{Contour mechanism and normalization.}
Exact Gaussian reciprocity first gives global, center-based Abel/Fresnel
integrals. The literal symbol bounds certify a holomorphic endpoint strip
of width $2$, with cyclic nonlinear Hessian row bound below $1/10$.
After centering at $x^*_{r,\nu}$, deform to
$z=u-i\operatorname{clip}(u,-1,1)$.
Since $K_0=B_m/4=I+L_{\mathrm{cycle}}/4$, monotonicity of clipping gives
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}K_0\operatorname{clip}(u)
 \ge\sum_j\min(u_j^2,|u_j|).
\]
The nonlinear correction leaves imaginary-phase damping at least
$(4/5)\sum_j\min(u_j^2,|u_j|)$.
Cauchy deformation is performed with positive Gaussian regularization;
dominated convergence removes it on the saturated contour.
The proof retains the full $4^mD_m$ class count, $D_m\le6^m$, amplitude
bound $2^m$, Jacobian bound $2^{m/2}$, and the factor $N^{m/2}/8^m$.
Their scalar integral estimate produces the displayed summed error.

\paragraph{Conditional tail without an artificial volume factor.}
For one integral normalize by its actual leading amplitude times
$(2\pi h)^{m/2}/\sqrt{\det K}$, where $K=-\Psi''(x^*)$.
Partition the saturated contour by its actual large-coordinate set $S$.
The retained core uses the exact principal Gaussian $K_{TT}^{-1}$,
$T=S^c$, on its box. Incident interactions cost only $C^{|S|}$;
convex-box moment and Poincar\'e estimates bound the core by $e^{Cmh}$.
The Schur determinant ratio also costs only $C^{|S|}$. Consequently the
normalized outside-box integral is at most
\[
 e^{Cmh}\left[\left(1+C\sqrt h\,e^{-2/(5h)}\right)^m-1\right].
\]
For each fixed $b_h$ with $mh\le b_h$, this is
$O_{b_h}(m\sqrt h\,e^{-2/(5h)})$.
The conditional Gaussian normalization is retained: a local box integral
divided by the full Gaussian factor is $\mathbb P_G(B_h)Z_{m,h}$.
Completing the core Gaussian on all of Euclidean space before bounding
its cross terms is not permitted.

\paragraph{Separate sharper corollary.}
With $\eta=10^{-4}$, $\gamma=10^{-7}$ and
\[
 \kappa=\log(1+\sqrt2)+\gamma-\tfrac12\log(1-4\eta),
\]
the conditional bound gives total tail
$O_{J,\beta}(\sqrt N\exp[-(4\pi/5-\beta\kappa)N])$
for $\beta<(4\pi/5)/\kappa$.
Indeed $a_*\le e^{\gamma m}$ and
$\det K\ge(1-4\eta)^mD_m/4^m$; summing the leading Gaussian weights
therefore costs at most $e^{\kappa m}$. This is a separate corollary,
not a replacement for the stronger conservative rate at $\beta=2$.
