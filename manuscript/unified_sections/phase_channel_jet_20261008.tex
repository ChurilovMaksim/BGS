\subsection{Max-entry phase memory and averaged pinching (TRACE-053)}
Let $S\in U(m)$, $P_{ij}=|S_{ij}|^2$, $\delta=\max_{ij}P_{ij}$,
and let $D$ have independent uniform coordinate phases. The same $D$ is
reused in every power. On the full complex Frobenius space write
$A=\Pi\operatorname{Ad}(S)$, where $\Pi$ is diagonal pinching, and
$\mathcal T_n=\mathbb E_D\operatorname{Ad}((DS)^n)$.
\begin{theorem}
For $n\ge2$,
\[
 \|\mathcal T_n-A^n\|_{2\to2}
 \le(n!-1)\sqrt\delta+n!n(n-1)\delta.
\]
Degree one is exact. At degree two the error has exact norm
$\max_{i\ne j}|S_{ij}S_{ji}|\le\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $Z=SXS^*$ and pair with an arbitrary complex mark $Y$.
For $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ put
$u(a)=\prod_{r<n}S_{a_{r+1},a_r}$. Phase integration gives
\[
 \langle Y,\mathcal T_nX\rangle
 =\sum_{\operatorname{occ}(a)=\operatorname{occ}(b)}
 \overline{Y_{a_nb_n}}Z_{a_1b_1}u(a)\overline{u(b)}.
\]
For each time permutation $\sigma$, first sum without collision restrictions
with $b_r=a_{\sigma(r)}$. Cauchy--Schwarz assigns $|Y|^2$ to the
positive $P$ chain and $|Z|^2$ to the reordered negative $P$ chain.
Distinct-time marginals of a bistochastic counting-measure path are
entries of positive powers of $P$, all bounded by $\delta$. A moved
endpoint therefore costs $\sqrt\delta$, and two moved endpoints cost
$\delta$, times $\|Y\|_F\|Z\|_F$. If both endpoints are fixed,
their unrestricted sums peel exactly, replacing the marks by
$S^*\Pi(Y)S$ and $S\Pi(Z)S^*$, both contractions. Any nonidentity
permutation eventually has a moved endpoint. The identity sum is $A^n$.

A prescribed collision at distinct formal times has probability at most
$\delta$ conditionally on an initial endpoint, since
\[
 \sum_c(P^{i-1})_{ce}(P^{j-i})_{cc}\le\delta\quad(i<j).
\]
The reversed bistochastic chain handles a terminal endpoint. The Cauchy
estimate therefore costs $\delta$ for a collision even with unrestricted
complex marks. Partition repeated paths by their first collision.
The true occupation-matched repeated part has at most $n!$ canonical
matching classes and costs $n!\binom n2\delta$. Separately, the repeated
part of the unrestricted permutation sum costs the same amount. Both
corrections are needed. The remaining $n!-1$ pairings give the theorem.
At degree two direct summation gives off-diagonal entries
$S_{ij}\overline{S_{ji}}(SXS^*)_{ji}$ and zero diagonal, proving the
exact norm formula.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal E_V$ denote full eigenspace pinching, including degeneracies.
For $L\ge2$ the positive Fejer operator
\[
 \mathcal F_L(V)=L^{-1}\sum_{|n|<L}(1-|n|/L)\operatorname{Ad}(V)^n
\]
has eigenmatrix multipliers
$L^{-2}|\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}e^{ir\theta}|^2$, so
$0\le\mathcal E_V\le\mathcal F_L(V)$. If
$\|P-J_m\|\le\rho<1$, then on traceless matrices
$\|A^n\|\le\rho^{n-1}$ and hence
\[
 \beta_m:=\|\mathbb E_D\mathcal E_{DS}\|
 \le\frac{1+2/(1-\rho)}L+
 \frac2L\sum_{n=2}^{L-1}n!\{\sqrt\delta+n(n-1)\delta\}.
\]
For $X=\log(1/\delta)$ take $L=\lfloor X/(8\log X)\rfloor$.
The last term is at most
$2e^{-3X/8}+2L^2e^{-7X/8}$, so
$\beta_m=O_\rho(\log X/X)$. This is the norm of the average.
The samplewise pinching norm need not vanish.

\paragraph{Spectral extraction boundary.}
For the symmetric Fourier matrix, $P=J_m$ and $\delta=1/m$, whereas
\[
 \mathbb E|\operatorname{Tr}(DS)^2|^2=2-1/m,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E|\operatorname{Tr}(DS)^3|^2=6-9/m+4/m^2.
\]
At degree three the occupation multiplicities contribute
$36\binom m3+9m(m-1)+m$ divided by $m^3$.
The vanishing degree-two channel norm coexists with a nonvanishing
superoperator-trace defect. This witness is not the fixed deterministic map
and supplies no microscopic limiting law.

\subsection{Direct real-flow transport (TRACE-056)}
Let $S=S_m\in U(m)$ be a deterministic unitary family with
$\delta=\delta_m:=\max_{ij}|S_{ij}|^2\to0$ and uniform intensity
singular gap $\||S|^2-J_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\rho<1$, where
$J_m$ has all entries $1/m$. Let $H=H_m=H_m^*$ be deterministic
and independent of the random coordinate phases, with
$\operatorname{Tr}H=m$ and $\operatorname{Tr}H^2\le C_Hm$.
Positivity of $H$ is unnecessary for this result. Put
\[
 W_x=DS e^{ixH/m},\quad V_x=e^{ix/m}DS,\quad
 Z_m(s;V)=m^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}[(I-e^{-s/m}V)^{-1}-I].
\]
For bounded real $|x|\le R$, the preceding pinching bound holds uniformly
with $\beta_{m,R}=O_{\rho,R,C_H}(\log X/X)$. Indeed
$\delta\ge1/m$, the new maximum entry squared is at most
$(2+2R^2C_H)\delta$, and the intensity matrix changes in norm by at most
$2R\sqrt{C_H/m}$.
\begin{theorem}
For $\operatorname{Re}s>0$,
\[
 \mathbb E|Z_m(s;W_x)-Z_m(s;V_x)|
 \le\frac{|x|\sqrt{(C_H-1)\beta_{m,R}}}
 {2m\sinh(\operatorname{Re}s/m)}
 \le\frac{|x|\sqrt{(C_H-1)\beta_{m,R}}}{2\operatorname{Re}s}.
\]
The difference tends to zero locally uniformly in absorbed $L^1$,
including each fixed source derivative.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $B=H-I$. Absolutely continuous eigenangle branches of $W_x$ have
almost-everywhere velocities given by eigenvalues of the compressed
generator $H/m$, including at multiple eigenvalues. Thus
\[
 \sum_j|\theta_j'(x)-1/m|^2
 =m^{-2}\|\mathcal E_{W_x}B\|_F^2,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\|\mathcal E_{W_x}B\|_F^2
 \le\beta_{m,R}(C_H-1)m.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and integration give a branch matching with
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_j|\theta_j(x)-\theta_j(0)-x/m|
 \le |x|\sqrt{(C_H-1)\beta_{m,R}}.
\]
The coupled endpoint law has a uniform common phase. For
$r=e^{-\operatorname{Re}s/m}$ and
$k_r(\phi)=re^{i\phi}/(1-re^{i\phi})$,
\[
 \|k_r'\|_{L^1(d\phi/(2\pi))}
 =\frac{r}{1-r^2}=\frac1{2\sinh(\operatorname{Re}s/m)}.
\]
Apply the translated-kernel $L^1$ bound to each matched branch, average
over the common phase, and divide by $m$. Cauchy contour estimates on a
fixed absorbed neighborhood give convergence of every fixed derivative.
\end{proof}
This is an unweighted scalar trace with normal scalar absorption at both
unitary endpoints. It does not compare the anisotropic physical source
with $e^{-sH/m}$, identify the scalar phase law, control mixed products,
remove absorption or set $D=I$.

\subsection{Optimal matrix inner second jets (TRACE-054)}
Let $F(z)=zG(z)$, where $G$ is square rational inner and analytic on the
closed disk after removal of removable singularities. On $z=e^{i\theta}$
write $d\mu=d\theta/(2\pi)$,
$T=F^*zF'\ge I$, and $\dot T=dT/d\theta$.
\begin{theorem}
\[
 \int\operatorname{Tr}(\dot T T^{-1}\dot T)\,d\mu
 \le\frac13\int\operatorname{Tr}(T^3-T)\,d\mu.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \int\|F''T^{-1/2}\|_F^2d\mu
 \le\frac43\int\operatorname{Tr}T^3d\mu
 -2\int\operatorname{Tr}T^2d\mu
 +\frac23\int\operatorname{Tr}T\,d\mu.
\]
The leading constant $4/3$ is optimal in every matrix dimension.
For nonnegative periodic $C^2$ weights $\omega$, the weighted versions
have the additional right-hand term
$(2/3)\int\omega''\operatorname{Tr}T\,d\mu$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Finite factorization can be obtained directly. If $\det G$ has a disk
zero $a$, choose a unit vector $v\in\ker G(a)$, $P=vv^*$ and
$B=I+(b_a-1)P$, with $b_a=(z-a)/(1-\bar az)$.
Right division $GB^{-1}$ is analytic because $G(z)v$ vanishes at $a$.
It is rational inner and its determinant degree drops by one.
Repeat. A zero-free rational inner determinant is constant unimodular;
the maximum principle applied to both residual matrix and its inverse
forces unitary values throughout the disk, hence a constant matrix.
Thus $F$ starts at $zU$ and appends finitely many such right factors.

For $A>0$, a projection $P$ and $p>0$, arbitrary matrices $X,Y$ with
$PY=Y$ obey
\[
 \|(A+pP)^{-1/2}(X+Y)\|_F^2
 \le\|A^{-1/2}X\|_F^2+p^{-1}\|Y\|_F^2.
\]
The block row $(A+pP)^{-1/2}[A^{1/2},\sqrt p P]$ is a coisometry;
apply its contraction bound to
$[A^{-1/2}X;p^{-1/2}Y]$. Singular $pP$ causes no inversion problem.

Append $B=I+(b_a-1)P$ to a previous product. Its Poisson kernel is
$p=\bar b_a z b_a'>0$. Set
$A=B^*T_{\rm old}B$ and $C=B^*\dot T_{\rm old}B$.
Then
\[
 T=A+pP,\qquad \dot T=C+ip(AP-PA)+p'P.
\]
Use the asymmetric split
$X=C+ipAP$, $Y=p'P-ipPA$; it has $PY=Y$.
Expansion under the trace, retaining noncommutativity, gives
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Tr}(\dot T T^{-1}\dot T)
 &\le\operatorname{Tr}(CA^{-1}C)
 +p^2\operatorname{Tr}(PA)+p\operatorname{Tr}(PA^2)
 +(p'^2/p)\operatorname{Tr}P,\\
 \operatorname{Tr}(T^3-A^3)
 &=3p\operatorname{Tr}(PA^2)+3p^2\operatorname{Tr}(PA)
 +p^3\operatorname{Tr}P.
\end{align*}
All cross traces in the first expansion vanish by cyclicity, not by
matrix commutation. The exact scalar identity
\[
 3p'^2/p=p^3-p+2p''
\]
shows that the integrated cubic-minus-linear derivative functional cannot
increase at a factor. With $\omega$, integrate by parts twice, giving
$\int\omega p''=\int\omega''p$. Accordingly the functional
\begin{align*}
 \Phi_\omega(T)={}&\int\omega\operatorname{Tr}(\dot T T^{-1}\dot T)\,d\mu
 -\frac13\int\omega\operatorname{Tr}(T^3-T)\,d\mu\\
 &-\frac23\int\omega''\operatorname{Tr}T\,d\mu
\end{align*}
cannot increase. Its base value at $T=I$ is zero. Induction proves both
derivative inequalities.

The exact complex-derivative identity is
$F^*z^2F''=T^2-T-i\dot T$. Since $T^{-1}$ commutes with $T^2-T$,
the cross trace vanishes and
\[
 \|F''T^{-1/2}\|_F^2
 =\operatorname{Tr}(T^3-2T^2+T)
 +\operatorname{Tr}(\dot T T^{-1}\dot T).
\]
This proves the asserted right-weighted jet bounds.

For sharpness take $F_r=zb_r$ with $r\uparrow1$ and $T=1+p_r$.
Put
\[
 M_2=\frac{1+r^2}{1-r^2},\qquad
 M_3=\frac{1+4r^2+r^4}{(1-r^2)^2}.
\]
The derivative energy loss from replacing $p_r$ by $1+p_r$ is between
zero and $\int(p_r'/p_r)^2=M_2-1$. Thus the jet energy is between
$(4/3)M_3+2/3$ and $(4/3)M_3+M_2-1/3$, while
$\int T^3=M_3+3M_2+4$. The ratio tends to $4/3$.
Embedding this scalar factor beside $m-1$ copies of $z$ gives sharpness
in each fixed matrix dimension.
\end{proof}
