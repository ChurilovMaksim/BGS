\section{A two-window phase crossover}
\label{sec:two-window-crossover}
Fix $q\ge1$, $R\ge2q$, $v\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}$ and put
$t=|\operatorname{tr}A|$. Define one fixed-support family
\[
 g_\varepsilon=(C^2-tC+I)^q(e_v+e_{-v})
                         +\varepsilon i(e_v-e_{-v}).
\]
Let $L_{R,n,m}(\varepsilon)$ be the $nm$-dimensional Gram matrix in
$L^2(\mathbb T^2)$ of
\[
 \lambda^{-r}C^lh_r(g_\varepsilon),\qquad
 R+1\le r\le R+n,\quad 1\le l\le m.
\]
The first index is lag, the second simultaneous time translation.

\begin{theorem}[Two-window lower edge]\label{thm:two-window-edge}
For $a=\min(n,m)$ there are positive constants depending only on
$A,v,q,R$ such that, uniformly in $n,m\ge1$ and real $\varepsilon$,
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(L_{R,n,m}(\varepsilon))
 \asymp (n^{-2q}+\varepsilon^2)(a^{-2q}+\varepsilon^2).
\]
This is a two-sided order estimate, not a claimed exact leading constant.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $u(\theta)=2-2\cos\theta$ and
$H(\theta)=u(\theta)(t^2-4+u(\theta))$. The exact symbol of
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-variable-tail} is
\begin{align*}
 F_\varepsilon(\omega,\theta)
 ={}&4\pi^2\xi(v)^2\eta(v)^2
       \bigl(\lambda^{2q}H(\theta)^q+\varepsilon^2\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{2em}\cdot
       \bigl(\lambda^{-2q}H(\omega-\theta)^q+\varepsilon^2\bigr).
\end{align*}
Indeed the characteristic polynomial obeys
\[
 |((\lambda e^{i\theta})^2-t\lambda e^{i\theta}+1)^q|^2
       =\lambda^{2q}H(\theta)^q,
\]
and the reciprocal-radius formula is analogous. The opposite orientations
cancel the sine perturbation's mixed terms.

For this single-orbit family the exact output row weight, relative to the
symbol, is $(1-\lambda^{-2k})^2$. The temporal filter has degree $2q$, so
$k\ge R+1-2q\ge1$. Thus the actual finite Gram matrix lies between the
Toeplitz form and
$(1-\lambda^{-2(R+1-2q)})^2$ times that form. This is a relative Loewner
comparison, with no additive error near a closing gap. Since $H\asymp u$,
it suffices to treat the symbol
$(u(\theta)^q+\varepsilon^2)(u(\omega-\theta)^q+\varepsilon^2)$.

Extend an $n$ by $m$ coefficient array by zero to $\mathbb Z^2$. Let $D_1$
and $D_2$ be first differences in directions $(1,0)$ and $(-1,1)$.
The form is exactly
\begin{align*}
 &\|D_1^qD_2^qx\|_2^2
 +\varepsilon^2\bigl(\|D_1^qx\|_2^2+\|D_2^qx\|_2^2\bigr)
 +\varepsilon^4\|x\|_2^2. \tag{TW.1}
\end{align*}
All boundary terms are included. A sequence supported on $s$ consecutive
sites has first Dirichlet difference eigenvalue
$4\sin^2(\pi/(2(s+1)))$. Fourier Jensen therefore gives
$\|D^qy\|_2^2\ge c_qs^{-2q}\|y\|_2^2$.
Horizontal chains have length $n$, diagonal chains length at most $a$.
After $D_1^q$ the latter length is at most
$\min(n+q,m)\le(q+1)a$. Applying these inequalities successively proves
\[
 \|D_1^qD_2^qx\|_2^2\ge c_qn^{-2q}a^{-2q}\|x\|_2^2,
\]
as well as the two single-difference lower bounds. Inserting them in (TW.1)
proves the lower estimate.

For the upper estimate take
\[
 x_{j,l}=f(j/(n+1))f(l/(m+1)),\qquad
 f(s)=s^{2q}(1-s)^{2q}\mathbf1_{[0,1]}(s).
\]
Vanishing derivatives through order $2q-1$ at both endpoints give the finite
difference bounds, including boundary crossings. If $E_j,E_l$ are shifts,
$D_2=E_j^{-1}(D_l-D_j)$. The binomial expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 \|D_1^qx\|_2&\le C_qn^{-q}\|x\|_2,\\
 \|D_2^qx\|_2&\le C_q(n^{-1}+m^{-1})^q\|x\|_2,\\
 \|D_1^qD_2^qx\|_2&\le C_qn^{-q}(n^{-1}+m^{-1})^q\|x\|_2.
\end{align*}
Use this trial vector in (TW.1). The finitely many small window lengths are
absorbed in the constants.
\end{proof}

When $m\ge n$, the edge has order $(n^{-2q}+\varepsilon^2)^2$, with
threshold $|\varepsilon|\asymp n^{-q}$. When $m\ll n$, the two thresholds
are $n^{-q}$ and $m^{-q}$, and the three orders are respectively
$n^{-2q}m^{-2q}$, $\varepsilon^2m^{-2q}$ and $\varepsilon^4$.
In particular a single simultaneous time, $m=1$, gives order $n^{-2q}$ at
zero perturbation, whereas $m\ge n$ gives $n^{-4q}$. Setting the dynamical
frequency to zero before specifying the time window would miss this
proved distinction. No identification with a quantum spectral form factor
is made.
