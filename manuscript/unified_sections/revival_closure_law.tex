\begin{theorem}[Twisted closure formula, BGS-TRACE-133]
\label{thm:revival-closure}
Let \(N\) be odd, \(m\ge1\), \(D\) diagonal and \(D_j=M_N^jDM_N^{-j}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(D_0\cdots D_{m-1})
=N\sum_{a\in\Lambda_m}e_N(\beta(a))\prod_{j<m}\hat d(a_j),
\]
where
\(\Lambda_m=\{a:\sum_ja_jS^je_2\equiv0\}\),
\(\beta(a)=-2\sum_{i<j}a_ia_jy_{j-i}\) and
\(S^d=\begin{pmatrix}x_d&4y_d\\2y_d&x_d\end{pmatrix}\).
The set has \(\lvert\Lambda_m\rvert=N^{m-2}\) for \(m\ge2\).
If \(S^m\equiv I\pmod N\), then
\(\operatorname{Tr}U^m=(\operatorname{Tr}M_N^m/N)\operatorname{Tr}(D_0\cdots D_{m-1})\)
and \(\lvert\operatorname{Tr}M_N^m\rvert=N\).
Dropping \(e_N(\beta)\) gives the classical orbit sum.
\end{theorem}

The formula is an explicit form, with the Pell cocycle, of an exact expansion
of the type of Boasman and Keating. It specialises the trace closure of
BGS-TRACE-126 and the scalar return of BGS-TRACE-129. Its phase average is the
standard Kluyver average
\(\mathbb E\lvert\operatorname{Tr}(D_\varphi M)^m\rvert^2
=\sum_{x,y}K(x)\overline{K(y)}\prod_rJ_0(w_r\lvert V_r(x)-V_r(y)\rvert)\).

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise revival bound, BGS-TRACE-134]
\label{thm:revival-bound}
Let \(S^t\equiv I\pmod N\), \(N>18\) and \(2\le A<N/2\). Then, for every
\(\varphi\),
\(\lvert\operatorname{Tr}U^t/\operatorname{Tr}M_N^t-\hat d(0)^t\rvert\le E_T+E_D+E_R\),
with the explicit terms of the canonical note. Moreover
\(\lvert\hat d(0)-J\rvert\le W^3e^W/6\).
If \(N\to\infty\) with \(\eta^2t\to\theta\) and
\(t=2\log_\lambda N+o(\sqrt t)\), then
\(\operatorname{Tr}U^t/\operatorname{Tr}M_N^t\to e^{-\theta\sigma_d/2}\)
uniformly in \(\varphi\).
This applies along the Bonechi--De~Bi\`evre minimal-period sequence:
\(7,41,239,1393,\dots\) with \(\kappa=4\), and \(35,1189,40391,\dots\) with
\(\kappa=32\).
\end{theorem}

The proof rests on a norm lemma in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\): a closure with
coefficients at most \(A\) in a window of length \(L\), with
\(A^2\lambda^L\le4N^2\), is an exact multiple of \(x^2-6x+1\).

The explicit bound gives no information at computable dimensions. At
\(\lambda_{\rm rev}=1/4\) it first falls below \(1\) near \(N\approx10^{191}\),
and at \(\lambda_{\rm rev}=1\) it is still \(2.36\) at \(N\approx10^{19139}\).
Monte Carlo at \(N\le8119\) is consistent with the limit.

\begin{theorem}[Half-period parity reduction, BGS-TRACE-135]
\label{thm:revival-parity}
If \(N\) is an odd prime and \(t\) is even, then \(S^{t/2}\equiv-I\) and
\(M_N^{t/2}=cP\), with \(P\) the parity. Hence
\(\operatorname{Tr}U^t/\operatorname{Tr}M_N^t=N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(A\,PAP)\)
with \(A=D_0\cdots D_{t/2-1}\).
\end{theorem}

The parity structure is classical (Hannay--Berry; Kurlberg--Rudnick).

Two statements remain open: BGS-TRACE-136 and BGS-TRACE-137.
\begin{itemize}
\item BGS-TRACE-136 is a free-probability heuristic for even \(t\). It
predicts \(\mathbb E\lvert R\rvert^2\to e^{-\lambda}(1+0.1205\lambda^2)\).
Monte Carlo at \(61\) and \(101\) agrees within \(3.4\sigma\) and rejects the
classical-orbit law.
\item BGS-TRACE-137 is the exponential law for odd \(t\) at generic
dimensions. Monte Carlo agrees within \(5\%\) for \(71\le N\le263\) and is
\(18\%\) low at \(N=47\), \(\lambda=3\).
\end{itemize}
Neither statement is proved.
