\section{The same sharp law for censored and full heterodyne detection}
\label{sec:heterodyne-aperture}
We now change the measurement, rather than silently identifying binary
coherent tests with heterodyne detection. Standard multimode heterodyne
measurement produces $Z\in\mathbb C^s$ with Lebesgue density
$Q_\rho(z)=\pi^{-s}\langle z|\rho|z\rangle$. Its radius-$R$ censored version
reports $Z$ when $\|Z\|\le R$ and a single symbol $\star$ otherwise. Every
copy is counted, including outcomes labelled $\star$; the data are not
postselected or renormalized. For $R=\infty$ this is ordinary heterodyne.
Let $\mathfrak H_B(R)$ be its minimax expected trace-distance error on the
same class $\mathcal S_{s,\beta,E}$, optimized over all estimators from $B$
independent outcomes.

\begin{theorem}[Heterodyne minimax law]
\label{thm:heterodyne-minimax}
Fix $s\ge1$, $\beta,E,R_0>0$. Uniformly for $R\ge R_0$, including
$R=\infty$ and radii depending on $B$, one has
\[
 \boxed{
 c\left(\frac{1+\log_+((\log B)/R^2)}{\log B}\right)^{\beta/2}
 \le\mathfrak H_B(R)\le
 C\left(\frac{1+\log_+((\log B)/R^2)}{\log B}\right)^{\beta/2}}
 \tag{B.1}\label{eq:heterodyne-minimax}
\]
for all sufficiently large $B$. The lower bound holds on pure states. The
upper bound needs no rank constraint. Constants may depend on the fixed
$s,\beta,E,R_0$, not on $R$ or $B$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Upper bound from local counts and stable regularization}
No independent repetitions at prescribed points are available in heterodyne
sampling. Instead estimate the density locally, using the very same samples
at each of the finitely many interpolation nodes. For every state,
\[
 |Q_\rho(z)-Q_\rho(w)|\le\pi^{-s}\|z-w\|,
 \tag{B.2}\label{eq:heterodyne-lipschitz}
\]
since the operator norm distance of the two coherent projectors is
$\sqrt{1-e^{-\|z-w\|^2}}\le\|z-w\|$.

Put $T=\log B$, choose $K=\lfloor cK_R(T)\rfloor$ and
$S=\min(R^2/4,K)$, and use the Chebyshev grid with $L=128sK$ from the
previous section. Every node $z_j$ has norm at most $R/2$. Let
$h=B^{-1/(4s+2)}$ and $v_{2s}=\pi^s/s!$, the unit-ball volume in
$2s$ real dimensions. For large $B$ one has $h<R_0/2$. Therefore the
balls $B(z_j,h)$ lie within the recorded region, and their membership is
known even for censored observations. Define
\[
 \widehat Q_j=\frac{1}{Bv_{2s}h^{2s}}
          \sum_{b=1}^B\mathbf1_{\{Z_b\in B(z_j,h)\}}.
 \tag{B.3}\label{eq:heterodyne-density-estimate}
\]
The bias is at most $\pi^{-s}h$ by \eqref{eq:heterodyne-lipschitz}.
Hoeffding's inequality and the union bound, without independence between
nodes, give simultaneously with probability at least $1-B^{-2}$,
\[
 |\pi^s\widehat Q_j-p_\rho(z_j)|
 \le h+\frac{s!}{h^{2s}}
       \sqrt{\frac{\log(2(L+1)^{2s}B^2)}{2B}}
 =\exp\!\left(-\frac{T}{4s+2}+O_s(\log T)\right).
 \tag{B.4}\label{eq:heterodyne-density-error}
\]
Replacing $R$ by $R/2$ in the analytic interpolation estimate changes
$\Phi_R(K)$ by at most $K\log4$, hence by a fixed factor. Choose $c>0$
small enough that the noise amplification is at most $\exp(T/(8s+4))$
up to polynomial factors. Lemmas~\ref{lem:aperture-coefficients} and
\ref{lem:aperture-state}, and the analytic bias bound
\eqref{eq:aperture-bias}, then give expected loss at most
$CK_R(T)^{-\beta/2}$. The estimate is the normalized positive part of a
finite reconstructed block; it is a genuine density operator. This proves
the upper bound in \eqref{eq:heterodyne-minimax}.

\subsection{An explicit exponentially indistinguishable pure-state pair}
Use \eqref{eq:aperture-pair}, now with even $n=2k$. With $a_z,b_z$ as in
the preceding section,
\[
 \bigl(\sqrt{Q_+(z)}-\sqrt{Q_-(z)}\bigr)^2
       \le4\pi^{-s}\min(a_z^2,b_z^2)
       \le4\pi^{-s}a_zb_z.
\]
Integration over the whole space separates the vacuum factors in modes
$2,\ldots,s$ and gives the finite, explicit bound
\begin{align}
 h^2(Q_+,Q_-)
 &\le4\sqrt{u(1-u)}\frac{\Gamma(n/2+1)}{\sqrt{n!}}\nonumber\\
 &=\frac{4\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{\sqrt{\binom{2k}{k}}}
 \le4\sqrt{u(n+1)}\,2^{-n/2}.
 \tag{B.5}\label{eq:heterodyne-overlap}
\end{align}
The last inequality follows from
$\binom{2k}{k}\ge2^{2k}/(2k+1)$: the central binomial coefficient is the
largest of $2k+1$ coefficients whose sum is $2^{2k}$.
This is an upper bound on statistical distinguishability, not merely a
small difference of mean measurements.

For a ball centred at the origin the two probabilities of $\star$ are
exactly equal. Indeed the difference of the two densities is proportional
to $\cos(n\arg z_1)$, whose angular integral vanishes on any such ball.
The censored Hellinger distance therefore has only the inside-ball
contribution. Bounding its integrand by $4\pi^{-s}b_z^2$ and integrating
the other modes over their whole spaces gives
\[
 h_R^2(Q_+,Q_-)
 \le \frac{4u}{n!}\int_0^{R^2}e^{-x}x^n\,dx
 \le\frac{4uR^{2n+2}}{(n+1)!}.
 \tag{B.6}\label{eq:heterodyne-aperture-overlap}
\]
For $R^2\le n$ this is at most $4uR^{2n}/n!$. Together with
\eqref{eq:heterodyne-overlap} and $n!\ge(n/e)^n$, it follows that
\[
 h_R^2(Q_+,Q_-)
 \le C_E\sqrt{n+1}\,
                              \exp(-c\Phi_R(n))
 \tag{B.7}\label{eq:heterodyne-small}
\]
for an absolute $c>0$ and large $n$, uniformly for every $R>0$.
For $R^2>n$ the global estimate suffices; for $R^2\le n$ compare the
two exponent bounds, as in \eqref{eq:aperture-hellinger}.

For $B$ independent outcomes, product affinity gives
$h^2(\mathbb P_+^{\otimes B},\mathbb P_-^{\otimes B})\le B h_R^2$.
Choose the least even integer $n\ge C K_R(\log B)$ with a sufficiently
large constant. Then \eqref{eq:heterodyne-small} makes the transcript total
variation at most $1/2$ for all large $B$. The two states remain separated
in trace distance by at least $\sqrt{2E}n^{-\beta/2}$. The same two-point
testing argument as before gives risk at least one eighth of that distance.
This proves the lower bound and completes Theorem~\ref{thm:heterodyne-minimax}.

\subsection{Consequences and comparison with prior tomography bounds}
Both the censored-heterodyne model and the adaptive binary coherent-test
model have the same minimax order, although they are different statistical
experiments and our proofs use different upper estimators and Hellinger
bounds. The copy-complexity law \eqref{eq:aperture-complexity} holds for both.
For fixed mean energy, ordinary heterodyne therefore requires and suffices
with $\exp(\Theta(\varepsilon^{-2}))$ copies in this worst-case trace-distance
problem. For a fixed finite recorded ball, the corresponding formula is
\[
 B_*(\varepsilon,R)=
 \exp\!\bigl(\Theta(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\varepsilon))\bigr)
 \quad(\beta=1,\ 0<R<\infty\text{ fixed}).
\]
No statement here covers arbitrary squeezed Gaussian measurements,
homodyne detection, or measurements jointly acting on several copies.
Adaptive passive transformations followed by uncensored heterodyne only
relabel the complete outcome and cannot evade its lower bound.

Exponential heterodyne upper bounds at a hard photon cutoff were already
reported by Gandhari et al.~\cite{Gandhari2024}; their paper explicitly
leaves optimal lower bounds open. General moment-constrained tomography
with more general measurements is studied by Mele et al.~\cite{Mele2025}.
These are not priority claims for the difficulty of heterodyne inversion.
The result proved here is a matching trace-distance rate under only one
photon-moment bound, with no state truncation assumption and a uniform
finite-aperture transition.

A source-level distinction matters in comparing Becker et al.~\cite{BDLR2024}.
The explicit heterodyne Theorem 4 in the journal version (Theorem 11 in
arXiv v2) retains the cutoff-dependent norm
$\widetilde\Sigma_r^{(\alpha)}(M,R)$ in its sample bound. It must not be
replaced by a uniform constant. The overview Theorem 2 of the journal
version (Theorem 3 in arXiv v2), read as a uniform polynomial-in-accuracy
bound for full trace-norm recovery using ordinary heterodyne under only a
fixed moment bound, is incompatible with the explicit pair
\eqref{eq:heterodyne-overlap}. For an integer moment order $\beta$ our pair
also satisfies
$\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_n^\pm(I+\mathcal N)^\beta)\le1+2^\beta E$ uniformly
in $n$, so changing between the two moment conventions cannot remove the
conflict. This narrow comparison does not retract the explicit
cutoff-dependent theorem, the homodyne results, or statements about a fixed
finite set of polynomial observables. No author correspondence or accepted
correction is claimed; the comparison and our proof require independent
review.
